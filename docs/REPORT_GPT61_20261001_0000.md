## 3. WP5: what consequential pivots look like (W75; new)

**Protocol** (`docs/insights/ANNOTATION_PROTOCOL.md`, including Amendment 1):
- 80 states, 20 per sign, frozen before labelling; 20 are double-reviewed.
- The rubric is frozen by hash, and the packets are blinded: no model, precision or outcome.
- **Author decision (Amendment 1): the reviewers are two independent Sonnet 5.5 instances, not humans**, with a third instance adjudicating. In the paper this is "LLM-annotated", never "human".
- The label and adjudication shas were committed before unblinding (62ec2a1, bd6164d), and the key's sha matches the frozen one.
- The earlier Qwen3-30B labels (that model is also the τ² simulator) are kept as a separate, weaker labeller.

**Agreement on the double states** (`analysis/annotation/wp5/agreement_v1.json`):
- κ ≥ 0.8 for defensible, groundedness, argument validity, consent, irreversible change, loop, budget exhaustion, defect corrected and retries.
- κ 0.69 for requirements satisfied and user clarification. All 7 unresolved adjudications are user clarification.
- κ 0.29 for tool validity, which is unusable.

**Table 4** (`analysis/annotation/wp5/table4_v1.json`): 20 states per sign, as sample frequencies, not prevalences.
- **Rescue:** the BF16 action with the Q continuation succeeds and the Q action with the Q continuation fails (Y_BQ = 1, Y_QQ = 0).
- **Damage:** the reverse (Y_BQ = 0, Y_QQ = 1).

| label | rescue | damage | both succeed | both fail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| quantized pivot action judged indefensible | 7 | **12** | 10 | 12 |
| quantized pivot has a local defect | 9 | **14** | 9 | 16 |
| BF16 pivot judged indefensible | 8 | 5 | 6 | 9 |
| QQ: unconsented irreversible write | **9** | 1 | 1 | 5 |
| BQ: unconsented irreversible write | 2 | **9** | 1 | 7 |
| QQ: pivot defect corrected later | 4 | **10** | 4 | 7 |
| QQ: loop or budget exhaustion | **5** | 0 | 1 | 7 |

**Reading:**
- The local quality of the quantized pivot does not predict the sign of its consequence. In damage states the quantized pivot looks worse, yet in half of them it is corrected later and that branch succeeds.
- The failing branch carries an unconsented irreversible write about 45% of the time, and this flips with which branch fails.

**Caveats:**
- n = 20 per sign, and there are no intervals yet.
- LLM annotators. The requirements-satisfied label only weakly tracks the true outcome: "yes" has precision 0.80 and covers 52% of the successes.
- An irreversible write is part of *how* a branch fails, not evidence of a cause.
