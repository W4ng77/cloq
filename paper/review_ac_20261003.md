### W1. [MAJOR, central] "Continuation dependence" (26.9%) is not calibrated against any no-interaction or re-roll null

**Where:**
- abstract.tex:3, "in 26.9\% of divergent states the swap's effect depends on which model continues";
- introduction.tex:13, "\textbf{Upgrade value is continuation-dependent}";
- results_r1.tex:10-11;
- discussion.tex:4;
- appendix_moved.tex:32, "Because replay is exact, each off-diagonal state shows a real interaction on the success scale, not replay noise.";
- appendix.tex:158, which admits that the only control "is a pairing agreement control, not a no-interaction null and not a chance baseline for controller dependence".

**Why a reviewer attacks it.** With pattern digits (Y_BB, Y_BQ, Y_QB, Y_QQ), a state is off-diagonal (Δ_Q ≠ Δ_B) exactly when an odd number of branches succeed (k = 1 or 3), or when it is a strict reversal (1001, 0110). Any re-roll noise in branch outcomes creates odd-k states.

Under an **exchangeable-branch null**, the four outcomes of a state are permuted at random while k is kept. The expected off-diagonal share is then P(k odd) + P(k = 2)/3. From `gen_patterns.tex` (reviewer computation, point estimates):

| Cell / pool | Off-diag observed | Exchangeable null | Odd-k share | Strict reversals | P(only BB succeeds \| k = 1) (null 0.25) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| q4 AWQ-W4 | 29.3% | 30.5% | 26.2% | 3.1% | 0.24 |
| q4 GPTQ-W4 | 25.0% | 27.9% | 22.4% | 2.6% | 0.21 |
| q8 AWQ-W4 | 30.5% | 30.3% | 26.2% | 4.2% | 0.30 |
| q8 GPTQ-W4 | 22.9% | 24.7% | 20.2% | 2.7% | 0.39 |
| **W4 pool (equal weight)** | **26.9%** | **28.4%** | **23.8%** | 3.2% | |
| KV8 pool | 15.3% | 18.9% | 14.1% | 1.2% | |

The observed share is at or below the exchangeable null in every W4 cell. Four other facts point the same way:
- the mean interaction is +0.0 [−1.9, +2.0] pp (results_r1.tex:11);
- under re-sampled users, 0.060 of the raw 0.110 mean squared interaction is replicate noise (appendix.tex:193);
- a second W4 checkpoint gives the same off-diagonal share (26.2% vs 25.0%; results_r2.tex:13);
- Bank U text-flip labels mostly did not reproduce under a second simulator (appendix_moved.tex:59).

Together these say the 26.9% measures how often branches disagree (state fragility). It does not measure a reproducible dependence of an action's value on the continuation.

Real structure does exist, but it lives elsewhere:
- In q8 GPTQ-W4, "only BB succeeds" is enriched (0.39 vs 0.25), consistent with F > G there.
- The mean mode contrasts within cells differ (q8 G−H; RTN-W3 takeover; q14 action).

A statistician reviewer can redo this table in five minutes from Table `tab:patterns`. If the paper does not do it first, Finding 1 will be described as "a noise statistic presented as an effect".

**Cheapest fix (CPU only; do it before the freeze).**
1. Compute the exchangeable-branch null per cell and pool with block-bootstrap intervals. Also run a test of arrangement uniformity within k (permutation), per cell. Run it on the BFCL, ALFWorld and holdout four-arm outputs too.
2. Add a task-level variance decomposition of I across the 20 blocks: between-task variance of mean I, net of within-task variance divided by the number of blocks. This gives the share of interaction that is a reproducible task property.
3. Rewrite Finding 1 as "Upgrade modes must be compared on the same states". Anchor it on the mean contrasts (G, H, F within cells) and report the off-diagonal share descriptively next to its null.
4. Rewrite appendix_moved.tex:32 (wording in §4).

**Needs data still coming?** No.

### W8. [MAJOR] Positioning: the design has close analogues that are not cited, and basic citations are missing

**Where:** related_work.tex:4-12; introduction.tex:9 and 18; method.tex:9 and 11.

**Why.**
- **(a) RL framing.**

- **(c) Numerics work the method relies on is in the bib but uncited:** du2026greedy, he2025defeating (the batch-invariant kernels used in method.tex:11) and yuan2025nondeterminism.

> A quantized agent can hand one decision, or the rest of an episode, to its BF16 reference. End-to-end success cannot say whether either helps, because it mixes the value of the replaced action with the value of whoever executes afterwards. We freeze closed-loop states that replay bit-exactly on our serving stack, cross the source of the current action with the model that continues, and repeat the design with a second W4 checkpoint in place of BF16. On τ²-bench with Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B, replacing a W4 agent's first divergent tool action with BF16's flips 15.5-20.9% of outcomes in both directions, for a net −1.9 to +2.9 pp. A second W4 checkpoint flips as many (17.7% vs 16.8%), and on common states BF16's action is not shown to add value over it (+1.0 and +0.8 pp; 95% intervals [−3.5, +5.4] and [−1.8, +3.3]). # Part 1: Structured review

## Summary

The paper asks when a BF16 reference should replace a W4 (or KV-quantized) agent's current action, take over the rest of the episode, or both.

**Design.**
- At bit-exact frozen τ² states, it crosses the source of the current action (B or Q) with the model that continues (B or Q), giving four branches. From these it defines three upgrade gains: G (this step), H (from the next step) and F (both).
- It adds a same-precision reference: a second GPTQ-W4 checkpoint Q′ in place of B.
- It adds a common-state design with three action sources.
- It tests a frozen Q-only uncertainty trigger against random selection and a hindsight oracle.

**Data.** Qwen3-4B and 8B on 85 retail and airline development tasks with 20 nonce blocks; BFCL and ALFWorld (one run per item); and a pre-registered 34-task telecom holdout.

**Findings.**
- Replacing the first tool-divergent action flips 15.5-20.9% of units in both directions, with a net of −1.9 to +2.9 pp.
- In 26.9% of divergent states the swap's effect differs between continuations.
- Within the q8 cells, on the fixed pool, action replacement beats takeover.
- A second W4 checkpoint produces similar turnover and coupling.
- At common q8 states, BF16's action is not shown to add value over Q′.
- The uncertainty trigger captures flips but not net gains.
- The holdout estimate is negative (an inconclusive reading by the pre-registration).

## Strengths

- **[S1] Replay exactness is engineered and audited, not assumed.**
  - Gate: Q→Q 20/20 and B→B 10/10 across every hash, message, DB, reward and budget field (method.tex:31).
  - A 10% hash-sampled QQ no-op on all 924 development states (method.tex:32).
  - Determinism pairs 257/257 and 1520/1520 (method.tex:33).
  - Cross-study byte identity, 9/9, which I checked in `REPLAY_8of9.md`.
  - A reuse audit with 4113/4114 token-identical reuses (appendix_moved.tex:51).
  - The scope limits are stated (method.tex:33).
- **[S2] The same-precision reference is the right control and is well matched.** BF16 and Q′ have the same first-divergence rate (76.9% vs 76.9%) and similar action agreement with Q (55.7% vs 53.9%; appendix_moved.tex:103). It turns a "precision effect" claim into a falsifiable one.
- **[S3] Inference is explicit about denominators and populations.** There are three denominators (method.tex:38). Fixed-pool and new-task intervals are reported side by side. "Identified vs descriptive" is stated for cross-cell comparisons.
- **[S4] Pre-registration and disclosure.** There is a hash-locked holdout (appendix.tex:461-473). The trigger was frozen before scoring. Amendments are dated, and a two-look sensitivity analysis is reported (appendix_moved.tex:101). Withdrawn results are kept (W×KV, appendix.tex:551-553).
- **[S5] Provenance.** Every number carries a `% src:`. The checker passes on 3616 occurrences (run by me), and my semantic spot-checks matched.
- **[S6] Related-work descriptions are accurate.** I checked the Replay Gap, Flat Score and DMR descriptions against their texts.
- **[S7] The limitations are candid and specific.** limitations.tex:3-19 states what is not claimed.

- **[W2] FATAL if unfixed:** over 8 pages; abstract over 200 words.

- **[W4] MAJOR:** the G−H headline is post hoc, fixed-pool, from one simulator, and not reproduced.

- **[W8] MAJOR:** missing positioning (RL takeover, step-level escalation, numerics) and missing basic citations.

## Questions for Authors

- **[Q1]** What off-diagonal share do you expect if each state's four branch outcomes were exchangeable? Is the arrangement of successes within k non-uniform beyond chance in any cell?
- **[Q2]** Was G−H designated as the primary mode contrast before the four-branch results were computed? If not, why does it appear in the abstract with Holm p-values?
- **[Q3]** How many BF16 calls and tokens does each mode consume per state? What is the value per BF16 call?
- **[Q4]** Do r, d, M, G_all and G−H change under τ²'s official reward instead of DB match? How many successes are transfers to a human?
- **[Q5]** Why is Qwen3-8B BF16 success (30.4%) below Qwen3-4B BF16 (35.8%)?
- **[Q6]** What is the minimal detectable net gain of the trigger test at each budget?
- **[Q7]** Why did the holdout pre-registration have no negative reading? What is the telecom end-to-end BF16-vs-W4 comparison?
- **[Q8]** How was the Fig. 1a case selected?
- **[Q9]** Will the harness, run logs and frozen states be released (anonymised at review)?
- **[Q10]** At common states, both K(c) intervals are within ±5.4 pp. What sample size would resolve ±3 pp at the observed variance?

## Verdict

This is a careful, unusually transparent closed-loop evaluation study whose infrastructure and controls are better than most agent papers.

**Its main empirical message differs from what the title and abstract claim.** At W4 on Qwen3-4B/8B τ², a BF16 substitution is indistinguishable from a second W4 checkpoint in how often it diverges, flips and couples with the continuation. No upgrade mode has a detectable pooled value. The one confirmatory test is negative.

**The headline "continuation dependence" statistic** sits at or below an exchangeable-branch null that a reviewer can compute from the paper's own appendix. **The headline G−H result** is post hoc and fragile.

**Revision priority:** high, but almost entirely writing and CPU re-analysis. The paper is also over length. As-is: Soundness 3.0, Excitement 2.5, Overall 2.5. After the fixes: about 3.0 overall. **Confidence:** 4.
