\paragraph{Large outcome changes, small net estimate.} The development panel has 85 tasks and 20 complete blocks per configuration. We replace Q's action at its first divergence with B's and let Q continue. This changes 15.5--20.9\% of outcomes, with rescues and damages of similar size (Figure~\ref{fig:rd}a). No new-task interval of the net excludes zero. Pooled over the four W4 configurations, the full-pool net gain $\Gall$ is $+0.7$\pp{} (new-task 95\% [$-2.1$, $+3.4$]). The net is a local replacement effect, not an end-to-end comparison (Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}). % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md (cells; W4:all pooled row: G block bootstrap, M); analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.json (pooled.W4:all:pooled_equal_weight.G.crossed_ci95)

\paragraph{Action-only fallback can harm new tasks.} The holdout tests 34 unseen telecom tasks over 12 fresh blocks and three W4 configurations. Its protocol was fixed before any task ran. Replacement again produces both rescues and damages (Figure~\ref{fig:rd}b). The primary full-pool net gain $\Gall$, $-4.57$\pp{} (crossed 90\% [$-7.17$, $-1.96$]), lies below zero: action-only replacement reduced success on this holdout. Its cause is unknown. The frozen readings had only positive-gain and small-effect categories, a design omission, so the protocol classifies the result as \emph{inconclusive} (Figure~\ref{fig:rd}b; Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}). Both pre-registered secondary checks passed: rescues and damages were each positive, and task fragility repeated across independent prompt blocks (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}). % src: analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md (TL;DR; Readings; S1; S2); docs/insights/PREREG_FORK_CONFIRM.md §0, §8
Full baseline replay rules out a restoration mismatch at the tested states. The holdout changes domain as well as tasks (Appendix~\ref{app:holdoutdev}). % src: analysis/fork/out/holdout_qqfull_audit_20261006T061033Z.md (TL;DR); docs/insights/HOLDOUT_QQFULL_PROTOCOL.md (§1-§5, Amendment 1); docs/insights/PREREG_FORK_CONFIRM.md §1

The negative estimate concerns a hybrid policy: B supplies one action, then Q handles its consequences. This differs from running B throughout the task. Higher precision at one step therefore does not guarantee a better complete trajectory.

\paragraph{The intervention defines which rescues count.} Figure~\ref{fig:rd}c reports all three modes on the W4 and KV8 divergent-state pools. For W4, $G$, $H$ and $F$ average $+0.5$, $+0.2$ and $+0.7$\pp{}, respectively; all three new-task intervals cover zero. Similar averages still hide different successes. Joint rescue, where only changing both succeeds, occurs on 4.0\% of W4 divergent states. Rescue undone, where B's action succeeds with Q continuing but fails with B continuing, occurs on 4.8\%. These are observed shares, not evidence of enrichment over a permutation reference (Appendix~\ref{app:offdiag}). Action-only evaluation misses the former; joint-replacement evaluation misses the latter. Thus a measured gain for one mode cannot stand in for another.

The RTN-W3 scope check illustrates this distinction in aggregate: action-only replacement has a near-zero estimate, whereas continuation takeover and joint replacement have positive gains (Appendix~\ref{app:scope}). This configuration was selected from five exploratory stress settings; it shows why evaluating only the current action cannot settle the value of a takeover.

These mode-specific values answer a question that a single success-rate comparison cannot: what happens when this action is changed and this controller handles the consequences? A beneficial complete-agent substitution does not imply that borrowing one of its actions will help. Conversely, an action-only failure does not settle whether taking over the continuation would be useful.
