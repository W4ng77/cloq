\paragraph{Rescues and damages nearly balance.} We test 85 development tasks over 20 prompt blocks per configuration. B replaces Q's action at their first divergence, then Q continues. Outcomes change in 15.5--20.9\% of task--block units (Figure~\ref{fig:rd}a). Counting only these changes would suggest a large effect. Counting their direction gives a different picture: rescues and damages are of similar size. Averaging the four configuration estimates equally, full-pool net gain $\Gall$ is $+0.7$\pp{} (new-task 95\% [$-2.1$, $+3.4$]). None of the four configurations establishes a net gain on new tasks. End-to-end model scores appear separately in Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md (cells; W4:all pooled row: G block bootstrap, M); analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.json (pooled.W4:all:pooled_equal_weight.G.crossed_ci95)

\paragraph{The telecom holdout has a negative estimate.} The pre-registered holdout tests 34 unseen telecom tasks over 12 fresh blocks and three W4 configurations. Here damages exceed rescues (Figure~\ref{fig:rd}b). Full-pool net gain $\Gall$ is $-4.57$\pp{} (crossed 90\% [$-7.17$, $-1.96$]): action-only replacement reduced success in this setting. The protocol nevertheless classifies the result as \emph{inconclusive}, because its frozen readings included positive-gain and small-effect categories but omitted a negative-effect category. Both registered secondary checks passed: rescues and damages were positive, and task fragility repeated across independent prompt blocks. Appendix~\ref{app:prereg} gives the readings and secondary tests. % src: analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md (TL;DR; Readings; S1; S2); docs/insights/PREREG_FORK_CONFIRM.md §0, §8
Full baseline replay rules out a restoration mismatch at the tested states. The holdout changes domain as well as tasks (Appendix~\ref{app:holdoutdev}). % src: analysis/fork/out/holdout_qqfull_audit_20261006T061033Z.md (TL;DR); docs/insights/HOLDOUT_QQFULL_PROTOCOL.md (§1-§5, Amendment 1); docs/insights/PREREG_FORK_CONFIRM.md §1

This intervention creates a hybrid trajectory: B supplies one action, then Q handles its consequences. The result shows that an action-only fallback can be harmful; it does not identify the cause. For example, difficulty continuing from B's action is one possible explanation, but these data do not isolate it. This differs from running B throughout.

\paragraph{The intervention defines which rescues count.} Figure~\ref{fig:rd}c reports all three modes on the W4 and KV8 divergent-state pools. For W4, $G$, $H$ and $F$ average $+0.5$, $+0.2$ and $+0.7$\pp{}, respectively; all three new-task intervals cover zero. Similar averages still hide different successes. Joint rescue, where only changing both succeeds, occurs on 4.0\% of W4 divergent states. Rescue undone, where B's action succeeds with Q continuing but fails with B continuing, occurs on 4.8\%. These are observed shares, not evidence of enrichment over a permutation reference (Appendix~\ref{app:offdiag}). Action-only evaluation misses the former; joint-replacement evaluation misses the latter. Thus a measured gain for one mode cannot stand in for another.

The five lower-bit stress settings provide the complementary case (Table~\ref{tab:scope}). Joint replacement has a positive new-task interval in Qwen3-4B KV4 and Qwen3-8B RTN-W3; the other three remain unresolved. In RTN-W3, borrowing one action has a near-zero estimate while handing over later execution helps. The distinction between modes matters when substantial gains are present as well as when average gains are small.

The practical distinction is between borrowing an action and handing over the task. The former relies on Q to use the substituted action; the latter changes the policy that handles later decisions. Each mode requires its own measurement, even with similar average estimates.
