\paragraph{Many rescues, many damages, small net.} On the \tautwo{} development panel (85 tasks $\times$ 20 complete blocks), replacing a W4 agent's first tool-structure-divergent action with B's, while Q continues, changes the task outcome in $M=15.5$--$20.9$\% of units (Figure~\ref{fig:case}b). Rescues and damages are of similar size, $r=6.8$--$11.4$ and $d=7.5$--$10.1$\pp{}, so the net gain $\Gall$ lies between $-1.9$ and $+2.9$\pp{}. Two fixed-pool intervals exclude zero (negative for q4 GPTQ-W4, positive for q8 GPTQ-W4); no new-task interval does. KV8 cells turn over fewer outcomes ($M=6.5$--$8.1$\%, a descriptive comparison). $\Gall$ is a local replacement effect, not an end-to-end comparison (Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}). % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md
Pooled over the four W4 cells, $\Gall=+0.7$\pp{} (fixed pool [$-0.3$, $+1.6$]; new tasks [$-2.1$, $+3.5$]) with $M=18.6$\%. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md (W4:all pooled row: G block bootstrap, M); analysis/fork/out/task_fragility_20261005T134836Z.md (Full pool, pool G crossed)

\paragraph{The consequences recur on the same tasks.} We split the 20 nonce blocks into two halves by an outcome-independent hash of the block name (written before this post hoc analysis loads outcomes), ranked tasks by turnover on one half and measured the other half (Table~\ref{tab:fragility}). The 17 most fragile tasks (top 20\%) carry 41.0\% (crossed [26.6, 55.9]) and 42.3\% ([27.0, 57.5]) of the held-out turnover in the two directions, against 20\% if fragility did not persist. Their turnover is 38.7\%, while the other 68 tasks still turn over 13.7\%. Both groups have rescues and damages; neither group's net gain is resolved under the crossed interval (Appendix~\ref{app:concentration}). This replicates fragility on other nonces of the same tasks; it does not predict which new tasks are fragile. % src: analysis/fork/out/task_fragility_20261005T134836Z.md (Split-half fragility, both directions); analysis/fork/out/task_fragility_split_20261005T134815Z.json; derived: 68=85-17

\paragraph{Which mode is evaluated decides which rescues are counted.} On divergent states, the average effect of the action swap has the same point estimate under Q's and B's continuation (W4 pool: $\DQ=\DB=+0.5$\pp{}), and no average gain is detected for upgrading this step, the rest, or both ($G=+0.5$, $H=+0.2$, $F=+0.7$\pp{}; all fixed-pool and new-task intervals cover zero, Appendix~\ref{app:r2main}). % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§1 pools, W4:all; F - H = Δ_B); analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.json
Averages hide states where the single-mode evaluations disagree (exploratory, post hoc). In a \emph{joint rescue}, only $\YY{BB}$ succeeds (Figure~\ref{fig:case}a). These are 4.0\% of W4 divergent states (fixed-pool 95\% [3.5, 4.5]; new-task [2.7, 5.3]), and testing only the current action misses them, because B's action needs B's continuation too. In a \emph{rescue undone}, B's action rescues under Q's continuation ($\YY{BQ}=1$, $\YY{QQ}=0$) but B's takeover undoes it ($\YY{BB}=0$). These are 4.8\% (fixed-pool [4.1, 5.4]; new-task [3.2, 6.5]), and testing only a whole takeover, which swaps the action and everything after it, misses them. % src: analysis/fork/out/outcome_classes_20261004T143614Z.md
A conditional label-permutation reference (which keeps how many of the four branches succeed) already produces both patterns at similar size (joint rescue 4.0\% vs 3.5\%; rescue undone 4.8\% vs 5.8\%); the observed frequencies give no generalisable evidence of extra enrichment (Appendix~\ref{app:offdiag}). The reference does not model execution, so it does not say how these outcomes arise. Whatever their cause, the patterns mean that a gain measured for action replacement cannot be reused as the gain of a whole takeover, or the reverse. % src: analysis/fork/out/pattern_ref_20261005T134721Z.md (§1, pool W4:all)

\paragraph{A gain measured for one mode does not transfer to another under a budget.} We rank tasks by one mode's mean gain on half of the nonce blocks and execute a mode on the same tasks in the other half (exploratory, post hoc; known tasks across nonces, not new tasks or single decisions). When the top 10\% of tasks receive joint replacement, choosing them by the joint-replacement label gains $+4.35$\pp{} of all units and choosing them by the continuation label gains $+1.52$\pp{}, a paired loss of $2.82$\pp{} (crossed 95\% [1.40, 3.90], uncorrected; all budgets and directions in Appendix~\ref{app:transfer}). On ordinary decision states the corresponding differences are not resolved. A selection label should match the intervention that will be executed. % src: analysis/fork/out/label_transfer_20261005T185537Z.md (Bank P dev_all units, pool W4, split-half, budget 10%); docs/insights/LABEL_TRANSFER_POSTHOC.md
