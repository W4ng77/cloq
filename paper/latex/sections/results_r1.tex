\paragraph{Many rescues, many damages, small net.} On the \tautwo{} development panel (85 tasks $\times$ 20 complete blocks), replacing a W4 agent's first tool-structure-divergent action with B's changes the task outcome in $M=15.5$--$20.9$\% of units (Figure~\ref{fig:case}b). Rescues and damages are of similar size, $r=6.8$--$11.4$ and $d=7.5$--$10.1$\pp{}, so the net gain $\Gall$ lies between $-1.9$ and $+2.9$\pp{}. Two fixed-pool intervals exclude zero (negative for q4 GPTQ-W4, positive for q8 GPTQ-W4); no new-task interval does. Pooled over the four W4 cells, $\Gall=+0.7$ [$-0.3$, $+1.6$]\pp{} with $M=18.6$\%. KV8 cells turn over fewer outcomes ($M=6.5$--$8.1$\%, a descriptive comparison). % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md
$\Gall$ is a local replacement effect, not an end-to-end comparison (Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}).

\paragraph{Splitting the upgrade changes which states it rescues.} On divergent states, the average estimates of the action swap are the same under Q's and under B's continuation (W4 pool: $\DQ=G=+0.5$ [$-0.7$, $+1.7$], $\DB=+0.5$ [$-1.0$, $+2.1$]\pp{}), and no average gain is detected for upgrading this step, the rest, or both ($H=+0.2$, $F=+0.7$\pp{}, all intervals covering zero). % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§1 pools, W4:all; F - H = Δ_B)
The averages hide two opposite groups of states (exploratory, post hoc; W4 pool; \% of divergent states; fixed-pool, then new-task 95\% intervals; Figure~\ref{fig:modes}b). In a \emph{joint rescue}, 4.0\% ([3.5, 4.5]; [2.7, 5.3]), only $\YY{BB}$ succeeds, so $F$ counts a rescue that $G$ and $H$ both miss (Figure~\ref{fig:case}a). In a \emph{rescue undone}, 4.8\% ([4.1, 5.4]; [3.2, 6.5]), B's action rescues under Q's continuation ($\YY{BQ}=1$, $\YY{QQ}=0$) while B's takeover of the continuation undoes it ($\YY{BB}=0$), so $G$ counts a rescue that $F$ misses. More broadly, changing the action changes the outcome under at least one continuation in 34.9\% ([33.6, 36.0]; [28.8, 41.0]), and changing the continuation controller does so under at least one action in 29.6\% ([28.5, 30.7]; [24.2, 35.3]). % src: analysis/fork/out/outcome_classes_20261004T143614Z.md
The two sets overlap in the 26.9\% ([25.7, 28.1]; [22.1, 32.0]) where the swap's effect depends on the continuation, so the shares must not be added; all four arms agree in 62.4\%. These shares describe operational consequences of specific upgrade modes, not a distinct interaction mechanism (five classes, a label-permutation reference, task share of variance and tests: Appendix~\ref{app:offdiag}). % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§2; crossed column); analysis/fork/out/outcome_classes_20261004T143614Z.md (TL;DR classes)

\paragraph{Paired comparisons distinguish upgrade modes.} All three upgrade modes are measured on the same states, so they can be compared by paired differences (Figure~\ref{fig:modes}a). Over the four core W4 cells, $G-H$ is a fixed Holm family ($m=4$; fixed task pool, $t$ over blocks). In both Qwen3-8B cells, action replacement outperforms continuation takeover: $G-H=+2.9$ (GPTQ-W4) and $+3.4$\pp{} (AWQ-W4), Holm-adjusted $p=.013$ and $.012$; this ranks the two modes and does not show that replacing the action beats QQ. In the Qwen3-4B cells the point estimates favour continuation takeover ($-3.6$ and $-1.3$\pp{}), but neither is significant after Holm adjustment. All four new-task intervals include zero, so the finding is limited to the fixed task pool and stays exploratory (intervals and further cautions: Appendix~\ref{app:r2main}). % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_multiplicity_20261001T225727Z.md; analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§1)
In the low-success stress cell q8 RTN-W3, continuation takeover beats action replacement ($G-H=-15.7$ [$-27.5$, $-3.9$]\pp{}, three blocks). % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§1, q8:rtn_w3 row)
For Qwen3-14B GPTQ-W4 on BFCL (one exploratory configuration), action replacement wins ($G-H=+5.3$ [$+2.3$, $+8.4$]\pp{}), and action and continuation are complementary there (Appendix~\ref{app:r2main}). % src: analysis/fork/out/ext_q14_20261002T085647Z.md (§2, bfcl:q14:gptq_w4_d1)
The joint upgrade $F$ has the largest point estimate in both examples, and we compare no compute costs, so no cost-optimal mode is established.

\paragraph{Other states, tasks and environments.} On BFCL multi-turn and ALFWorld, which have no simulated user (one run per item), the replacement turns over 5.2--24.6\% of items in both directions, and the off-diagonal share is 4.7--17.1\% in BFCL W4 cells and 32.8--40.0\% in q8 ALFWorld W4 cells (post hoc; Appendix~\ref{app:extr2}). % src: analysis/fork/out/ext_20261001T135106Z.md; analysis/fork/out/ext_r2_20261002T024433Z.md (§3, §6)
On 34 unseen telecom tasks, under a protocol frozen before any holdout unit ran (12 fresh blocks, three W4 cells), the replacement again rescued and damaged ($r=+5.21$, $d=+9.77$\pp{}), and task fragility repeated across nonce halves ($\rho=0.59$). The primary $\Gall=-4.57$\pp{} (crossed 90\% [$-7.17$, $-1.96$]) is \emph{inconclusive} by the pre-registered readings, which had no negative category; outside them, the interval lies below zero (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}). % src: analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md; docs/insights/PREREG_FORK_CONFIRM.md §0
At ordinary (Bank U) states, turnover is lower (W4 cells, U1 and U2B: 4.7--9.0\%), the net gain covers zero, and most outcome changes come from differences in text, whose labels did not reproduce under a second user simulator, so we do not report them as a property of the action (Appendix~\ref{app:banku}). % src: analysis/fork/out/bank_u_f01_f02_f03_20261002T003037Z.md (§2); analysis/fork/out/simctrl_20261001T212742Z.md
On the shared q8 BF16-sourced panel (\S\ref{sec:r4}), B's action alone is resolved in no cell, while B's takeover or full substitution is in several (Appendix~\ref{app:panels}). % src: analysis/fork/out/newmethod_q8_20261004T051538Z.md (τ² U2B all states: G, H, F with crossed columns)
