\paragraph{Two-sided turnover at the fork.} Table~\ref{tab:atlas} reports every cell with its denominator. On the \tautwo{} panel (85 tasks $\times$ 20 complete blocks), replacing a W4 agent's first divergent action changes the task outcome in $M=15.5$--$20.9$\% of units. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md
Rescues and damages are of similar size: $r=6.8$--$11.4$ and $d=7.5$--$10.1$\pp{}. The fixed-pool $\Gall$ lies between $-1.9$ and $+2.9$\pp{}. Its intervals exclude zero in two cells, negative for q4 GPTQ-W4 and positive for q8 GPTQ-W4, and none of the crossed intervals do. Pooled over the four W4 cells, $\Gall$ is $+0.7$ [$-0.3$, $+1.6$]\pp{} with $M=18.6$\%. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md
KV8 turns over fewer outcomes ($M=6.5$--$8.1$\%). Stress cells reach similar $M$ with only three blocks. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md
 The external benchmarks show the same two-sided pattern, with W4 turnover of 5.2--24.6\% of items; $\Gall$ covers zero except for BFCL q4 AWQ-W4. % src: analysis/fork/out/ext_20260930T221613Z.md
Small net values therefore coexist with substantial turnover in both directions. $\Gall$ is a quantity at the forked state. How end-to-end success compares is a separate question, and our end-to-end intervals are too wide to call it small (Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}).

\paragraph{What the $2\times2$ adds.} On divergent states, the mean action, controller and interaction terms are mostly indistinguishable from zero (Table~\ref{tab:atlas}). The clearest exception is q8 GPTQ-W4. There B's action helps under B's continuation ($A=+5.0$ [$+3.2$, $+6.8$]), and B's continuation helps after B's action ($C=+2.1$ [$+0.2$, $+4.0$]). Interaction is frequent at the state level: $I\neq0$ on about 15--30\% of divergent states. Its mean interval still covers zero in every W4 cell. Strict reversals, where the better action under one controller is the worse under the other, occur on 1.2--4.2\% of divergent states. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md (strict reversal column); analysis/fork/out/w67_audit_v2_20261001T0600Z_20261001T070500Z.md (note on I != 0)
At the level of means, then, neither the current action nor the continuation controller dominates. Consequences are state-specific and two-sided, which motivates looking at \emph{which} states and \emph{which} differences carry them.

\paragraph{Concentration in fragile tasks (exploratory, development).} Outcome flips are not spread evenly. The per-task dispersion of $M$ is 4.0--8.7 times its binomial null in the W4 and KV8 cells. The top 20\% of tasks hold 57--71\% of all flips. % src: analysis/fork/out/consequence_v2_20261001T070850Z.json (concentration.dispersion_index, share_of_consequences_in_top20pct_tasks)
We split the 20 blocks into two disjoint halves of 10 nonces. Task-level $M$ correlates across halves (Spearman 0.52--0.86). The direction repeats too. Tasks that were net-rescue in one half have a mean $G$ in the other half that is 14--51\pp{} higher than that of tasks that were net-damage, in all 12 cell-directions. % src: analysis/fork/out/w67_audit_v2_20261001T0600Z_20261001T070500Z.md (Concentration and Direction, panel85)
A Freedman--Lane test conditions on measured baselines: QQ success propensity and BF16 success, plus trajectory length in a post-hoc variant. With them, one half's task rate still predicts the other's in all 6 configurations $\times$ 2 directions for both $M$ and $G$ (Holm-adjusted). This is a \emph{retrospective conditional association} on viewed development data. It is carried by retail. Airline alone gives 8 of 12 for $M$ (5 after Holm), and 3 of 12 under a heteroscedasticity-robust wild bootstrap. % src: analysis/fork/out/w67_audit_v2_20261001T0600Z_20261001T070500Z.md (panel85, airline27 counts)
Direction persistence beyond flip opportunity holds more often for rescues than for damages, in 11 versus 6 of the 12 cell-directions. Different quantizers flip the same tasks: the task-identity null is rejected in 7 of 8 quantizer pairs. When both flip, however, they agree in direction only 42--81\% of the time. % src: analysis/fork/out/w67_audit_v2_20261001T0600Z_20261001T070500Z.md (Cross-quantizer co-susceptibility)
This overlaps with the stable failure set of \citet{jang2026flat}. What is new here is the signed, two-sided view: shared fragility, but with near-random relative direction across quantizers.

\paragraph{Pre-registered confirmation (pending).} Before any holdout unit was run, we froze a confirmatory protocol (sha256 \texttt{902892dd\ldots}). It covers 34 unseen telecom tasks $\times$ 12 fresh nonce blocks $\times$ 3 cells (q4 GPTQ-W4, q8 GPTQ-W4, q8 AWQ-W4, weighted 1/2, 1/4, 1/4). % src: docs/insights/PREREG_FORK_CONFIRM.md §0; manifests/fork/splits_v1.json (holdout.prereg_sha256)
The primary endpoint is $\Gall$, read on a crossed task $\times$ block 90\% interval against $\pm$3\pp{} bounds. The secondaries are two-sidedness ($r>0$ and $d>0$) and cross-half repeatability of task fragility. The repeatability test compares real task pairings with persona-preserving random pairings. It makes no claim beyond task difficulty. On development data only, the planned crossed interval width at $K=12$ is about 4.1\pp{}. % src: analysis/fork/out/holdout_k_planning_20261001T144122Z.md
