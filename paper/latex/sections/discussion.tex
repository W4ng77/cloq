\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What an upgrade evaluation should report.} A study of precision routing or escalation should report the net task gain of the intervention it actually executes, as rescues and damages, together with that intervention's cost: the current action only, a continuation takeover, or both, under the continuation that runs afterwards. A gain measured for one of these is not the gain of another (\S\ref{sec:value}). Enrichment of outcome-changing decisions validates a signal's sensitivity, not its value; our trigger result concerns one frozen Q-only rule, and learned routers such as DMR \citep{li2026dynamicmp} and ReDAct \citep{piatrashyn2026redact} are untested (\S\ref{sec:r4}). Our experiments measure task value. They do not give a cost-optimal upgrade policy, and we measure no cost (simulated quantization; Limitations).

\paragraph{How to read flips.} The two references show which consequences appear without more bits (\S\ref{sec:sameprec}). They are interventions on their own populations, not noise floors to subtract from B's turnover. One hypothesis, not an identified mechanism, is that a change in the history the agent reads propagates through the continuation. We tested one key-order change and specific checkpoint substitutions, not a perturbation curve, so the results do not say that any token change has these consequences.

\paragraph{Precision is not shown to be irrelevant.} Two fixed-pool intervals exclude zero (uncorrected), the holdout estimate is negative (classified inconclusive by the frozen readings), and at common states equivalence of B's and Q$'$'s actions is not shown. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md (cells G block intervals); analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md (TL;DR; Readings); analysis/fork/out/sameprec_20261004T070601Z.md (Amendment A1); analysis/fork/out/commonstate_q8ext_20261003T231159Z.md

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We evaluate a precision upgrade as a specified intervention at a restored state whose replay is audited: we count the rescues and damages of replacing the action, the continuation or both, and read them against a same-bit-width checkpoint and a representation change that keeps the tool operation identical. These references also change outcomes (two-sided for the same-bit-width checkpoint; observed in both directions, exploratory, for key order), and at common states B's extra action value over the same-bit-width checkpoint is not resolved; equivalence is not shown. Outcome sensitivity alone therefore does not show that more bits are needed. Upgrade evaluations should report the rescues and damages of the executed intervention under the intended continuation, read them against reference interventions, and validate selection rules by net value, not flip enrichment.
