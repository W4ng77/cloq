\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{When to escalate.} Average success, action divergence and replacement gain answer different questions and can disagree: the four W4 cells have small end-to-end differences (Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}), yet replacing their first tool-structure-divergent action turns over many units with a near-zero pooled net gain (\S\ref{sec:r1}), so the first two cannot tell whether a BF16 fallback would raise success. Escalation can also hurt (q4 KV8 $H$, \S\ref{sec:r2}; telecom holdout, \S\ref{sec:r3}). Text actions should be evaluated as actions but, given \S\ref{sec:r3}, not reported as a property of the action without a simulator control. We recommend reporting $r$, $d$ and $M$ next to any net gain, each with its denominator, and keeping fixed-pool and new-task intervals apart.

\paragraph{Which part to replace.} We found no replacement unit that is best in every cell. On the fixed task pool, $G-H$ is positive in the two q8 W4 cells (Holm $p=.013$, $.012$) and strongly negative in the severe q8 RTN-W3 stress cell. These within-cell orderings are exploratory: new-task intervals mostly cover zero, a second simulator (W4 cells only) neither confirms nor reverses the q8 orderings, and cross-cell contrasts are descriptive because cells select different states. Measure $G$, $H$ and $F$ per configuration and environment. % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_multiplicity_20261001T225727Z.md
