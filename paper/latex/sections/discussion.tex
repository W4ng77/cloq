\paragraph{Why a fallback cannot be judged by its average gain.} A post hoc decomposition of the \tautwo{} W4 pool accounts for the small net: where QQ fails (non-divergent units count as no rescue), B's action rescues 14.6\% of units, and where QQ succeeds it damages 26.1\%, but QQ succeeds in only $34.6\%$. \paragraph{Recommendations.} (i)~Specify the intervention and report its rescues, damages and net on the same states, with fixed-pool and new-task intervals. (ii)~Include a same-precision reference. (iii)~Match the selection label to the executed intervention. % nocheck: list labels (i)-(iii)

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Rather than one success or flip rate, our method evaluates a precision upgrade as one specified intervention at an exactly restored state, measures the rescues and damages of replacing the action, the continuation or both on the same states, and checks their size against a same-precision substitute and a meaning-preserving representation change. On our agents these references also change outcomes in both directions, so outcome sensitivity alone does not show that more bits are needed. Still open are which states deserve an upgrade (our signals find outcome-changing decisions, not net-beneficial ones), the cause of the holdout's negative estimate, deployed low-bit kernels, and wider simulator and model coverage.
