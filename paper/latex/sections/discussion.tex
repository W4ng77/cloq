\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{When to escalate.} Average success, the rate of action divergence and the gain from replacing an action answer different questions, and our data show they can disagree. The four W4 cells have small end-to-end differences with wide intervals (Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}). Their Bank P states replace actions with 15.5--20.9\% turnover, but the pooled replacement gains are near zero. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md
Reporting only the first two cannot tell whether a BF16 fallback is worth its cost. The outcome labels of text-only differences at ordinary states did not reproduce reliably under an alternative, stochastic relay simulator (\S\ref{sec:r3}). Text actions should be evaluated as actions, but their consequences should not be reported as a property of the action without a simulator control. We recommend reporting $r$, $d$ and $M$ next to any net replacement gain, each with its denominator. Fixed-pool and new-task intervals should be kept apart.

\paragraph{Which part to replace.} The unit of replacement matters, and its best choice is not universal. In the q8 W4 cells, replacing the current action beats replacing the continuation. In q8 GPTQ-W4, handing over the rest of the episode adds further gain. In a severely damaged W3 cell, only the continuation matters (\S\ref{sec:r2}). These within-cell results suggest measuring $G$, $H$ and $F$ per deployment configuration rather than transferring one answer across quantizers.

\paragraph{Escalation can hurt.} Replacements damage about as often as they rescue (\S\ref{sec:r1}). A handover can also remove a recovery the quantized agent would have made: in one frozen case (q4 AWQ-W4, retail task 61), Q recovers from its own failed write and succeeds, while B continuing after that write fails. % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§4 Case 6)
In q4 KV8, continuing with B lowers success on average (\S\ref{sec:r2}), and on the unseen telecom tasks replacing the first divergent action lowers it by 4.6\pp{} (\S\ref{sec:r3}). % src: analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md
A rule that escalates whenever B's action ``looks better'' would not gain.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} We do not claim success equivalence between BF16 and W4 agents; equivalence tests at $\pm$3\pp{} fail. % src: analysis/insights/iter_4/out/success_ni_ALIGNED_20260930T213009Z.md
$G$, $H$ and $F$ are oracle quantities, not the value of a deployable policy (\S\ref{sec:r4}). We do not claim a W$\times$KV interaction, and we do not present static precision restoration as a method (Appendix~\ref{app:withdrawn}).
