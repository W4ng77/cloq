\paragraph{Match the label to the executed mode.} The three upgrade modes spend BF16 computation at different points. Replacing one action lets Q handle its consequences. Taking over the continuation preserves Q's current action. Joint replacement changes both. A selector's outcome target should match the mode it will execute. A label for joint takeover can count a success that action replacement cannot obtain. Conversely, a success under Q's continuation can disappear under B's. These patterns make the evaluation target consequential even when average gains are similar. BF16 also need not be the better end-to-end policy: q4 GPTQ-W4 achieves 40.0\% end-to-end DB success versus BF16's 35.8\% (Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}).

\paragraph{What a selector needs to predict.} Our frozen margin rule finds outcome changes on later blocks of the same tasks. It captures rescues and damages together, giving no reliable net benefit over matched random selection. The finding concerns this frozen rule. The hindsight oracle identifies available gains after outcomes are known; it does not establish that Q-visible features can predict them. Learned routing should be evaluated on held-out decisions by signed task benefit. Cost-aware comparisons should also match BF16 computation, because continuation takeover can use more decisions than a single-action replacement.

\paragraph{Why fidelity can track flips without guiding upgrades.} At a fixed continuation, outcome changes require different rendered actions. Their frequency therefore combines two quantities: how often the action changes, and how often that change affects success. An exploratory diagnosis across quantization configurations places KL's strongest association in the first quantity ($\rho=0.99$). Within configurations, its mean AUC is 0.46 for a flip among differing actions and 0.55 for the direction of a flip. These diagnostics locate the gap between numerical fidelity and signed task benefit (Appendix~\ref{app:kl}); they do not establish a causal mechanism. % src: analysis/fork/out/kl_decomp_20261005T000559Z.md (§2, §3)

\paragraph{From realised consequences to deployment value.} Exact replay makes a comparison auditable under fixed execution conditions. It does not make that setting representative of deployment. Key-order reversal demonstrates that individual labels can depend on the representation the agent reads. To estimate expected benefit, specify a distribution of prompts and user conditions, repeat paired branches across it, and average the success differences. Average benefit and individual label stability are separate questions. Our task-level intervals address task sampling; they do not cover every change to the user model or serving stack. On the telecom holdout, the action-only estimate indicates a net loss in the tested setting, although the frozen protocol has no category for it. Because domain and tasks change together, these data do not identify the cause or establish a general domain-transfer effect.

\paragraph{Coverage and practical use.} The scope checks ask whether the distinction between outcome changes and benefit also matters beyond the main panel (Table~\ref{tab:scope}). These checks vary the environment, model family and quantization setting, using the populations in Table~\ref{tab:scope}. For a fallback evaluation, choose the intended upgrade mode, measure its rescues and damages on paired states, interpret them with reference substitutions, and validate the selection policy's net benefit. Appendix~\ref{app:scope} reports their paired estimates, controls and admission rules.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Precision fallback should be evaluated by the task benefit of the upgrade actually executed. Our audited forks distinguish action replacement, continuation takeover and joint replacement. The W4 panel shows substantial rescues and damages with little net gain. Same-precision and representation references establish why flips alone cannot diagnose a need for more bits. A useful selector must identify signed benefit under the intended continuation.
