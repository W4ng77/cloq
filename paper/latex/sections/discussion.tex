\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{When to escalate.} Average success, action divergence and replacement gain answer different questions. The four W4 cells have small end-to-end differences (Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}), yet replacing their first tool-structure-divergent action turns over many units with a near-zero pooled net gain (\S\ref{sec:r1}). Escalation can also hurt (q4 KV8 $H$, \S\ref{sec:r2}; telecom holdout, \S\ref{sec:r3}). We recommend reporting $r$, $d$ and $M$ next to any net gain, each with its denominator, and keeping fixed-pool and new-task intervals apart.

\paragraph{Which part to replace.} No replacement unit was best in every cell. On the fixed task pool, $G-H$ is positive in the two q8 W4 cells (Holm $p=.013$, $.012$) and strongly negative in the severe q8 RTN-W3 stress cell. These orderings are exploratory: new-task intervals mostly cover zero, a second simulator (W4 cells only) neither confirms nor reverses the q8 orderings, and cells select different states. $G$, $H$ and $F$ should be measured per configuration and environment. % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_multiplicity_20261001T225727Z.md

\paragraph{Bank P and Bank U see different parts of the effect.} Bank P skips text-only differences by design, yet 150 of the 208 outcome-changing rows at ordinary states are such differences (\S\ref{sec:r3}). Bank P measures the consequences of tool-structure divergences and Bank U those of any action difference, so they are not two estimates of one quantity. The text-difference labels are also the ones that did not reproduce under a second simulator.
