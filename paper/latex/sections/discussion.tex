\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{When to escalate.} Average success, action divergence and replacement gain answer different questions and can disagree. The four W4 cells have small end-to-end differences with wide intervals (Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}). Replacing their first tool-structure-divergent action turns over 15.5--20.9\% of task$\times$block units (full pool), but the pooled net gain is near zero ($+0.7$ [$-0.3$, $+1.6$]\pp{}). % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md
Reporting only the first two cannot tell whether a BF16 fallback would raise success. Text actions should be evaluated as actions but, given \S\ref{sec:r3}, not reported as a property of the action without a simulator control. We recommend reporting $r$, $d$ and $M$ next to any net gain, each with its denominator, and keeping fixed-pool and new-task intervals apart.

\paragraph{Which part to replace.} We found no replacement unit that is best in every cell. On the fixed task pool, $G-H$ is positive in the two q8 W4 cells (Holm $p=.013$, $.012$) and strongly negative in the severe q8 RTN-W3 stress cell. These within-cell orderings are exploratory: new-task intervals mostly cover zero, a second simulator (W4 cells only) neither confirms nor reverses the q8 orderings, and cross-cell contrasts are descriptive because cells select different states. Measure $G$, $H$ and $F$ per configuration and environment. % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_multiplicity_20261001T225727Z.md

\paragraph{Escalation can hurt.} Replacements damage as well as rescue (\S\ref{sec:r1}). In q4 KV8, B continuing has a negative fixed-pool estimate ($H=-2.5$ [$-4.5$, $-0.5$]\pp{}, unadjusted; new-task interval covers zero; \S\ref{sec:r2}); on 34 unseen telecom tasks, replacing the first divergent action has an estimated effect of $-4.6$\pp{} (crossed 90\% CI $-7.2$ to $-2.0$; pre-registered primary reading inconclusive; \S\ref{sec:r3}). % src: analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md; analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§1)
