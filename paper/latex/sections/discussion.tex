\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{From model change to intervention value.} A single success or flip rate mixes three questions: how often an upgrade changes the outcome, which intervention the gain label refers to, and what higher precision adds over a same-precision change (\S\ref{sec:value}, \S\ref{sec:sameprec}). We do not conclude that precision is irrelevant: the resolved fixed-pool cells and the negative holdout estimate remain. % src: analysis/fork/out/sameprec_20261004T070601Z.md (Amendment A1); analysis/fork/out/commonstate_q8ext_20261003T231159Z.md

\paragraph{Why a fallback cannot be judged by its average gain.} A post hoc decomposition of the \tautwo{} W4 pool explains the small net. Among task$\times$block units where QQ fails (non-divergent units count as no rescue), B's action rescues 14.6\%; where QQ succeeds, it damages 26.1\%. The totals are similar partly because QQ succeeds in only $34.6\%$ of units. The decomposition conditions on outcomes, so it cannot guide escalation online. % src: analysis/fork/out/task_fragility_20261005T134836Z.md (Conditional rates, pool)

\paragraph{Recommendations.} (i)~Specify the intervention and report its rescues, damages and net on the same states, with fixed-pool and new-task intervals. (ii)~Include a same-precision reference. (iii)~Match the selection label to the executed intervention. % nocheck: list labels (i)-(iii)
