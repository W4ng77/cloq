\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A quantized LLM agent is cheaper to serve than its BF16 reference, and a deployment can keep the reference as a fallback, as dynamic precision routing does \citep{li2026dynamicmp}. Suppose the quantized agent (Q) reaches a decision at which the BF16 agent (B) would act differently. There are three ways to spend B here: \textbf{replace only this step, let B take over from the next step, or both}. Average task success does not answer this. On our checkpoints, quantized-minus-BF16 success ranges from $-0.2$ to $+4.2$\pp{} across four W4 cells, and an equivalence test at $\pm$3\pp{} fails in every cell (Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}); even a precise end-to-end number would not say which step an upgrade should touch. % src: analysis/insights/iter_4/out/success_ni_ALIGNED_20260930T213009Z.md

Figure~\ref{fig:case}a shows one such decision from our data. In an airline task, Q looks up a reservation id that does not exist and receives ``not found''; B looks up the user's profile. B's action looks better, and we can check what it is worth. We froze the state and ran all four combinations of \{B's or Q's action\} $\times$ \{B or Q continuing\}. Only B's action followed by B's continuation succeeds. When B supplies only this step, Q continues, searches for flights, writes a change and fails; when Q's failed lookup stands and B takes over, B fails too. % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§4 Case 5)
The task value of an action is thus co-determined by the execution that follows it, and judging the action alone, however carefully, cannot reveal it.

We turn this observation into an evaluation. At a frozen state that replays bit-exactly, including the environment databases, the user simulator's view and the remaining step and error budget, we cross the source of the current action with the model that executes the rest of the episode (\S\ref{sec:method}). The four branches give, on the same state, the value of upgrading this step, the rest of the episode, or both. To read these values, we add a same-precision reference substitution: the replacement action and continuation come from a second W4 checkpoint of the same model, calibrated on a different sample, instead of from B. We study Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B under simulated weight and KV-cache quantization in \tautwo{} \citep{barres2025tau2}, BFCL multi-turn and ALFWorld. % nocheck: model sizes

\paragraph{Sensitivity is not value.} Two properties of an intervention are easily conflated. \emph{Sensitivity} is its ability to change task outcomes: how many units flip ($M$) and how often the effect of the action depends on the continuation. \emph{Value} is its ability to improve them: the net gain, which units it rescues and which it harms, and whether a selector can find the rescues in advance. Our central finding is that \textbf{the ability to change task outcomes and the ability to choose a beneficial precision intervention have to be measured separately.} The evidence has three links.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{BF16 replacement is sensitive but has small net value} (\S\ref{sec:value}). Replacing a W4 agent's first tool-structure-divergent action with B's changes the outcome in 15.5--20.9\% of task$\times$block units, with rescues and damages of similar size and a net effect of $-1.9$ to $+2.9$\pp{} (Figure~\ref{fig:case}b). In 26.9\% of divergent states the swap has an effect under one continuation and none, or the opposite, under the other. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md; analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§2)
\item \textbf{A same-precision substitution is about as sensitive, partly on different units} (\S\ref{sec:sameprec}). Swapping only the W4 calibration checkpoint also produces two-sided flips and continuation dependence of comparable size, so flips alone do not diagnose a need for precision. The two substitutions share many sensitive episodes (107 units flip under both, against 30 expected by chance), yet 138 of the 245 flipping units flip under only one of them. Equivalence of the two turnovers within $\pm$3\pp{} is not shown. % src: analysis/fork/out/sameprec_20261002T142834Z.md (Amendment A1); analysis/fork/out/sameprec_overlap_20261002T143218Z.md; derived: 138=64+74; 245=107+64+74
\item \textbf{Detecting sensitivity is not selecting value} (\S\ref{sec:r4}). A frozen Q-only uncertainty trigger captures more outcome-changing decisions than random selection, but they are rescues and damages alike: it captures 2--5 of the 34--38 net units that a hindsight oracle with the same budget shows are available. % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3); analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1a)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What should change in evaluation practice.} A study that reports how often a precision change flips outcomes, or how strongly a model's uncertainty predicts flips, has measured sensitivity. To claim that a precision intervention is worth making, it must also report value, separately: the net gain with its rescue and harm counts and their denominators, under the execution that follows the intervention; and it must report the same quantities for a same-precision reference substitution, so that what higher precision adds can be told apart from what a change of model at the same precision already does.

\paragraph{Contributions.} (1)~\emph{An evaluation methodology} that measures, on the same state, the task value of the current action, of the subsequent execution and of both, with a same-precision reference substitution for reading them. The factorial design is standard; the contribution is making the counterfactuals a precision decision needs executable and auditable in closed-loop agents (exact state restoration, budget accounting, replay acceptance, explicit denominators). (2)~\emph{Empirical findings} that separate sensitivity from value: an action's value varies with the execution that follows; a fixed-precision checkpoint swap produces sensitivity of comparable size; and a sensitivity signal locates outcome changes without yet converting them into net gain. % nocheck: list labels (1)-(2)
