\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A quantized LLM agent is cheaper to serve than its BF16 reference, and a deployment can keep the reference as a fallback, as dynamic precision routing does \citep{li2026dynamicmp}. Suppose the quantized agent (Q) reaches a decision at which the BF16 agent (B) would act differently. There are three ways to spend B here: \textbf{replace only this step, let B take over from the next step, or both}. Average task success does not answer this. On our checkpoints, Q's success minus B's ranges from $-0.2$ to $+4.2$\pp{} across four W4 cells, and equivalence within $\pm$3\pp{} is not established in any cell (Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}). Even a precise end-to-end number would not say which step an upgrade should touch. % src: analysis/insights/iter_4/out/success_ni_ALIGNED_20260930T213009Z.md

Figure~\ref{fig:case}a shows one such decision from our data. In an airline task, Q looks up a reservation id that does not exist and receives ``not found''; B looks up the user's profile. We froze the state and ran all four combinations of \{B's or Q's action\} $\times$ \{B or Q continuing\}. Only B's action followed by B's continuation succeeds. When B supplies only this step, Q continues, searches for flights, writes a change and fails; when Q's failed lookup stands and B takes over, B fails too. % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§4 Case 5)
In this state B's action is worth a success only if B also continues, and no evaluation of the action alone can show that.

A fallback decision therefore needs the value of one specified intervention, measured under the policy that runs afterwards. Recent work forks live agent trajectories and continues them with a different model, against same-model control forks \citep{gonuguntla2026replay}; in that design the new model writes both the next action and everything after it, so the effect of the action cannot be separated from that of the continuation. We cross the source of the current action with the model that executes the rest of the episode, at a frozen state that replays bit-exactly, including the environment databases, the user simulator's view and the remaining step and error budget (\S\ref{sec:method}). To test whether the consequences require a higher bit width, we also put a second W4 checkpoint of the same model in place of B. We study Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B \citep{yang2025qwen3} under simulated weight and KV-cache quantization in \tautwo{} \citep{yao2024tau,barres2025tau2}, BFCL multi-turn \citep{patil2025bfcl} and ALFWorld \citep{shridhar2021alfworld}, and Mistral-Small-3.2-24B \citep{mistral2025small32} on ordinary BF16-sourced \tautwo{} states. % nocheck: model sizes

\paragraph{Findings.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{A specified upgrade has two-sided consequences and a small net} (\S\ref{sec:value}). Replacing a W4 agent's first tool-structure-divergent action with B's changes the outcome in 15.5--20.9\% of task$\times$block units, with rescues and damages of similar size and a net effect of $-1.9$ to $+2.9$\pp{} (Figure~\ref{fig:case}b). On held-out telecom tasks under a pre-registered protocol the net is $-4.6$\pp{}, inconclusive by the pre-registered readings. The consequences recur on the same tasks under other nonces (exploratory, post hoc). Action-only and whole-takeover evaluation each miss some rescues that the other counts (exploratory, post hoc; Figure~\ref{fig:case}a). On known tasks (exploratory, post hoc), selecting tasks for joint replacement by the continuation gain lost value at first divergences; on ordinary states this is unresolved (\S\ref{sec:value}). % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md; analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md; analysis/fork/out/task_fragility_20261005T134836Z.md
\item \textbf{Outcome sensitivity is not by itself evidence that more bits are needed} (\S\ref{sec:sameprec}). A second checkpoint at the same bit width, at W4 and at W3, produces two-sided flips and continuation dependence at comparable size (equivalence not shown), and on common states BF16's additional action value over a second W4 checkpoint is not resolved. % src: analysis/fork/out/sameprec_20261004T070601Z.md (Amendment A1); analysis/fork/out/commonstate_q8ext_20261003T231159Z.md
\item \textbf{Current sensitivity signals do not yet select beneficial upgrades} (\S\ref{sec:r4}). Across eleven q8 cells, approximate token KL is strongly associated with action divergence but has not shown reliable predictive power for task consequences or net value (exploratory). A frozen Q-only uncertainty trigger selects more outcome-changing decisions than random selection at two of three escalation rates (held-out blocks of the same tasks). It selects rescues and damages alike. Its net is $+4$, $+2$ and $+5$ outcomes, against $+34$, $+38$ and $+38$ for a hindsight oracle with the same budget. % src: analysis/fork/out/kl_decomp_20261005T000559Z.md; analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3); analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1a)
\item \textbf{Duration and scope} (\S\ref{sec:scope}). Reversing tool-argument key order, an identical operation, changes 6.7\% of outcomes on 300 verified states, not distinguished from 9.0\% for B's action (exploratory). As a supplementary finding, in SpinQuant-W4A4, chosen after seeing its sustained-takeover value ($+11.8$\pp{}; exploratory, 3 blocks, BF16-sourced states), a one-decision BF16 takeover shows no detectable net gain, and a four-decision takeover falls $5.5$\pp{} short of sustained takeover (crossed 95\% [0.8, 10.6]); under GPTQ-W4 the effect of takeover length is unresolved. % src: analysis/fork/out/takeover_20261005T1604Z.md (Reading); analysis/fork/out/takeover_20261005T1604Z.json (cells.spinquant_w4a4.primary.all_states_pp)
The replacement also rescues and damages at ordinary states, on BFCL and ALFWorld (post hoc), and for Mistral-Small-3.2-24B. % src: analysis/fork/keyorder/eligibility_v1.md (TL;DR; Eligible); nocheck: model name
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Contributions.} (1)~A same-state protocol with audited exact replay that measures action, continuation and joint replacement separately, counts rescues and damages, and reads them against a same-precision reference and a hindsight oracle. Valuing a step under the continuing policy is standard \citep{setlur2024rewarding}; the decomposition and the references are what we add. (2)~The empirical findings above. % nocheck: list labels (1)-(2)
