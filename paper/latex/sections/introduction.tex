\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A quantized LLM agent is cheaper to serve than its BF16 reference, and a deployment can keep the reference as a fallback, as dynamic precision routing does \citep{li2026dynamicmp}. Suppose the quantized agent (Q) reaches a decision at which the BF16 agent (B) would act differently. There are three ways to spend B here: \textbf{replace only this step, let B take over from the next step, or both}. Average task success does not answer this. On our checkpoints, quantized-minus-BF16 success ranges from $-0.2$ to $+4.2$\pp{} across four W4 cells (positive: Q succeeds more often), and equivalence within $\pm$3\pp{} is not established in any cell (Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}); even a precise end-to-end number would not say which step an upgrade should touch. % src: analysis/insights/iter_4/out/success_ni_ALIGNED_20260930T213009Z.md

Figure~\ref{fig:case}a shows one such decision from our data. In an airline task, Q looks up a reservation id that does not exist and receives ``not found''; B looks up the user's profile. We froze the state and ran all four combinations of \{B's or Q's action\} $\times$ \{B or Q continuing\}. Only B's action followed by B's continuation succeeds. When B supplies only this step, Q continues, searches for flights, writes a change and fails; when Q's failed lookup stands and B takes over, B fails too. % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§4 Case 5)
Here the task value of B's action depends on the execution that follows it, and judging the action alone, however carefully, cannot reveal it.

The quantity a fallback decision needs is therefore not how much the model changed, but what a specific intervention is worth under the policy that executes afterwards. Recent work forks live agent trajectories and continues them with a different model, against same-model control forks \citep{gonuguntla2026replay}; there, the new model writes the next action and everything after it, so the two effects cannot be told apart. We cross the source of the current action with the model that executes the rest of the episode, at a frozen state that replays bit-exactly, including the environment databases, the user simulator's view and the remaining step and error budget (\S\ref{sec:method}). A same-precision substitution, a second W4 checkpoint of the same model in place of B, asks whether the consequences require a higher bit width. We study Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B \citep{yang2025qwen3} under simulated weight and KV-cache quantization in \tautwo{} \citep{yao2024tau,barres2025tau2}, BFCL multi-turn \citep{patil2025bfcl} and ALFWorld \citep{shridhar2021alfworld}, and Mistral-Small-3.2-24B \citep{mistral2025small32} on ordinary BF16-sourced \tautwo{} states. Three distinctions organise the results: an action change does not imply an outcome change; outcome changes are both rescues and damages; and consequences of B's action do not imply more value than a same-precision substitute would bring. % nocheck: model sizes

\paragraph{Findings.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{A specified upgrade has two-sided consequences and a small net} (\S\ref{sec:value}). Replacing a W4 agent's first tool-structure-divergent action with B's changes the outcome in 15.5--20.9\% of task$\times$block units, with rescues and damages of similar size and a net effect of $-1.9$ to $+2.9$\pp{} (Figure~\ref{fig:case}b). On held-out telecom tasks under a pre-registered protocol the net is $-4.6$\pp{}, inconclusive by the pre-registered readings. The consequences recur on the same tasks under other nonces (exploratory, post hoc). Action-only and whole-takeover evaluation each miss beneficial interventions that the other counts (Figure~\ref{fig:case}a), so the two cannot share one gain label. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md; analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md; analysis/fork/out/task_fragility_20261005T134836Z.md
\item \textbf{Outcome sensitivity is not evidence that more bits are needed} (\S\ref{sec:sameprec}). A second checkpoint at the same bit width, at W4 and at W3, produces two-sided flips and continuation dependence at comparable size (equivalence not shown), and on common states BF16's additional action value over a second W4 checkpoint is not resolved. % src: analysis/fork/out/sameprec_20261004T070601Z.md (Amendment A1); analysis/fork/out/commonstate_q8ext_20261003T231159Z.md
\item \textbf{Current sensitivity signals do not yet select beneficial upgrades} (\S\ref{sec:r4}). Approximate token KL is strongly associated with action divergence but has not shown reliable predictive power for task consequences or net value; a frozen Q-only uncertainty trigger captures more outcome-changing decisions than random selection at two of three escalation rates (held-out blocks of the same tasks), but rescues and damages alike: it nets $+4$, $+2$ and $+5$ outcomes, against $+34$, $+38$ and $+38$ for a hindsight oracle with the same budget. % src: analysis/fork/out/kl_decomp_20261005T000559Z.md; analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3); analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1a)
\item \textbf{Duration and scope} (\S\ref{sec:scope}). In SpinQuant-W4A4, a cell chosen after its sustained-takeover value ($+11.8$\pp{}) was seen, a one-decision BF16 takeover shows no detectable net gain and a four-decision takeover falls $5.5$\pp{} short of sustained takeover (crossed 95\% [0.8, 10.6]; exploratory, 3 blocks, BF16-sourced states); GPTQ-W4 is unresolved. % src: analysis/fork/out/takeover_20261005T1604Z.md (Reading); analysis/fork/out/takeover_20261005T1604Z.json (cells.spinquant_w4a4.primary.all_states_pp)
A key-order experiment on one representation change (300 states) is pending. At ordinary states, on BFCL and ALFWorld, and for Mistral-Small-3.2-24B, the replacement also both rescues and damages. % src: analysis/fork/keyorder/eligibility_v1.md (TL;DR; Eligible); nocheck: model name
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Contributions.} (1)~\emph{An executable, auditable same-state intervention protocol} that measures action-only, continuation-only and joint replacement separately, with a same-precision reference substitution. Valuing a step under the policy that continues is standard \citep{setlur2024rewarding}; we decompose an upgrade into action, continuation and joint replacement, count rescues and damages, and read them against a same-precision reference and a hindsight oracle, at exactly restored and audited states. (2)~\emph{Empirical findings}: the value of an upgrade depends on which intervention is evaluated; high turnover hides two-sided consequences and is not by itself evidence that more bits are needed; and current sensitivity signals have not established reliable beneficial selection. % nocheck: list labels (1)-(2)
