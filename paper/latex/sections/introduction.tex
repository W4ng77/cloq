\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Quantized LLM agents are usually evaluated by average task success next to the full-precision reference, and in closed-loop benchmarks such as \tautwo{} \citep{barres2025tau2} that comparison can look reassuring. On our checkpoints, quantized-minus-BF16 success ranges from $-0.2$ to $+4.2$\pp{} across four W4 cells. Its new-task (crossed) intervals are about $\pm$6--7\pp{} wide, so an equivalence test at $\pm$3\pp{} fails in every cell (Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}). % src: analysis/insights/iter_4/out/success_ni_ALIGNED_20260930T213009Z.md
Yet the quantized agent (Q) still takes actions that its BF16 reference (B) would not. If B is available as a fallback, as in dynamic precision routing \citep{li2026dynamicmp}, a practical question arises at every such state: \textbf{is it worth letting BF16 take over, for just this step or from here on?}

\paragraph{Judging the two actions does not answer this.} Consider a state from our data (q4 AWQ-W4, \tautwo{} airline task 31, nonce block f13, decision 1 of 12). Q looks up a reservation id that does not exist and receives ``not found''; B looks up the user's profile. B's action plainly looks better. We froze this state and ran all four combinations of \{B's or Q's action\} $\times$ \{B or Q continuing\}. Only one succeeds: B's action \emph{followed by} B. If B supplies only this step and Q continues, Q searches for flights, writes a change and fails. If Q's failed lookup stands and B continues, B also fails. % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§4 Case 5)
Here, the better-looking action has value only under one continuation. \textbf{This case illustrates why average success, action divergence and precision-replacement gain should be measured separately; R1 and R2 quantify how often it matters.}

\paragraph{What we do.} At a frozen state whose no-op replay is bit-exact, we cross the source of the current action with the controller of the remaining episode (Figure~\ref{fig:fork}). Three paired gains answer the three escalation questions on the same states: $G$ replaces only the current action, $H$ only the continuation, $F$ both (\S\ref{sec:method}). We apply the design to two state banks: first tool-structure divergences between B and Q along Q's trajectory, and ordinary states drawn independently of any outcome.

\paragraph{What we find.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{R1, replacing the current action} (\S\ref{sec:r1}). Replacing a W4 agent's action at the first decision where the two models' actions differ in tool structure (text-only differences are skipped) changes the outcome in 15.5--20.9\% of task-block units, rescuing about as often as damaging; the net gain is $-1.9$ to $+2.9$\pp{}. This is a local effect, not end-to-end equivalence. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md
\item \textbf{R2, where to replace} (\S\ref{sec:r2}). In about a quarter of W4 divergent states (pooled 26.9\%), the swap's effect depends on who continues; strict reversals are rare and the mean interaction is near zero. Orderings of the three gains are exploratory: on 80 re-run states, $G-H$ is $-2.5$\pp{} under the original simulator and $-8.5$\pp{} under a second (change $-6.0$ [$-16.2$, $+4.2$]); no ordering is established that transfers. % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md; analysis/fork/out/simctrl_p_20261002T015200Z.md
\item \textbf{R3, sampling and environment} (\S\ref{sec:r3}). At outcome-independent states most outcome changes under our simulator are text differences, but these labels did not reproduce reliably under another simulator. On 34 pre-registered unseen telecom tasks, the swap again rescues and damages, task fragility repeats, and the estimated effect is $-4.6$\pp{} (90\% CI $-7.2$ to $-2.0$), inconclusive under the frozen readings. BFCL and ALFWorld also turn over both ways. % src: analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md
\item \textbf{R4, finding replacements in advance} (\S\ref{sec:r4}). Q's token uncertainty is associated with divergences, and a frozen trigger captures more outcome-changing decisions than random selection at two of three rates, but is not shown to pick net-beneficial replacements.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Contributions.} (1)~An auditable same-state crossed intervention protocol for quantized agents: state restoration with bit-exact replay acceptance, full-action interventions, paired estimands ($G$, $H$, $F$ and the $(\DQ,\DB)$ table) with explicit denominators, and inference that separates a fixed task pool from new tasks. The $2\times2$ crossing itself is a standard factorial design. (2)~An empirical analysis over weight, KV-cache and stress quantization, two model sizes and three benchmarks (R1--R4). Withdrawn results are in Appendix~\ref{app:withdrawn}. % nocheck: list labels (1)-(2)
