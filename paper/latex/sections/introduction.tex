\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Quantized LLM agents are usually compared with their full-precision reference by average task success. In closed-loop benchmarks such as \tautwo{} \citep{barres2025tau2}, that comparison cannot distinguish difference from equivalence. On our checkpoints, quantized-minus-BF16 success ranges from $-0.2$ to $+4.2$\pp{} across four W4 cells, and the new-task (crossed) intervals are about $\pm$6--7\pp{} wide, so an equivalence test at $\pm$3\pp{} fails in every cell (Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}). % src: analysis/insights/iter_4/out/success_ni_ALIGNED_20260930T213009Z.md
Yet the quantized agent (Q) still takes actions that its BF16 reference (B) would not. If B is available as a fallback, as in dynamic precision routing \citep{li2026dynamicmp}, each such state poses a decision: \textbf{upgrade precision for this step only, from the next step on, or both?} The answer depends on the action the upgrade changes and on the execution that follows it.

\paragraph{Judging the two actions is not enough.} Consider one state from our data (q4 AWQ-W4, \tautwo{} airline task 31, decision 1 of 12). Q looks up a reservation id that does not exist and receives ``not found''; B looks up the user's profile. B's action looks better. We froze this state and ran all four combinations of \{B's or Q's action\} $\times$ \{B or Q continuing\}. Only B's action followed by B succeeds. If B supplies only this step, Q continues, searches for flights, writes a change and fails. If Q's failed lookup stands and B takes over, B fails too. % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§4 Case 5)
The better-looking action has task value under one continuation and none under the other, and no judgement of the action alone could show this.

\paragraph{A crossed same-state intervention.} At a frozen state whose no-op replay is bit-exact, we cross the source of the current action with the controller of the remaining episode (Figure~\ref{fig:fork}). This turns ``this step'' ($G$), ``from the next step'' ($H$) and ``both'' ($F$) into directly comparable interventions on the same states (\S\ref{sec:method}). We study Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B (simulated weight and KV-cache quantization, BF16 compute) in \tautwo{} with one greedy user simulator, and in BFCL multi-turn and ALFWorld. We fork at the first decision where the two models' actions differ in tool structure (text-only differences are skipped) and, separately, at ordinary states drawn independently of any outcome. % nocheck: model sizes

\paragraph{What we find.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{The value of an action depends on the controller that continues} (\S\ref{sec:r2}). In 26.9\% of development W4 divergent states, the same action swap has a different outcome effect under B's continuation than under Q's; strict reversals are rare. A post hoc analysis finds the same dependence on BFCL (4.7--17.1\% per W4 cell) and q8 ALFWorld (32.8--40.0\%), which have no LLM user, and on the telecom holdout (20.7\%). Cross-cell comparisons are descriptive, and we establish no $G-H$ ordering that transfers across cells. A same-precision baseline (a second GPTQ-W4 calibration draw in place of B) gives \pending{[same-precision reading pending]}. % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§2); analysis/fork/out/ext_r2_20261002T024433Z.md (§3); analysis/fork/out/holdout_r2_posthoc_20261002T024414Z.md (§3 pool); docs/insights/SAMEPREC_PROTOCOL.md
\item \textbf{Local precision upgrades redistribute success and failure} (\S\ref{sec:r1}, \S\ref{sec:r3}). Replacing a W4 agent's first tool-structure-divergent action rescues or damages 15.5--20.9\% of task$\times$block units, while the net effect is $-1.9$ to $+2.9$\pp{}. On 34 pre-registered unseen telecom tasks, the swap again rescues and damages, task fragility repeats, and the estimated effect is $-4.6$\pp{} (90\% CI $-7.2$ to $-2.0$); the frozen primary reading is inconclusive. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md; analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md
\item \textbf{Finding sensitive decisions and choosing beneficial replacements are different targets} (\S\ref{sec:r4}). A frozen Q-uncertainty trigger captures more outcome-changing decisions than random selection at two of three rates on later nonce blocks, but has not been shown to select net-beneficial replacements. A hindsight oracle with the same per-cell budget nets $+3.3$ to $+3.7$\pp{}, an upper reference that is positive by construction. % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3); analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1a)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Contributions.} (1)~A crossed-intervention framework for evaluating precision upgrades in agents: same-state restoration with bit-exact replay acceptance, full-action interventions, paired estimands ($G$, $H$, $F$ and the $(\DQ,\DB)$ table) with explicit denominators, and inference that separates a fixed task pool from new tasks. (2)~A study over weight, KV-cache and stress quantization, two model sizes and three benchmarks, covering two-sided consequences, controller dependence across environments, text actions at ordinary states (whose outcome labels did not reproduce under a second simulator; \S\ref{sec:r3}) and diagnostics for precision selection. Negative and withdrawn results are in Appendix~\ref{app:withdrawn}. % nocheck: list labels (1)-(2)
