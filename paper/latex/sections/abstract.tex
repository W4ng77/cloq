\begin{abstract}
A quantized agent reaches a decision and its BF16 reference is available. Should BF16 replace only this step, take over the rest of the episode, or both? The task benefit of such an upgrade depends on which step is upgraded and on who executes afterwards, and whole-model swap evaluations mix these effects: end-to-end success compares entire episodes, and forking a trajectory and continuing it with another model changes the action and the continuation together. We measure them separately. At a frozen state that replays bit-exactly, a $2\times2$ design crosses the source of the current action with the model that executes the rest of the episode. A second W4 checkpoint of the same model, used in place of BF16, tests whether turnover and continuation dependence require a higher bit width, and benchmarks BF16 substitution against a fixed-precision alternative. % nocheck: design label
On \tautwo{} with Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B, replacing a W4 agent's first diverging action with BF16's changes the outcome of 15.5--20.9\% of task--block units, in both directions, for a net effect of $-1.9$ to $+2.9$\pp{}, and in 26.9\% of divergent states the swap's effect depends on which model continues. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md; analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§2)
On the fixed task pool, action replacement outperforms continuation takeover for both Qwen3-8B W4 methods (by 2.9 and 3.4\pp{}; Holm-adjusted $p=.013$ and $.012$ across four comparisons). Qwen3-4B point estimates favor continuation takeover, but neither contrast is significant. All four new-task intervals include zero, limiting this exploratory finding to the fixed task pool. % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_multiplicity_20261001T225727Z.md
A higher bit width is not necessary for two-sided flips and continuation dependence: swapping only the W4 calibration checkpoint also produces them at comparable size, and whether BF16's action adds value beyond another W4 action on the same states is not resolved. % src: analysis/fork/out/sameprec_20261004T070601Z.md (Amendment A1); analysis/fork/out/commonstate_20261002T201612Z.md (Key estimand table)
A frozen uncertainty trigger finds outcome-changing decisions but captures little of the available net gain. The rescues and damages of a specific upgrade mode must therefore be measured on the same states, not inferred from how sensitive a decision is. % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3); analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1a)
\end{abstract}
