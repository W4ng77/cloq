\begin{abstract}
Precision fallback asks whether calling a higher-precision model improves an agent's task outcome. We measure three upgrades at audited, restored states: replacing the current action, taking over the continuation, or both. On Qwen3 agents under simulated quantization in \tautwo{}, replacing the first tool-structure-divergent W4 action changes 15.5--20.9\% of outcomes, but rescues and damages nearly cancel. On a pre-registered telecom holdout, the net estimate is negative; the protocol classifies it as inconclusive because its reading rules omitted a negative-effect category. A second W4 checkpoint and a semantically identical tool-argument reordering also change outcomes, so flips alone do not indicate a need for more bits. At common states, BF16's additional action value over the second checkpoint remains unresolved, and equivalence is not established. A frozen uncertainty trigger finds outcome-changing decisions without a reliable net benefit over random selection. Fallback evaluation should measure the signed task benefit of the specific upgrade executed, use reference interventions to interpret sensitivity, and validate selectors by net benefit.
