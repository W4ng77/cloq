\begin{abstract}
How much does a precision upgrade help a low-precision agent complete a task? We measure three interventions at a restored decision state: replacing the current action with BF16's, taking over the continuation, or both. Audited replay enables paired comparisons of rescues and damages. On Qwen3 agents under simulated quantization in \tautwo{}, replacing the first tool-structure-divergent W4 action changes 15.5--20.9\% of outcomes, with pooled net gain $+0.7$ points (new-task 95\% interval [$-2.1$, $+3.4$]). The pre-registered telecom holdout estimate is negative ($-4.6$ points; 90\% [$-7.2$, $-2.0$]); the protocol lacked a negative-effect category and classifies it as inconclusive. A second W4 checkpoint also produces two-sided outcome changes. At common states, BF16's extra action value over this checkpoint remains unresolved; equivalence is not established. Reordering tool-argument keys also changes outcomes despite identical tool operations. Finally, a frozen uncertainty trigger enriches outcome-changing decisions without a reliable net gain. These results distinguish sensitivity from the value of a specific upgrade: evaluations need reference interventions and selectors validated by net task benefit.
