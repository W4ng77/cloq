\begin{abstract}
When a quantized agent and its BF16 reference reach similar average task success, but the quantized agent takes a different action at some state, should BF16 take over, and if so for this one step or from here on? Our results indicate that the answer depends on the state and on the controller that continues, so average success, action divergence and the gain from precision replacement need to be measured separately. We propose a crossed same-state intervention: at a frozen, bit-exactly replayable state, we cross the source of the current action (BF16 or quantized) with the controller of the remaining episode. This yields three replacement gains on the same states: the current action only ($G$), the continuation only ($H$), or both ($F$). We study Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B under simulated weight and KV-cache quantization with BF16 compute, one greedy user simulator, and forks at the first decision where the two models' actions differ in tool structure (text-only differences are skipped). In \tautwo{} (85 tasks, 20 nonce blocks), replacing a W4 agent's action there changes the task outcome in 15.5--20.9\% of task-block units, in both directions, while the net gain is between $-1.9$ and $+2.9$\pp{}. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md
In about a quarter of the divergent states that each W4 cell samples (pooled 26.9\%; new-task interval 22.1--32.0\%), the outcome effect of the same action swap changes with the controller that continues. In this deterministic setting, this share includes sensitivity of the continuation to any change of controller; no same-precision perturbation control separates the two. Strict sign reversals are only 1.2--4.2\% of states in the W4 and KV8 cells, and, in the pooled development W4 states, dependence in the two directions is about equally frequent, so the mean interaction is near zero there (not in the post hoc telecom holdout analysis). The three replacement types therefore need to be compared pairwise on the same states; our results do not establish an ordering among them that transfers across configurations, tasks and user simulators. % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md
At outcome-independent states, most outcome-changing differences under our user simulator are text differences with the same tool structure. These outcome labels did not reproduce reliably under an alternative, stochastic relay simulator. Quantized-model uncertainty at the start of an action is associated with whether the two models' actions differ (AUC 0.66--0.91, development block); a frozen uncertainty trigger captured more outcome-changing decisions than matched random selection at two of three rates on later nonce blocks of the same tasks, but has not been shown to select net-beneficial replacements. % src: analysis/fork/out/bank_u_f01_f02_f03_20261002T003037Z.md (§3); analysis/fork/out/simctrl_20261001T212742Z.md; analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261001T185031Z.md; analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md
On 34 unseen telecom tasks, replacing the first tool-structure-divergent action of three W4 cells (weighted $\tfrac12,\tfrac14,\tfrac14$) has an estimated effect of $-4.6$\pp{} (pre-specified crossed 90\% CI $-7.2$ to $-2.0$); the frozen rule reads the primary endpoint as inconclusive (neither the positive-gain nor the small-effect reading is met), while both pre-registered secondaries (two-sided consequences, fragility repeatability) pass. % src: analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md
\end{abstract}
