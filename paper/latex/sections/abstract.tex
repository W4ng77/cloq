\begin{abstract}
Evaluations of low-precision agents usually ask how much the model changed. A deployment with a BF16 fallback needs a different quantity: what a specific upgrade is worth under the policy that executes afterwards. We present an auditable same-state protocol that separately measures replacing the current action, the subsequent controller, or both, counts the rescues and damages each causes, and compares the BF16 replacement with a same-precision substitute. On Qwen3 agents under simulated quantization in \tautwo{}, replacing a quantized agent's first divergent action changes many outcomes in both directions for a small net effect; on held-out telecom tasks under a pre-registered protocol, the net effect is $-4.6$ points (crossed 90\% interval [$-7.2$, $-2.0$]). Action-only and whole-takeover evaluations miss different rescues; in an exploratory follow-up on a configuration chosen for its large sustained-takeover value, a one-decision BF16 takeover shows no detectable net gain. A second checkpoint at the same bit width produces comparable turnover, so outcome sensitivity is not by itself evidence that more bits are needed. \end{abstract}
