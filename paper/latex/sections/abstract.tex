\begin{abstract}
A quantized agent reaches a decision and its BF16 reference is available. Should BF16 replace only this step, take over the rest of the episode, or both? End-to-end success cannot answer this. We measure the options directly: at a frozen state that replays bit-exactly, a $2\times2$ design crosses the source of the current action with the model that executes the rest of the episode, and a second W4 checkpoint of the same model serves as a same-precision reference substitution. % nocheck: design label
Our central finding is that the ability of an intervention to change task outcomes and its ability to improve them have to be measured separately. On \tautwo{} with Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B, replacing a W4 agent's first diverging action with BF16's changes the outcome of 15.5--20.9\% of task--block units, in both directions, for a net effect of $-1.9$ to $+2.9$\pp{}; in 26.9\% of divergent states the swap's effect depends on which model continues. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md; analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§2)
Swapping only the W4 calibration checkpoint, with no increase in bit width, also produces two-sided flips and continuation dependence of comparable size, yet 138 of the 245 units that flip do so under only one of the two substitutions; equivalence of the two turnovers within $\pm$3\pp{} is not shown (interim). % src: analysis/fork/out/sameprec_20261002T142834Z.md (Amendment A1); analysis/fork/out/sameprec_overlap_20261002T143218Z.md; derived: 138=64+74; 245=107+64+74
A frozen uncertainty trigger finds outcome-changing decisions more often than random selection but captures little of the net gain a hindsight oracle shows is available. % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3); analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1a)
Evaluations of precision interventions for agents should therefore report sensitivity and value separately and include a same-precision reference substitution.
\end{abstract}
