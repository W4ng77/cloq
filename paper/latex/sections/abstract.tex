\begin{abstract}
In a closed-loop agent, the value of a single precision upgrade is jointly determined by the action it changes and by the execution that follows. When a quantized agent and its BF16 reference act differently at a state, should BF16 supply this step, take over from the next step, or both? We measure this directly with a crossed same-state intervention: at a frozen, bit-exactly replayable state, we cross the source of the current action (BF16 or quantized) with the controller of the rest of the episode, a standard $2\times2$ factorial design made executable for agents. We study Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B under simulated weight and KV-cache quantization with BF16 compute, one greedy user simulator in \tautwo{}, and forks at the first decision where the two models' actions differ in tool structure. % nocheck: model sizes and design label
Three findings emerge. (i)~The value of an action depends on the controller that continues: in 26.9\% of development W4 divergent states, the same action swap has a different outcome effect under BF16 and under quantized continuation, and such dependence also appears on BFCL and ALFWorld, which have no LLM user. Without a same-precision perturbation control, this share includes the continuation's sensitivity to any change of controller. % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§2); analysis/fork/out/ext_r2_20261002T024433Z.md (§3, §6)
(ii)~Local upgrades redistribute success and failure: replacing a W4 agent's action rescues or damages 15.5--20.9\% of task-block units while the net effect stays small, and on unseen telecom tasks its estimated effect is $-4.6$\pp{} (90\% CI $-7.2$ to $-2.0$; pre-registered primary reading inconclusive). % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md; analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md
(iii)~Finding sensitive decisions and choosing beneficial replacements are different targets: a frozen uncertainty trigger captures more outcome-changing decisions than matched random selection at two of three rates, but has not been shown to yield a net gain, while a hindsight oracle with the same budget leaves headroom. % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3); analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1a)
Evaluating precision upgrades for agents therefore requires measuring the task value of the replacement under the execution that follows.
\end{abstract}
