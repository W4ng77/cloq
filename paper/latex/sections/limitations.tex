\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Inference scope.} The contrasts measure realised task effects under the fixed execution stack. Existing intervals leave meaningful BF16 advantages possible (Appendix~\ref{app:resolution}); we do not infer small effects from a failure to resolve them. Fixed-pool and new-task intervals address different targets; intervals containing zero leave an effect unresolved, while equivalence requires the designated equivalence test. $G$, $H$ and $F$ are offline intervention values rather than the gain of a deployed selection policy. The rescue-pattern and label-permutation analyses are exploratory and post hoc (Appendix~\ref{app:offdiag}).

\paragraph{Two model families, limited coverage.} Most crossed forks use Qwen3 models: 4B and 8B throughout, plus a single Qwen3-14B configuration on BFCL. % src: analysis/fork/out/ext_q14_20261002T085647Z.md
The second family, Mistral-Small-3.2-24B (GPTQ-W4, RTN-W4), was forked only at ordinary BF16-sourced \tautwo{} states of an 83-task conditional population (Appendix~\ref{app:mistral}); it has no first-divergent-state forks or same-precision reference. % src: analysis/fork/out/m24_acceptance_review_A.md; docs/insights/NEWMETHOD_PANEL_PROTOCOL.md (A3); analysis/fork/out/newmethod_m24_20261004T024453Z.md
Effects may differ at other scales and in other families. End-to-end runs also exist for Qwen3-14B and Qwen3-32B; apart from that one Qwen3-14B configuration they have no crossed forks, and we draw no conclusion from them (Appendix~\ref{app:coverage}). A time-boxed Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct check (GPTQ-W4, BFCL multi-turn) was stopped at its gate: BF16 solved 5 of 120 gate items, so the four arms carried almost no outcome variance, and it yields no evidence for that family. % src: analysis/fork/ext_l8_gate/GATE.md; derived: 120=20+100

\paragraph{Simulated quantization.} Quantized checkpoints are dequantized and served with BF16 compute on a deterministic path. Deployed low-bit kernels change the numerics and add serving variation that we deliberately remove. We make no claims about latency, memory or deployed kernels, and the cost of a BF16 fallback is outside our scope.

\paragraph{One user simulator; two small controls.} All \tautwo{} episodes use a single greedy simulator, and continuation effects include its replies. Both second-simulator controls replace only the user after a fixed history, cover 78 and 80 states, and say nothing about real users. The first-divergent-state control has 20 states per configuration, too few to confirm or refute few-pp contrasts. % src: docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W91, W97; analysis/fork/out/simctrl_p_20261002T015200Z.md
Neither control can be reproduced draw for draw: the replacement user is a hosted Sonnet 5.5 model sampled with its default settings, without temperature or seed control. In the ordinary-state control, Claude Code subagents answered many relay requests in one context, so a responder could see other arms or replicates of the same conversation; the first-divergent-state control removed this exposure by issuing one isolated, single-request call per user turn. % src: docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W95 (relay simulator stochastic; subagent, not API Sonnet); docs/insights/SIMCTRL_P_PROTOCOL.md §6 (no shared context; no temperature, no seed; comparison with W95)
The same-precision reference (\S\ref{sec:sameprec}) covers q4 and q8 GPTQ-W4 on the development panel (six blocks) and GPTQ-W3 on three blocks, and swaps one calibration sample for another, re-drawn from the same calibration trajectories; it does not cover checkpoints calibrated on independent datasets. The six-block same-precision panel is the longest complete common prefix under its frozen rule; its seventh q8 block is incomplete. This is separate from the common-state experiment and its extension. It is our only reference for how much turnover and continuation dependence a change of model without a change of bit width produces, and it says nothing about AWQ, KV8, RTN or rotated KV4.

\paragraph{Small confirmation and partial state sets.} The pre-registered holdout covers 34 telecom tasks and three configurations. Its pre-registered readings had only positive-gain and small-effect categories; omitting a negative-effect category was a design omission, and it leaves the protocol able to classify the negative estimate only as inconclusive. It applies only to telecom, these three configurations and this simulator. A post hoc replay of all 1202 divergent states reproduced every original QQ suffix (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}); it rules out a replay artifact, not other causes of the negative estimate. % src: analysis/fork/out/holdout_qqfull_audit_20261006T061033Z.md (TL;DR); docs/insights/HOLDOUT_QQFULL_PROTOCOL.md (§1-§5, Amendment 1); docs/insights/PREREG_FORK_CONFIRM.md §1
Most development results come from retail and airline, so the holdout confounds a domain shift (telecom) with generalisation to new tasks. % src: docs/insights/PREREG_FORK_CONFIRM.md §1
On 80 telecom development tasks, the two q8 holdout configurations leave the net effect unresolved (Appendix~\ref{app:holdoutdev}), but their 6-block intervals are wide and q4 has no telecom development forks. These data cannot separate a telecom domain effect from heterogeneity among the 34 holdout tasks. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T093944Z.md (Telecom development)
First-divergent-state forks intervene only at the first tool-structure divergence per task and block. Ordinary-state forks sample one state per task and block; their main analyses use three blocks, so their block intervals rest on two degrees of freedom, and the later panels use two to six blocks. Prompt nonces define replicate blocks; they are a researcher-chosen perturbation, not a deployment distribution.
