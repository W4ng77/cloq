\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} We do not claim success equivalence between BF16 and W4 agents; equivalence tests at $\pm$3\pp{} on new-task intervals fail. % src: analysis/insights/iter_4/out/success_ni_ALIGNED_20260930T213009Z.md
$G$, $H$ and $F$ are oracle quantities, not the value of a deployable policy (\S\ref{sec:r4}). We do not claim that BF16 and a second W4 checkpoint are equivalent replacements. We do not claim a W$\times$KV interaction, and we do not present static precision restoration as a method (Appendix~\ref{app:withdrawn}).
We do not claim a distinct action--continuation interaction mechanism, or that outcomes are mainly determined by the action: the outcome classes and the label-permutation reference (Appendix~\ref{app:offdiag}) are exploratory and post hoc, with headline shares from the retail and airline development panel, and the action-over-continuation gap comes only from the 13.8\% of W4 divergent states with exactly two successful arms. % src: analysis/fork/out/outcome_classes_20261004T143614Z.md

\paragraph{Two model families, limited coverage.} Most crossed forks use Qwen3 models: 4B and 8B throughout, plus a single Qwen3-14B configuration on BFCL. % src: analysis/fork/out/ext_q14_20261002T085647Z.md
The second family, Mistral-Small-3.2-24B (GPTQ-W4, RTN-W4), was forked only at ordinary BF16-sourced \tautwo{} states of an 83-task conditional population (\S\ref{sec:value}); it has no first tool-structure-divergence forks, no same-precision reference and no outcome-class analysis. % src: analysis/fork/out/m24_acceptance_review_A.md; docs/insights/NEWMETHOD_PANEL_PROTOCOL.md (A3); analysis/fork/out/newmethod_m24_20261004T024453Z.md
Effects may differ at other scales and in other families. End-to-end runs also exist for Qwen3-14B and Qwen3-32B; apart from that one Qwen3-14B configuration they have no crossed forks, and we draw no conclusion from them (Appendix~\ref{app:coverage}). A time-boxed Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct check (GPTQ-W4, BFCL multi-turn) was stopped at its gate: BF16 solved 5 of 120 gate items, so the four arms carried almost no outcome variance, and it yields no evidence for that family. % src: analysis/fork/ext_l8_gate/GATE.md; derived: 120=20+100

\paragraph{Simulated quantization.} Quantized checkpoints are dequantized and served with BF16 compute on a deterministic path. Deployed low-bit kernels change the numerics and add serving variation that we deliberately remove. We make no claims about latency, memory or deployed kernels, and the cost of a BF16 fallback is outside our scope.

\paragraph{One user simulator; two small controls.} All \tautwo{} episodes use a single greedy simulator, and continuation effects include its replies. Both second-simulator controls replace only the user after a fixed history, cover 78 and 80 states, and say nothing about real users. The Bank P control has 20 states per cell, too few to confirm or refute few-pp contrasts. % src: docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W91, W97; analysis/fork/out/simctrl_p_20261002T015200Z.md
Neither control can be reproduced draw for draw. The replacement user is a hosted Sonnet 5.5 model sampled with its default settings, without temperature or seed control. In the Bank U control, Claude Code subagents answered many relay requests in one context, so a responder could see other arms or replicates of the same conversation. The Bank P control removed this exposure by issuing one isolated, single-request call per user turn. % src: docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W95 (relay simulator stochastic; subagent, not API Sonnet); docs/insights/SIMCTRL_P_PROTOCOL.md §6 (no shared context; no temperature, no seed; comparison with W95)
The same-precision reference (\S\ref{sec:sameprec}) covers q4 and q8 GPTQ-W4 on the development panel (six blocks: the frozen rule takes the longest complete common prefix, and block f07 of the same-precision q8 design is incomplete) and GPTQ-W3 on three blocks, and swaps one calibration sample for another; it is our only reference for how much turnover and continuation dependence a change of model without a change of bit width produces, and it says nothing about AWQ, KV8, RTN or rotated KV4.

\paragraph{LLM annotation.} Process labels in Appendix~\ref{app:injection} come from LLM annotators (Sonnet 5.5, two independent instances plus an adjudicator) and rule-based checks. No human annotation has been done. Agreement among instances of one model family shows reproducibility, not correctness. % src: analysis/annotation/wp5/table4_v2.md

\paragraph{Small confirmation and partial banks.} The pre-registered holdout covers 34 telecom tasks and three cells. Its pre-registered readings had no negative category, so a clearly negative estimate could only be read as inconclusive; its negative point estimate, inconclusive by those readings, applies only to telecom, these three cells and this simulator (its replay acceptance was audited post hoc and is complete; Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}). Most development results come from retail and airline, so the holdout confounds a domain shift (telecom) with generalisation to new tasks. % src: docs/insights/PREREG_FORK_CONFIRM.md §1
On 80 telecom development tasks, the two q8 holdout cells show no detectable net effect (Appendix~\ref{app:holdoutdev}), so the telecom domain alone does not produce a detectable drop; but their 6-block intervals are wide and q4 has no telecom development forks, so the holdout drop may reflect heterogeneity among its 34 tasks as much as a domain effect. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T093944Z.md (Telecom development)
Bank P intervenes only at the first tool-structure divergence per task and block. Bank U samples one state per task and block; its main analyses use three blocks, so their block intervals rest on two degrees of freedom, and the later panels use two to six blocks. Prompt nonces define replicate blocks; they are a researcher-chosen perturbation, not a deployment distribution.
