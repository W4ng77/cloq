\section{Setup Details}
\label{app:setup}

\paragraph{Splits.} The development split has 58 retail, 27 airline and 80 telecom tasks. The holdout has 34 telecom tasks, and its pre-registration hash is recorded in the split file. % src: manifests/fork/splits_v1.json
The telecom split was fixed from \tautwo{}'s 114-task base split by a stratified, salted hash before any run. % src: manifests/telecom/dev_v1.json
The external benchmarks use BFCL multi-turn (base, miss\_func, miss\_param and long\_context, 197 items each) and ALFWorld (134 out-of-distribution games). Items were selected by hash in manifests committed before any model run. % src: docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W36-ext (manifests/bfcl/bfcl_mt_v1.json, manifests/alfworld/alfworld_text_v1.json)

\paragraph{Success endpoints.} For retail and airline we use the final-database match with normal termination. This is not \tautwo{}'s official reward, which additionally scores natural-language or communicate checks. Telecom uses the official deterministic task-basis success. Context overflow and unparseable output count as failures. Infrastructure outages are missing, never failures, and a block with a missing unit leaves the primary analysis.

\paragraph{Fork mechanics.} The forced action is returned by a fork agent for the one step that follows $h_t$. A tool call is executed by the real environment, and text is answered by the real simulator, which never reuses observations from the original trajectory. tau2 resets step and error counters when it starts from a history. The fork restores them to the values used at $t$, so the remaining budget equals the original. Every max\_steps and too\_many\_errors episode on the checked blocks is reproduced by the recount. % src: docs/insights/FORK_PROTOCOL.md §2-§4

The per-task dispersion of $M$ is 4.0--8.7 times its binomial null in the W4 and KV8 cells, and the top 20\% of tasks hold 57--71\% of all flips. % src: analysis/fork/out/consequence_v2_20261001T070850Z.json (concentration.dispersion_index, share_of_consequences_in_top20pct_tasks)
We split the 20 blocks into two disjoint halves of 10 nonces. Task-level $M$ correlates across halves (Spearman 0.52--0.86). Tasks that were net-rescue in one half have a mean net gain in the other half that is 14--51\pp{} higher than that of net-damage tasks, in all 12 cell-directions. % src: analysis/fork/out/w67_audit_v2_20261001T0600Z_20261001T070500Z.md (Concentration and Direction, panel85)
A Freedman--Lane test conditions on measured baselines: QQ success propensity and BF16 success, plus trajectory length in a post-hoc variant. With them, one half's task rate still predicts the other's in all 6 configurations $\times$ 2 directions for both $M$ and $G$ (Holm-adjusted). This is a \emph{retrospective conditional association} on viewed development data. It is carried by retail. Airline alone gives 8 of 12 for $M$ (5 after Holm), and 3 of 12 under a heteroscedasticity-robust wild bootstrap. % src: analysis/fork/out/w67_audit_v2_20261001T0600Z_20261001T070500Z.md (panel85, airline27 counts)
Direction persistence beyond flip opportunity holds more often for rescues than for damages, in 11 versus 6 of the 12 cell-directions. Different quantizers flip the same tasks: the task-identity null is rejected in 7 of 8 quantizer pairs. When both flip, however, they agree in direction only 42--81\% of the time. % src: analysis/fork/out/w67_audit_v2_20261001T0600Z_20261001T070500Z.md (Cross-quantizer co-susceptibility)
This overlaps with the stable failure set of \citet{jang2026flat}. What is new here is the signed, two-sided view: shared fragility, but with near-random relative direction across quantizers.

\section{Controlled Injection and Process Audit}
\label{app:injection}

A natural explanation of R1 is a local-defect story: the quantized agent makes a local error, and the task fails unless the continuation recovers. Two analyses constrain this explanation. They limit what it can explain; they do not identify a mechanism.

\paragraph{Controlled defect injection.} We froze 30 ordinary states from q4 GPTQ-W4 trajectories, all from one development block. At each state we forced one of three actions as the next step: an \textsc{inv}alid action, a matched \textsc{val}id action, or a process-perturbation control (\textsc{pp}) that repeats the last successful read. Either B or Q then continued. The defect categories were wrong\_id, wrong\_tool and premature\_write, with 10 states each. % src: analysis/recovery/inject_q4/summary_v2.md; docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W71
Every state passed a replay audit of 22 recorded fields (VAL$\times$Q against the original episode). \paragraph{Result: two-sided turnover, no error-specific effect.} Injected defects change the outcome in 7/30 states under B (4 rescues, 3 damages) and 4/30 under Q (2 and 2). The process-perturbation control changes 5/30 under both controllers (Table~\ref{tab:recovery}). The error-specific effect, \textsc{inv} minus \textsc{pp}, is $0.00$ [$-0.13$, $+0.13$] under B and $-0.03$ [$-0.20$, $+0.10$] under Q. The controller dependence $D$ is nonzero in 8 of 30 states, but its mean is $+0.03$ [$-0.17$, $+0.27$]. % src: analysis/recovery/inject_q4/summary_v2.md
So injected defects cause heterogeneous two-sided turnover of the size a generic perturbation causes. We detect no error-specific effect beyond that perturbation and no mean controller difference, at $n=30$ states. An earlier reading of the same data, that injected defects ``seldom change outcomes'', was wrong and has been retracted (Appendix~\ref{app:withdrawn}).

These annotations are a \emph{process description} conditional on outcome-defined groups. They are not causal evidence, not prevalences and not human labels. Three patterns stand out (Table~\ref{tab:table4}). First, local defects and final failure do not correspond one-to-one. In damage states, where Q's action led to success and B's to failure, Q's first-action defect is corrected later in 10 of 20 QQ branches. Second, whether a write executes does not separate the failing from the succeeding continuation of the same state (rescue 17 vs.\ 17; damage 15 vs.\ 14). Third, the failing continuation more often carries the rubric label \texttt{unintended\_or\_unconsented\_change} (rescue: QQ 9 vs.\ BQ 2, paired difference $+0.35$ [$+0.08$, $+0.57$]; damage: 1 vs.\ 9, $-0.40$ [$-0.62$, $-0.11$]). In damage states, 7 of these 20 BQ branches carry the label on writes \emph{with} recorded consent. That is the semantic ``wrong change'' part of the label, and it overlaps with the database-defined outcome by construction. Loops and budget exhaustion also mark failing branches (rescue: QQ 5 vs.\ BQ 0). % src: analysis/annotation/wp5/table4_v2.md
We make no claim about consent. The programmatic consent field agrees poorly with the annotators, so we use it only as a mechanical row. Re-labelling 30 states with fresh instances of the same model family reproduces the key pattern. That shows reproducibility, not correctness. % src: docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W82 (analysis/annotation/wp5/human_check_v1/proxy_compare_v1.json)
