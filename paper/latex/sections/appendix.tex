\section{Setup Details}
\label{app:setup}

\paragraph{Splits.} The development split has 58 retail, 27 airline and 80 telecom tasks. The holdout has 34 telecom tasks, and its pre-registration hash is recorded in the split file. % src: manifests/fork/splits_v1.json
The telecom split was fixed from \tautwo{}'s 114-task base split by a stratified, salted hash before any run. % src: manifests/telecom/dev_v1.json
The external benchmarks use BFCL multi-turn (base, miss\_func, miss\_param and long\_context, 197 items each) and ALFWorld (134 out-of-distribution games). Items were selected by hash in manifests committed before any model run. % src: docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W36-ext (manifests/bfcl/bfcl_mt_v1.json, manifests/alfworld/alfworld_text_v1.json)

\paragraph{Success endpoints.} For retail and airline we use the final-database match with normal termination. This is not \tautwo{}'s official reward, which additionally scores natural-language or communicate checks. Telecom uses the official deterministic task-basis success. Context overflow and unparseable output count as failures. Infrastructure outages are missing, never failures, and a block with a missing unit leaves the primary analysis.

\paragraph{Fork mechanics.} The forced action is returned by a fork agent for the one step that follows $h_t$. A tool call is executed by the real environment, and text is answered by the real simulator, which never reuses observations from the original trajectory. \tautwo{} resets step and error counters when it starts from a history. The fork restores them to the values used at $t$, so the remaining budget equals the original. Every max\_steps and too\_many\_errors episode on the checked blocks is reproduced by the recount. % src: docs/insights/FORK_PROTOCOL.md §2-§4
\paragraph{Replay acceptance details.} In the engineering gate, one B$\rightarrow$B field, the official reward, was missing from older trajectories and was recomputed from the stored reward information before the verdict. Besides the 924 sampled development states, 328/328 sampled Bank U states passed the QQ no-op, and the Bank U gate passed a further 135/135 no-ops. Serving in separate processes closes the prefix-cache channel \citep{patodiya2026same}. % src: docs/insights/FORK_PROTOCOL.md §8 (analysis/fork/gate/); analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md; analysis/fork/bank_u/*/f0*/bank_summary.json (noop); analysis/fork/bank_u/gate/*/f01/bank_summary.json; derived: 328=18+13+15+31+14+20+16+24+19+16+17+19+25+17+16+15+18+15
The same-precision run's acceptance counts are in Appendix~\ref{app:sameprec}.

\paragraph{User simulator.} The served user simulator is Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct in every episode; a user-model label stored in the fork state files is outdated and should be ignored. % src: /data/ziyangs/cloq_work/sims/*.json (hf_id); docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md (stale user_model_id field)

\subsection{Marginal-preserving pairing agreement control}
\label{app:agreement}

For each cell, observed agreement is the diagonal share of the $(\DQ,\DB)$ table, $p_o=\sum_k P(\DQ=k,\DB=k)$, so the off-diagonal share is $1-p_o=P(I\neq0)$. The control re-pairs states independently within a cell while keeping both marginals, $p_e=\sum_k p_Q(k)\,p_B(k)$, and Cohen's $\kappa=(p_o-p_e)/(1-p_e)$. Pools use the paper's equal weight per cell over common complete blocks, with the same bootstrap draws as Table~\ref{tab:ghf}. This is a pairing agreement control, not a no-interaction null and not a chance baseline for controller dependence (\S\ref{sec:r2}). % src: analysis/fork/out/agreement_control_20261002T024240Z.md (Definitions; Aggregation)
In the W4 pool the control expects 38.0\% [36.7, 39.0] off-diagonal against the observed 26.9\%, so $\kappa=0.29$ [0.27, 0.32] (new-task [0.22, 0.36]); in the KV8 pool it expects 33.6\% ($\kappa=0.55$). The effects under the two controllers agree more than random cross-state pairing would. % src: analysis/fork/out/agreement_control_20261002T024240Z.md (§2 equal-weight pools W4:all, KV8:all)
On BFCL and ALFWorld the observed shares also lie below the control (expected 16.2--32.4\% and 42.9--46.4\% in the W4 cells; $\kappa$ 0.47--0.71 and 0.14--0.24; Table~\ref{tab:extr2}), and on the telecom holdout the pooled 20.7\% compares with 25.7\% expected ($\kappa=0.19$; Table~\ref{tab:holdoutr2}). % src: analysis/fork/out/ext_r2_20261002T024433Z.md (§3, §6); analysis/fork/out/holdout_r2_posthoc_20261002T024414Z.md (§3 pool)

\subsection{Mean squared interaction under the second simulator (exploratory)}
\label{app:simctrlp}

On the 80 states of the Bank P simulator control (Appendix~\ref{app:simctrlpmain}), each arm has $n_a=5$ replicates under Sonnet 5.5. Per state, $\hat I_s=\bar Y_{\mathrm{BQ}}+\bar Y_{\mathrm{QB}}-\bar Y_{\mathrm{BB}}-\bar Y_{\mathrm{QQ}}$ and $U_s=\hat I_s^2-\sum_a \bar Y_a(1-\bar Y_a)/(n_a-1)$, which is unbiased for $I_s^2$ if, given the state, the replicates of each arm are i.i.d.\ Bernoulli and the four arms are independent (no shared cache or seed). Pooled with equal weight over the four W4 cells, mean $\hat I_s^2$ is 0.110, the noise correction 0.060 and mean $U_s$ 0.050, with 95\% intervals [0.004, 0.107] over states and [0.001, 0.111] over task clusters. \section{Trigger Headroom and a KL Reference Row}
\label{app:oracle}

Table~\ref{tab:oracle} compares the frozen trigger of \S\ref{sec:r4} (frozen rule, unchanged) with two references on the same 1020 f02/f03 U1 rows, which hold 38 rescues and 31 damages. The \emph{matched action-replacement oracle} selects, per cell, exactly as many rows as the frozen rule did, taking only rows with $G>0$ and filling with $G=0$ rows when a cell has too few rescues; it never selects a damage. It is hindsight selection, not a policy. At the 10\% and 20\% budgets every rescue fits. % src: analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1a)
The \emph{three-option menu oracle} $E[\max(G,H,F,0)]$ is the share of states where some arm beats QQ. On Bank P divergent states it is 0.202 over the four W4 cells (state-weighted, the one exception to the paper's equal-weight pools) against 0.117 for the action alone (1127 vs.\ 654 of 5576 states), and 0.191 vs.\ 0.113 over the six core cells. It is hindsight selection that also allows different replacement types, with different costs, so it is not a same-cost deployment comparison and not comparable with the action rule. % src: analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1b)

The \emph{KL reference row} is a post-hoc auxiliary baseline. Its score is a truncated KL$(\mathrm{B}\|\mathrm{Q})$ over the union of both models' top-20 next-token sets plus a tail bucket, per $a_{\mathrm{Q}}$ token, teacher-forced on identical prompt ids. It needs a BF16 prefill of the prompt and $a_{\mathrm{Q}}$ at every decision (here 6709.3 prompt and 82.3 action tokens on average), so it is not Q-only. It captures turnover above chance (33 vs.\ 16.7 at 20\%) but no significant net gain (Holm $p=1.0$). Three aggregations over the $a_{\mathrm{Q}}$ tokens were fit on f01 only, with per-cell thresholds and the frozen rule's selection criterion; the maximum was chosen, and all of f02/f03 is reported. The two candidates not chosen on f01 look better on f02/f03 (e.g.\ the mean at 20\%: net $+9$, Holm $p=.0852$), but selecting them now would be selection on validation data. Because the f02/f03 outcomes had been seen, the row is post hoc, and it is not a DMR reproduction, which trains a router. % src: analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§2)

Each derived checkpoint takes every decoder linear from the BF16 model, from Q, or from an interpolation between them; all other tensors come from the BF16 model, and the endpoints reproduce BF16 and Q bit for bit. % src: docs/insights/PERTURBATION_PROTOCOL.md (TL;DR; §1)
Only finished end-to-end runs enter: q4 and q8, 85 tasks on each of three nonce blocks per cell (q3--q5 for W3, the development blocks f01--f03 for W4), with outage episodes excluded and context overflow counted as failure. Intervals are fixed-pool $t$ intervals over the three blocks, with crossed task$\times$block intervals alongside; there is no correction for multiplicity. % src: analysis/fork/out/backfill_20261003T193042Z.md (header; §1, §2)

\emph{Restoration location versus random modules.} Restoring the BF16 weights of one layer third, of attention only or of the MLP only is compared with restoring randomly chosen modules with the same number of BF16 parameter bytes (mean over the finished random draws, up to three), on the same task$\times$block units (Table~\ref{tab:restoreloc}). % src: docs/insights/PERTURBATION_PROTOCOL.md §2.1
In RTN-W3, where Q succeeds in 12.9\% (q4) and 13.3\% (q8) of episodes against 35.7\% and 30.6\% for BF16, restoring attention beat byte-matched random modules by $+9.8$ [$+6.1$, $+13.6$]\pp{} (q4 and q8 pooled; crossed [$+4.5$, $+15.1$]), and restoring the last third did worse than random, $-8.6$ [$-13.4$, $-3.8$] (crossed [$-12.9$, $-4.7$]); the middle third ($+4.5$, crossed [$-0.1$, $+9.4$]) and the MLP ($-5.4$ [$-14.1$, $+3.3$]) are not resolved. % src: analysis/fork/out/backfill_20261003T193042Z.md (TL;DR 1; §1 pooled table; §2, a = 0 and a = 1 rows)
In GPTQ-W3, where Q and BF16 succeed about equally often, and in q4 GPTQ-W4 and q8 AWQ-W4, none of the 30 contrasts excludes zero under both intervals; of all 39, the three that do are RTN-W3 contrasts. % src: analysis/fork/out/backfill_20261003T193042Z.md (TL;DR 1)
At the first divergent state itself (one decision, one random draw), restoring attention returned Q's action to B's more often than random modules did (q4 RTN-W3 $+20.7$ [$+6.3$, $+35.0$]\pp{}; GPTQ-W3 pooled $+35.7$ [$+27.3$, $+44.1$]), and restoring the last third did so less often (q4 RTN-W3 $-44.6$); in GPTQ-W3 this action effect has no counterpart in success ($+1.6$ [$-4.8$, $+7.9$]). % src: analysis/fork/out/backfill_20261003T193042Z.md (TL;DR 1; §1 pivot table and pooled table)

\emph{Error dose.} The residual intervention $W_\alpha=W+\alpha(W_{\mathrm{Q}}-W)$ scales the quantizer's own weight error, with $\alpha\in\{0, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2\}$; it is not a bit width (Table~\ref{tab:dose}). % src: docs/insights/PERTURBATION_PROTOCOL.md §2.2
Agreement with BF16's actions fell at every step of $\alpha$ in all six cells, with both intervals of every step below zero; in q8 RTN-W3, the share of BF16's decisions before the first differing action is 1, 0.32, 0.16 and 0.04 at $\alpha=0$, 0.25, 0.5 and 1. Success did not follow. No success step up to $\alpha=0.5$ is resolved under both intervals (in RTN-W3 the point estimates at $\alpha=0.25$ lie above BF16, 40.0 and 36.5\% against 35.7 and 30.6\%), and success fell between $\alpha=0.5$ and 1 only in RTN-W3, by $-20.8$ [$-22.5$, $-19.1$]\pp{} in q4 (crossed [$-29.8$, $-11.8$]) and $-14.1$ [$-26.9$, $-1.4$] in q8 (crossed [$-24.7$, $-3.9$]). % src: analysis/fork/out/backfill_20261003T193042Z.md (TL;DR 2; §2)
In GPTQ-W3, q4 GPTQ-W4 and q8 AWQ-W4 no success step is resolved under both intervals; doubling the q8 AWQ-W4 error ($\alpha=2$) changed success by $-10.6$ [$-16.4$, $-4.7$]\pp{} (crossed [$-20.8$, $0.0$]). The $\alpha=2$ arms of the four W3 cells did not finish. The action response to the error dose is thus monotone, whereas success shows only a drop between half and full RTN-W3 error. % src: analysis/fork/out/backfill_20261003T193042Z.md (TL;DR 2; §2 step tables)

\emph{Low-bit failure modes.} Table~\ref{tab:failmodes} assigns every finished, non-outage retail and airline episode to one ending (descriptive). BF16 failures nearly all end normally in a wrong end state (57.6--63.9\% of episodes; at most 6.3\% end otherwise), and GPTQ-W3 keeps this profile (1.8--9.0\% abnormal endings). RTN-W3 and q4 AWQ-W3 move failures to episodes that never end normally or never issue a parsed tool call. % src: analysis/fork/out/backfill_20261003T193042Z.md (TL;DR 3; §3)
q4 RTN-W3 runs away (context overflow or the 200-step cap) in 32.9\% of episodes; in 44.7\% it ends normally after writing its tool calls as plain JSON, which the frozen tool parser does not accept. No q4 RTN-W3 episode issues a parsed write call, and none of its 33 successes is on a task that requires a write. q8 RTN-W3 runs away in 20.4\% of episodes and hits the cap of 10 failed tool calls in 16.9\%. In the W2 panel (24 tasks, one block, loop guard on), 66.7--87.5\% of episodes run away. No episode ended on an unparseable output. KV-INT3 (q4, q8, q14), Gaussian weight noise on q14, q32 RTN-W3 and the q4 RTN-W2 panel had not finished. % src: analysis/fork/out/backfill_20261003T193042Z.md (TL;DR 3; §3 table and unfinished runs)
