\section{Setup Details}
\label{app:setup}

\paragraph{Splits.} The development split has 58 retail, 27 airline and 80 telecom tasks. The holdout has 34 telecom tasks, and its pre-registration hash is recorded in the split file. % src: manifests/fork/splits_v1.json
The telecom split was fixed from \tautwo{}'s 114-task base split by a stratified, salted hash before any run. % src: manifests/telecom/dev_v1.json
The external benchmarks use BFCL multi-turn (base, miss\_func, miss\_param and long\_context, 197 items each) and ALFWorld (134 out-of-distribution games). Items were selected by hash in manifests committed before any model run. % src: docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W36-ext (manifests/bfcl/bfcl_mt_v1.json, manifests/alfworld/alfworld_text_v1.json)

\paragraph{Success endpoints.} For retail and airline we use the final-database match with normal termination. This is not \tautwo{}'s official reward, which additionally scores natural-language or communicate checks. Telecom uses the official deterministic task-basis success. Context overflow and unparseable output count as failures. Infrastructure outages are missing, never failures, and a block with a missing unit leaves the primary analysis.

\paragraph{Fork mechanics.} The forced action is returned by a fork agent for the one step that follows $h_t$. A tool call is executed by the real environment, and text is answered by the real simulator, which never reuses observations from the original trajectory. tau2 resets step and error counters when it starts from a history. The fork restores them to the values used at $t$, so the remaining budget equals the original. Every max\_steps and too\_many\_errors episode on the checked blocks is reproduced by the recount. % src: docs/insights/FORK_PROTOCOL.md §2-§4
Fork state files record a stale default \texttt{user\_model\_id}; the served simulator is identified from the replica registry. % src: /data/ziyangs/cloq_work/sims/*.json (hf_id); docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md (stale user_model_id field)

\subsection{Marginal-preserving pairing agreement control}
\label{app:agreement}

For each cell, observed agreement is the diagonal share of the $(\DQ,\DB)$ table, $p_o=\sum_k P(\DQ=k,\DB=k)$, so the off-diagonal share is $1-p_o=P(I\neq0)$. The control re-pairs states independently within a cell while keeping both marginals, $p_e=\sum_k p_Q(k)\,p_B(k)$, and Cohen's $\kappa=(p_o-p_e)/(1-p_e)$. Pools use the paper's equal weight per cell over common complete blocks, with the same bootstrap draws as Table~\ref{tab:ghf}. This is a pairing agreement control, not a no-interaction null and not a chance baseline for controller dependence (\S\ref{sec:r2}). % src: analysis/fork/out/agreement_control_20261002T024240Z.md (Definitions; Aggregation)

\subsection{Mean squared interaction under the second simulator (exploratory)}
\label{app:simctrlp}

On the 80 SIMCTRL-P states (\S\ref{sec:r3}), each arm has $n_a=5$ replicates under Sonnet 5.5. Per state, $\hat I_s=\bar Y_{\mathrm{BQ}}+\bar Y_{\mathrm{QB}}-\bar Y_{\mathrm{BB}}-\bar Y_{\mathrm{QQ}}$ and $U_s=\hat I_s^2-\sum_a \bar Y_a(1-\bar Y_a)/(n_a-1)$, which is unbiased for $I_s^2$ if, given the state, the replicates of each arm are i.i.d.\ Bernoulli and the four arms are independent (no shared cache or seed). Pooled with equal weight over the four W4 cells, mean $\hat I_s^2$ is 0.110, the noise correction 0.060 and mean $U_s$ 0.050, with 95\% intervals [0.004, 0.107] over states and [0.001, 0.111] over task clusters. One cell is negative (q4 AWQ-W4, $-0.025$) and is kept, as are negative per-state values. % src: analysis/fork/out/simctrl_p_interaction_20261002T024357Z.md
The estimate is the mean squared probability-scale interaction over these 80 frozen states. It is not the share of states with interaction and not a variance, it is not comparable with the deterministic 26.9\% of \S\ref{sec:r2}, and we make no significance claim. Source: \nolinkurl{analysis/fork/out/simctrl_p_interaction_20261002T024357Z.md}. % src: analysis/fork/out/simctrl_p_interaction_20261002T024357Z.md

The \emph{matched action-replacement oracle} selects, per cell, exactly as many rows as the frozen rule did, taking only rows with $G>0$ and filling with $G=0$ rows when a cell has too few rescues; it never selects a damage. It is hindsight selection, not a policy. At the 10\% and 20\% budgets every rescue fits. % src: analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1a)
The \emph{three-option menu oracle} $E[\max(G,H,F,0)]$ is the share of states where some arm beats QQ. On Bank P divergent states it is 0.202 over the four W4 cells (state-weighted) against 0.117 for the action alone (1127 vs.\ 654 of 5576 states), and 0.191 vs.\ 0.113 over the six core cells. It is hindsight selection that also allows different replacement types, with different costs, so it is not a same-cost deployment comparison and not comparable with the action rule. % src: analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1b)

The \emph{KL reference row} is a post-hoc auxiliary baseline. Its score is a truncated KL$(\mathrm{B}\|\mathrm{Q})$ over the union of both models' top-20 next-token sets plus a tail bucket, per $a_{\mathrm{Q}}$ token, teacher-forced on identical prompt ids. It needs a BF16 prefill of the prompt and $a_{\mathrm{Q}}$ at every decision (here 6709.3 prompt and 82.3 action tokens on average), so it is not Q-only. It captures turnover above chance (33 vs.\ 16.7 at 20\%) but no significant net gain (Holm $p=1.0$). Three aggregations were fit on f01 only, with per-cell thresholds and the frozen rule's selection criterion; KLu\_max was chosen, and all of f02/f03 is reported. The two candidates not chosen on f01 look better on f02/f03 (e.g.\ KLu\_mean at 20\%: net $+9$, Holm $p=.0852$), but selecting them now would be selection on validation data. Because the f02/f03 outcomes had been seen, the row is post hoc, and it is not a DMR reproduction, which trains a router. % src: analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§2)

\section{Withdrawn and Negative Results}
\label{app:withdrawn}

\paragraph{W$\times$KV interaction (not supported).} An earlier q4 analysis reported a W4$\times$KV4 interaction of $-14.9$ [$-23.8$, $-6.0$]\pp{} ($k=3$). Its KV16 reference lacked the Q/K rotation used for KV4, so rotation and quantization were confounded. % src: analysis/ahook/out/wakv_20261001T054021Z.md (legacy kv16 row); docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W65
With a matched rotated reference, the q4 interaction is $-9.0$ [$-30.6$, $+12.5$]. % src: docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W74 (analysis/ahook/out/wakv_20261001T054021Z.md)
The pre-registered q8 replication gives $+2.1$ [$-5.8$, $+10.1$] over 6 blocks, crossed [$-8.2$, $+12.4$]. The replication is not supported, and we make no W$\times$KV claim. % src: analysis/ahook/out/wakv_20261001T094405Z.md

\paragraph{Static precision restoration (appendix only).} We tested whether restoring selected modules to higher precision recovers a damaged RTN-W3 model. We report this line as a mechanism stress test, not a method. In RTN-W3 the gains largely tracked the number of restored bits. A random MLP arm also recovered much of the gap, and no arm beat whole-model GPTQ-W4 at matched storage. % src: docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W67, W64
The line was then paused and deferred. Its q8 RTN-W3 comparison of restored attention against byte-matched random attention was not completed: the attention arm and one random-attention seed ran, the remaining random-attention seeds are deferred, and no restoration analysis of these runs was checked, so we report no result from it. % src: docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W67, W74 (restoration line paused); /data/ziyangs/cloq_work/queue/done/pert_q8_rtn_w3_restore_*attn*_q345.json; /data/ziyangs/cloq_work/queue/deferred/pert_q8_rtn_w3_restore_rand_attn_s*_q345.json

\paragraph{Success equivalence.} Not established (Table~\ref{tab:e2e}).

\paragraph{``Errors are absorbed''.} An interim reading of the injection study said that injected defects seldom change outcomes. The per-state analysis shows two-sided turnover (Appendix~\ref{app:injection}), so this reading is retracted.
