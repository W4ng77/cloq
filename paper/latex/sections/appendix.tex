\section{Statistical Details}
\label{app:stats}

\paragraph{$\Delta$ against the null.} For each task, the null mean $\bar Y_t^{\text{null}}$ is the mean outcome over null members (leave-one-out when the tested cell is itself a null member), computed on tasks with at least three null draws. $\Delta$ is the task average of $Y_t(C) - \bar Y_t^{\text{null}}$, pooled over retail and airline, and its CI is from a task-cluster bootstrap that resamples tasks and keeps all runs of a task together. Pooling across cells of one family averages over draws before the task difference is taken. A per-run rule used in an earlier version of our protocol (``inside the range of five nulls'') had a high per-run false-flag rate and was replaced by this statistic.

\paragraph{Wins and losses against the null majority.} An episode (task $\times$ draw) of a family is a win if it passes a task whose null mean is below 0.5 and a loss if it fails a task whose null mean is above 0.5; tasks with a null mean of exactly 0.5 count as neither.

\paragraph{Minimum detectable effect.} The pooled five-model free-zone test was pre-registered with an MDE of about 3.4\pp{}.

\paragraph{Bernoulli prediction for the null SD.} The predicted run-level SD is $\sqrt{\overline{p_t(1-p_t)}/n}$ with $p_t$ estimated from the null draws of each task; the observed SD uses near-complete null draws only (at least 90\% of the tasks with a null mean). The observed-to-predicted ratio was pre-registered to lie in $[0.5, 2]$. With 4--6 draws this check has little power. \pending{P4a pooled over five models $\times$ five nonce nulls (20 df) --- \nolinkurl{a6k2_<model>_null_n1..n5}; \nolinkurl{verify/v04_pooled_draws.py}}

\paragraph{Kaplan--Meier and hazard.} First divergence is the first decision whose raw tokens differ from the reference on an identical context (wording and format changes count; a behaviour-only divergence curve is a secondary measure). Tasks that end before diverging are censored. The per-decision hazard is the number of first divergences divided by the number of decisions at risk, with a task-bootstrap CI.

\paragraph{Calibration test of \S\ref{sec:intro}.} The gap was tested with an exchangeable-null permutation (each task's pair of outcomes swapped with probability 0.5, 100,000 draws), which equals the exact one-sided McNemar binomial test on 8 versus 1 discordant tasks ($p = 0.01953$); the two-sided McNemar $p$ is 0.0389.

\section{The 4B Deep Dive}
\label{app:deepdive}

These results come from an earlier study of Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 on two RTX 4080 GPUs: 18 GPTQ W4 (g128) checkpoints from six calibration strategies $\times$ three calibration draws, plus RTN and BF16, on the same 85 tasks, with an 8k harness and the same 4B model as the simulator. We report it separately because its harness and simulator differ from the main setup. The numbers were recomputed from analysis outputs of that study; the raw runs are not available to us for re-checking.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Calibration strategies are indistinguishable in closed loop.} The 18 checkpoints range from 0.271 to 0.424 in pooled database match against BF16 at 0.376 (long tasks: 0.054--0.270 against 0.297). An arm-label permutation test on the 18 checkpoint scores gives $p = 0.683$ (pooled) and 0.368 (long tasks); a draw-label permutation gives 0.881 and 0.744. Every arm-versus-arm bootstrap interval crosses zero. The calibration draw alone moves one arm's long-task success from 0.054 to 0.243. RTN scores 0.271 pooled, equal to the lowest GPTQ checkpoint.
\item \emph{Open-loop NLL is unrelated to closed-loop success.} Across the 18 checkpoints, the Spearman correlation between teacher-forced NLL per token and pooled success is $-0.124$ (mean over decisions) or $+0.054$ (token-weighted); both give $|\rho| \le 0.13$, and they disagree in sign, so we report both. The arm with the best NLL (0.0769) and near-best full tool-call agreement (0.840) has the worst long-task success (0.117).
\item \emph{Flips are state-determined.} The reference model's NLL on a state predicts open-loop flips of compressed checkpoints there with AUC 0.93, and flip indicators correlate 0.548 within an arm and 0.538 across arms. In 0.844 of diverging (checkpoint, task) pairs, the first divergence falls on the least-confident step so far, against 0.292 by chance.
\item \emph{Failures are write-dominated and loops are mostly agent-side.} The arm $\times$ failure-category test gives $p = 0.94$. Of 150 premature terminations, 36.7\% are agent-side loops, 35.3\% show no repetition, 20.0\% are mixed and 8.0\% simulator-side; excluding simulator-side loops changes any checkpoint's score by at most 0.009.
\item \emph{Write flips are recovered less often.} Among BF16-solved tasks, the compressed checkpoint still solved the task in 0.21--0.35 of cases when its action at the first tool-turn divergence was a write, against 0.40--0.80 when it was a read or lookup (recomputed from analysis files; raw-run check pending).
\item \emph{Re-rolls dominate the W4 effect.} The net W4 loss is 0.044 [$-0.038$, 0.126] against gross per-task churn of 0.261; checkpoint identity explains a variance share of 0.008 against a residual of 0.490; and the gain rate (fail under BF16, pass under W4) is 166/954 = 0.174, so W4 pass sets are not nested in BF16's.
\end{itemize}

Taken together with the main results, these describe a regime in which W4 compression of a 4B agent is not distinguishable from re-rolling, and in which comparisons of calibration strategies against one reference run mostly measure re-rolls.

\paragraph{Harness length.} Table~\ref{tab:harness} compares the 8k truncation harness of the earlier study with the 32k harness on the same 85 tasks (Qwen3-4B, BF16 and the first calibration draw of each arm). Removing truncation raises every model by 0.01--0.04 and leaves the ranking nearly unchanged. One W4 checkpoint (MatchedAlt c1) scores above BF16 under both harnesses, which is itself an instance of re-roll noise.

\paragraph{P1 in legacy cells and on q8.} Legacy q4 cells show the same two regimes as Table~\ref{tab:hazard}: numerics variant 0.18 [0.14, 0.24] per decision; $k{=}0.03$: 0.19--0.20; $k{=}0.3$: 0.39--0.42; $k{=}1$: 0.78--0.98. Legacy q8 has a lower floor (0.12--0.14 per decision at $k{=}0.03$; 0.11 on the two clean $k{=}0.03$ seeds) with nonce 0.21, $k{=}0.3$ 0.29--0.34, GPTQ-W4 0.41--0.46 and AWQ-W4 0.51--0.56. On clean cells at $k{=}0.3$ the text-to-tool flip ratio is 5.8 / 5.7 (q4) and 6.4 / 11.3 (q8).

\paragraph{Process channels.} On legacy q4 retail at $k{=}1$, episodes grow from 12.0--12.3 decisions (nulls) to 15.1 / 16.9, premature endings rise from 0 to 0.034 / 0.069, and repeated tool calls reach 0.236 against 0.04--0.07, while RTN-W4 and every $k \le 0.3$ cell stay at null level (11.1--12.9 decisions, no premature endings). \paragraph{The determinism incident in detail.} The compiled kernels of the legacy q4 determinism pair were identical across its two nodes, and on a third node cold, warm, concurrent and prefix-cache-free starts all reproduced the second node's output (30/30); eager execution produced a third variant. \section{Further Recommendations}
\label{app:recs}

Continuing the checklist of \S\ref{sec:recs}; (\textdagger) marks an item resting on tentative findings.
(6)~\textbf{Do not substitute open-loop fidelity for closed-loop success}: teacher-forced NLL and tool-call agreement did not track closed-loop success in our 4B study.
(7)~\textbf{Report process channels and a strict endpoint}: decisions per episode, premature endings, repeated tool calls and a strict error budget can move before the headline score.
(8)~\textbf{Look for nested pass sets} (\textdagger): damage shows up as a compressed cell that almost never passes a task the null fails; re-rolls do not.
(9)~\textbf{Disclose the harness}: context length and truncation policy moved scores by 0.01--0.04 in our setting.
