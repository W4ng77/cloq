\section{Sensitivity Without Higher Precision}
\label{sec:sameprec}

A large turnover or a frequent interaction at a state is tempting evidence that the state needs higher precision. The same-precision reference substitution (\S\ref{sec:estimands}) tests that reading: if these consequences come from precision, swapping one W4 checkpoint for another should produce markedly fewer of them. It also asks the value question on the same units: do the two substitutions rescue and harm the same episodes?

\paragraph{A higher bit width is not necessary for two-sided flips and continuation dependence.} They also appear when only the W4 calibration checkpoint is swapped (Figure~\ref{fig:sameprec}a). On the first \pending{6} complete blocks (interim; the final analysis uses the longest complete block prefix at the evidence freeze), Q$'$ diverges from Q as often as B does (76.9\% of units in both designs). Its action rescues $r'=9.3$ [7.0, 11.7] and damages $d'=8.4$ [6.4, 10.5]\pp{} of units ($M'=17.7$\%, against $M=16.8$\% for B), and 26.2\% [20.7, 31.3] of its divergent states are off the diagonal, against 25.0\% for B. Every one of these same-precision rates has a 95\% lower bound above zero. % src: docs/insights/SAMEPREC_PROTOCOL.md (A1.3, 95% lower bound); analysis/fork/out/sameprec_20261002T142834Z.md (TL;DR; Full pool; On divergent states; analysis set K = 6)
The difference in turnover is $\Delta M=M-M'=-0.98$\pp{} (90\% CI [$-3.26$, $+1.30]$). No precision-specific excess was detected, but equivalence within $\pm$3\pp{} is not shown either (two one-sided tests, $p=0.067$), and the same holds for the net gain ($\Delta\Gall=-1.57$ [$-5.17$, $+2.04$], $p=0.230$) and, descriptively, for the off-diagonal share ($-1.21$ [$-4.09$, $+1.67$], $p=0.133$; Figure~\ref{fig:sameprec}b). % src: analysis/fork/out/sameprec_20261002T142834Z.md (Amendment A1 table, pool rows)
We therefore do not say that the two designs are equivalent or that any change of model has the same effect. We say that flip rate and interaction size alone cannot diagnose a need for an upgrade: a fixed-precision swap already produces them at comparable size.

\paragraph{Similar flip volume, overlapping but not identical units.} Similar average turnover could come from the same or from disjoint sensitive episodes. It is neither (Figure~\ref{fig:sameprec}c). Of 1020 units, 107 flip under both substitutions, against 30 expected if the two flip sets were independent within cell and block (Cohen's $\kappa=0.53$ [0.46, 0.58]), and per-task flip rates correlate at $r=0.68$ across the two designs. Sharing goes beyond task difficulty: shuffling the second design's flips across blocks within each task, which keeps every task's flip propensity, gives 76 [69, 84] both-flip units, against 107 observed. Yet 138 of the 245 flipping units flip under only one substitution. These overlap analyses are descriptive and were added after the interim result was seen. % src: analysis/fork/out/sameprec_overlap_20261002T143218Z.md (Overlap table; Agreement; Task-level); derived: 138=64+74; 245=107+64+74
\paragraph{A shared action-side core, substitution-specific continuation effects.} Splitting each contrast into rescues and harms separates two regimes (Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:split}). On the action side ($G$), the two substitutions share a large core: 56 of B's 82 rescues and 51 of its 89 harms are also rescues and harms of Q$'$ ($\kappa=0.60$ and $0.54$), above a within-task shuffle (37 and 25 shared). Still, 56\% of the units that flip under either substitution flip under only one, against 72\% under the shuffle. % src: analysis/fork/out/sameprec_split_20261002T145148Z.md (Figure-panel table; §1)
On the continuation side ($H$) and under full substitution ($F$), overlap is at or near the shuffle level ($H$ rescues $\kappa=0.20$; $F$ rescues and harms $\kappa=0.34$ and $0.35$, at chance under a stricter shuffle within task and QQ outcome). Continuation dependence is largely substitution-specific: among the 697 units that diverge in both designs, agreement on being off the diagonal is $\kappa=0.29$ against 0.23 under the shuffle, and 70\% of off-diagonal units are off-diagonal under one design only. % src: analysis/fork/out/sameprec_split_20261002T145148Z.md (§1; §2); analysis/fork/out/sameprec_split_20261002T145148Z.json (controller.both_divergent: kappa_null_mean 0.2291, share_specific_pct 69.55)
Flip volume is similar for $G$ and $H$; under full substitution B flips more units than Q$'$ (193 vs.\ 149; $+4.3$ [$+0.4$, $+8.3$]\pp{}, one of several contrasts on six blocks). The net difference comes only from the substitution-specific units and is not resolved ($\Delta G=-1.6$ [$-6.2$, $+3.0$]\pp{}). No unit is rescued under one design and harmed under the other, but this is forced: both designs share the Q episode, so it is no evidence. Sensitivity of the same size thus comes with a shared action-side core and largely substitution-specific continuation effects; only value measures on the same units reveal this. % src: analysis/fork/out/sameprec_split_20261002T145148Z.md (§3 net decomposition; cross-direction counts)

\paragraph{The measured value of the BF16 replacement stands.} The reference changes how B's consequences are read, not what they are. On the full 20 blocks, B's action is worth $\Gall=+2.9$\pp{} in q8 GPTQ-W4 and $-1.9$\pp{} in q4 GPTQ-W4 on the fixed task pool (\S\ref{sec:value}); on the interim same-precision blocks, B's and Q$'$'s net gains are $-0.7$ [$-3.9$, $+2.5$] and $+0.9$ [$-3.1$, $+4.8$]\pp{}. A mean net gain near zero with an interval that covers zero is not a conclusion about value; whether B's action carries value beyond being a different plausible action is the question of the next comparison. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md; analysis/fork/out/sameprec_20261002T142834Z.md (Full pool, pool q4+q8 rows)

\paragraph{Common-state action sources.} Above, each design selects its own divergent states. A pre-registered comparison removes that difference: on 255 ordinary q8 states (85 tasks $\times$ 3 blocks, sampled before any outcome; none excluded), the current action comes from B, from Q (GPTQ-W4, first checkpoint) or from Q$'$ (second checkpoint), and B or Q continues. $K(c)=V(\mathrm{B},c)-V(\mathrm{Q}',c)$ asks what B's action adds beyond another W4 action. A frozen rule claims value only if both 95\% intervals exclude zero (Holm), equivalence only if both 90\% intervals lie within $\pm$3\pp{}. % src: docs/insights/COMMONSTATE_SOURCES_PROTOCOL.md (§2 design, §5 estimands, §6 frozen reading); analysis/fork/out/commonstate_20261002T201612Z.md (header; Excluded units: 0); derived: 255=85*3
B's action is not shown to be worth more than Q$'$'s (Figure~\ref{fig:commonstate}b): under Q's continuation $K=-0.4$\pp{} (95\%: $t$ [$-9.3$, $+8.5$], bootstrap [$-3.5$, $+2.7$]) and under B's $K=+2.4$\pp{} ([$-3.5$, $+8.2$], [$-0.8$, $+5.5$]). Both are ``not resolved'': neither a difference nor equivalence is shown. Each contrast turns over 5.5--7.5\% of states, and a second W4 checkpoint's action departs from Q's as often as B's does (agreement with Q's action 55.7\% against 57.6\%; B with Q$'$ 56.9\%; Figure~\ref{fig:commonstate}c). % src: analysis/fork/out/commonstate_20261002T201612Z.md (Key estimand table; Contrasts table, M column; Action agreement)
Any edge of B appears only when B also continues (31.4\% against 29.0\%; Figure~\ref{fig:commonstate}a), consistent with continuation dependence (\S\ref{sec:value}) but unresolved on three blocks (interaction $+2.7$\pp{}, 90\%: $t$ [$+0.5$, $+5.0$], bootstrap [$-0.4$, $+5.9$]; secondary). % src: analysis/fork/out/commonstate_20261002T201612Z.md (V table; Interaction table, bf16 − d2 row)
Changing outcomes is thus not improving them, and without a same-precision reference B's turnover would read as a precision effect; this does not make precision irrelevant or the two actions equivalent.
