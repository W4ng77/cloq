A large turnover or a frequent interaction is tempting evidence that a state needs higher precision. If these consequences required a higher bit width, the same-precision substitution (\S\ref{sec:estimands}) should produce markedly fewer of them. Because each design forks at its own first divergence, the two may select different states, so shared and exclusive flips cannot be split into effects caused and not caused by precision.

\paragraph{A higher bit width is not necessary for two-sided flips and continuation dependence.} They also appear when only the calibration checkpoint is swapped (Figure~\ref{fig:sameprec}a). At W4 (frozen final analysis set, $K=6$ blocks), the second checkpoint's action rescues $r'=9.3$ (fixed pool [7.0, 11.7]; new tasks [5.9, 13.2]) and damages $d'=8.4$ ([6.4, 10.5]; [5.5, 12.0])\pp{} of units ($M'=17.7$\% against $M=16.8$\% for B on the same 6 blocks and two GPTQ-W4 cells), and 26.2\% of its divergent states are off the diagonal, against 25.0\% for B; each same-precision rate has a fixed-pool 95\% lower bound above zero. % src: docs/insights/SAMEPREC_PROTOCOL.md (A1.3, 95% lower bound); analysis/fork/out/sameprec_20261004T070601Z.md (TL;DR; Full pool; On divergent states; analysis set K = 6); analysis/fork/out/sameprec_20261004T070601Z.json (pooled.same.rescue/damage.crossed_ci95)
In the W3 stress pair the turnovers are 17.3 and 18.8\pp{} and the off-diagonal shares 27.6\% and 27.3\%. Because a same-precision substitute produces turnover and off-diagonal shares of this size, flip rate and interaction size alone cannot diagnose a need for a higher bit width. We do not claim the turnovers are equal: neither pair is equivalent within $\pm$3\pp{} (Appendix~\ref{app:sameprec}). % src: analysis/fork/out/sameprec_w3_20261004T070607Z.md (TL;DR; Full pool, pool rows)

\paragraph{Units affected by both, or by only one, substitution.} At W4 (Appendix~\ref{app:sameprec}), 107 units flip under both substitutions, 64 only under B and 74 only under the second checkpoint. The reference that matters keeps each task's flip propensity: a within-task block shuffle gives 76 [69, 84] units flipping under both, so the substitutions share affected units beyond the fact that the same tasks are flip-prone anyway. % src: analysis/fork/out/sameprec_overlap_20261002T143218Z.md (Overlap table; Agreement); analysis/fork/out/sameprec_w3_split_20261003T024007Z.md (Figure-panel table, G rows)
The shared core is on the action side. We measure it with two Cohen's $\kappa$ statistics over the 1020 W4 units: $\kappa_r$ compares the unit-level indicators ``B's action rescues'' and ``the second checkpoint's action rescues'', and $\kappa_d$ the corresponding damage indicators; $\kappa_r=0.60$ and $\kappa_d=0.54$. When B also takes over the continuation, the affected units are largely substitution-specific (Appendix~\ref{app:sameprec}). The difference in net value comes only from the substitution-specific units and is not resolved ($-1.6$\pp{}; fixed pool [$-6.2$, $+3.0$], new tasks [$-6.5$, $+3.6$]). These analyses are descriptive (Appendix~\ref{app:sameprec}). % src: analysis/fork/out/sameprec_split_20261002T145148Z.md (TL;DR; Figure-panel table; §1; §2; §3); analysis/fork/out/sameprec_20261004T070601Z.json (pooled.paired.dG.crossed_ci95)

\paragraph{Common states: sensitivity, extra value and gain are different quantities.} A pre-registered comparison holds the states fixed: on 510 ordinary q8 states (85 tasks $\times$ 6 blocks), the current action comes from B, from Q or from a second W4 checkpoint Q$'$, and B or Q continues (Figure~\ref{fig:commonstate}b--d). Under Q's continuation, replacing Q's action by B's flips 7.1\% of outcomes for a net $+1.2$\pp{}; replacing it by Q$'$'s flips 8.0\% for a net $+0.2$\pp{}. What B's action adds beyond another W4 action, $K(c)=V(\mathrm{B},c)-V(\mathrm{Q}',c)$, is $+1.0$\pp{} under Q's continuation and $+0.8$\pp{} under B's. After the fixed extension from three to six blocks, no advantage is resolved (95\% $t$ [$-3.5$, $+5.4$] and [$-1.8$, $+3.3$]). Under B's continuation the ordinary 90\% interval lies within $\pm$3\pp{} (two one-sided tests, $t$ [$-1.2$, $+2.8$]), but the two-look sensitivity analysis (94\% intervals) does not, so we do not claim equivalence; q4 (two blocks) is also not resolved (Appendix~\ref{app:sameprec}). A second W4 action changes about as many outcomes as B's, and Q$'$ against Q under Q's continuation is within $\pm$3\pp{} by the same tests (descriptive). How sensitive the loop is to an action, what higher precision adds at a state and what a given upgrade gains must therefore be measured separately. % src: docs/insights/COMMONSTATE_SOURCES_PROTOCOL.md (§2, §5, §6; A1.1, A1.3; D2.2); analysis/fork/out/commonstate_q8ext_20261003T231159Z.md (TL;DR; Contrasts table: bf16 − d1 and d2 − d1 at c = d1, columns Δ and M, reading; Key estimand table, t95 and t90; Two-look reading table); analysis/fork/out/commonstate_q4_20261004T001159Z.md (TL;DR, q4 K)
