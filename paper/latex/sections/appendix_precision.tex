\subsection{Common states: extra value of the BF16 action}
\label{app:commonstate}
\paragraph{Common-state comparison: details.} Above, each design selects its own divergent states. A comparison pre-registered at three blocks removes that difference: on 510 ordinary BF16-sourced q8 states (85 tasks $\times$ 6 blocks, sampled by a fixed rule), the current action comes from B, from Q (GPTQ-W4, first checkpoint) or from Q$'$ (second checkpoint), and B or Q continues. $K(c)=V(\mathrm{B},c)-V(\mathrm{Q}',c)$ asks what B's action adds beyond another W4 action. A frozen rule claims value only if both intervals exclude zero (Holm), equivalence only if both lie within $\pm$3\pp{} (two one-sided tests). % src: docs/insights/COMMONSTATE_SOURCES_PROTOCOL.md (§2 design, §5 estimands, §6 frozen reading; A1.1); analysis/fork/out/commonstate_q8ext_20261003T231159Z.md (header); derived: 510=85*6
The design first ran three blocks, and that first look was unresolved (under Q's continuation $K=-0.4$\pp{}, 95\%: $t$ [$-9.3$, $+8.5$], bootstrap [$-3.5$, $+2.7$]; under B's $+2.4$\pp{}, [$-3.5$, $+8.2$], [$-0.8$, $+5.5$]). Three more blocks were then added. That decision followed the unresolved first look: a dated amendment fixed the extension and the two-look rule after that look and before any extension run had produced output, and the protocol designates this two-look reading (Pocock levels from a normal approximation: about 97\% intervals for a difference and 94\% for equivalence) as the confirmatory one. The Pocock constant is not calibrated for small-$k$ $t$ intervals, the task bootstrap or the joint rule, so the two-look reading carries no exact error guarantee. Because the extension was chosen after the first look, even the two-look reading cannot fully protect the error rate conditional on the observed first look (protocol D2.2). We therefore print it next to the unadjusted reading at the frozen levels and claim neither an advantage nor equivalence unless both agree. % src: analysis/fork/out/commonstate_20261002T201612Z.md (Key estimand table); docs/insights/COMMONSTATE_SOURCES_PROTOCOL.md (A1 'What was known', A1.3, D2.2)
After the fixed extension (six blocks), B's action is not shown to be worth more than Q$'$'s, and equivalence is not established. Under Q's continuation, $K=+1.0$\pp{} (95\%: $t$ [$-3.5$, $+5.4$], bootstrap [$-1.2$, $+3.1$]; two-look 97\%: [$-4.2$, $+6.2$], [$-1.4$, $+3.5$]). Under B's, $K=+0.8$\pp{} (95\%: [$-1.8$, $+3.3$] for both; two-look 97\%: [$-2.2$, $+3.8$], [$-2.0$, $+3.7$]). For equivalence under B's continuation, the ordinary 90\% intervals lie within $\pm$3\pp{} ($t$ [$-1.2$, $+2.8$], bootstrap [$-1.4$, $+2.9$]) and the 94\% intervals of the protocol-designated two-look confirmatory reading do not ($t$ [$-1.6$, $+3.2$]); because the extension followed the first look, we do not claim equivalence. Without the 26 states at which earlier studies had already produced outcomes, $K$ is $+0.5$ and $+0.8$\pp{}, also not resolved, and the unadjusted reading no longer shows equivalence under B's continuation either; BF16's own outcome at the extension states was also known in aggregate before the amendment (protocol D2.1). % src: analysis/fork/out/commonstate_q8ext_20261003T231159Z.md (Key estimand table; Two-look reading table; Sensitivity without the D2.1 states); docs/insights/COMMONSTATE_SOURCES_PROTOCOL.md (D2.1)
Under Q's continuation, B's action flips $M=7.1$\% of outcomes (rescue 4.1, damage 2.9) for a net $\Delta=+1.2$\pp{} (95\%: $t$ [$-1.3$, $+3.6$]); Q$'$'s flips 8.0\% (4.1, 3.9) for $+0.2$\pp{} ([$-2.3$, $+2.7$]; within $\pm$3\pp{} by two one-sided tests). A second W4 checkpoint's action departs from Q's at least as often as B's does (agreement with Q's action 53.9\% against 55.7\%; B with Q$'$ 57.3\%). The interaction of $K$ with the continuation is $-0.2$\pp{} (95\%: $t$ [$-4.5$, $+4.1$], bootstrap [$-2.7$, $+2.4$]; secondary, not resolved). % src: analysis/fork/out/commonstate_q8ext_20261003T231159Z.md (Contrasts table, c = d1 rows; Action agreement; Interaction table, bf16 − d2 row)
\emph{q4, a separate prospective analysis.} The same design on Qwen3-4B planned three blocks. The frozen quarantine rule removed f01 (below), leaving 170 states. $K=-1.2$\pp{} under Q's continuation (bootstrap 95\% [$-5.9$, $+3.5$]) and $-2.4$\pp{} under B's ([$-6.5$, $+1.2$]), both not resolved. With two blocks the $t$ interval has one degree of freedom. Under Q's continuation the two block means happen to coincide, so it collapses to a point. That is a coincidence of two values, not a precise measurement or zero variance, and the reading rests on the bootstrap. % src: analysis/fork/out/commonstate_q4_20261004T001159Z.md (TL;DR; Key estimand table)
\emph{Identity checks.} Across both models, 1{,}238 of 1{,}240 reference and implicit identity checks passed. Both failures are one q4 state. There the substituted action was semantically identical to the original after parsing, but its token sequence differed in the order of keys inside a tool-call argument. The canonical comparison treats such actions as equal, but the chat template renders the order, so the next prompt differed and the continuation could not be a replay. Every check with a token-identical action passed (869/869). As the frozen rule requires, the block was removed. % src: analysis/fork/commonstate_replay_check/Q4_F01_IDENTITY.md (TL;DR; acceptance table); docs/insights/COMMONSTATE_ERRATA.md (E-2)
Changing outcomes is thus not improving them; this does not make precision irrelevant or the two actions equivalent.

\paragraph{Common states, BF16 against Q$'$, by component.} At the same states and continuation, BF16's extra rescues over Q$'$ on q8 are $+0.3$\pp{} and its extra damages $-0.6$\pp{}, with the net $K=+0.9$ unresolved; each component lies within $\pm$3\pp{} under a 90\% two one-sided test (exploratory, post hoc; the margin is wide relative to rescue rates of 3--5\%). q4 is unresolved. % src: analysis/fork/out/commonstate_rd_20261005T185153Z.md

\subsection{Key-order control}
\label{app:keyorder}

\paragraph{Design.} Protocol \texttt{KEYORDER\_PERTURB\_PROTOCOL.md} (ko-v1, exploratory control). Eligible states are first tool-structure divergences of q8 GPTQ-W4 (Bank P) where Q's action is a tool call with at least two top-level argument keys, reversing them leaves the parsed call and the environment effects unchanged, and the next prompt tokens differ (300 of 1473 divergent states, 41 tasks). The arm KQ forces the reversed action and lets Q continue; QQ and BQ are reused from Bank P. Eligibility was fixed without outcomes; the analysis code (sha256-recorded) was frozen before any KQ outcome was read. All four tasks passed acceptance (KQ checks 300/300, shadow 300/300, QQ no-op 35/35, no errors or context overflows). Two tasks were restarted with resume during the run (Amendment 2026-10-05g). % src: docs/insights/KEYORDER_PERTURB_PROTOCOL.md; analysis/fork/keyorder/eligibility_v1.md; analysis/fork/out/keyorder_20261005T204349Z.md; derived: 300=72+80+73+75; 35=7+9+11+8
\paragraph{Results.} Conditional on eligible states (pooled ratio; intervals are the wider of block and crossed bootstrap), KQ changes 6.7\% [0.8, 14.4] of outcomes and B 9.0\% [2.9, 18.2]; KQ has 10 rescues and 10 damages, B 10 and 17, and the flips coincide on 5 rescues and 7 damages. Rescue and damage shares (Figure~\ref{fig:refs}b): KQ 3.3\% [0.0, 9.3] and 3.3\% [0.4, 8.0]; B 3.3\% [0.0, 9.2] and 5.7\% [1.5, 11.5]. Over the full 1700-unit pool, KQ contributes 1.2\pp{} [0.1, 2.8] of turnover against 17.9\pp{} for the BF16 swap on all divergent states. % src: analysis/fork/out/keyorder_20261005T204349Z.md (table; joint flips); derived: 10=5+5; 17=7+10
