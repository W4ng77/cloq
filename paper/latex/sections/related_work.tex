\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Quantized agents compared episode by episode.} \emph{Flat Score, Amplified Failures} \citep{jang2026flat} is the closest study in setting: on \tautwo{} retail and telecom, 4-bit agents keep a flat task score while process damage grows, with a two-sided paired task split (33 tasks worse, 26 better for Gemma-4-31B on telecom) and recovery equally frequent at both precisions (55\% after a failed call). % src: docs/insights/FLATSCORE_COMPARISON.md
Its episodes are sampled independently, so the precision of the action that went wrong and that of the controller that must recover change together; we hold the state fixed and vary the two separately. Two-sided flips under compression were first shown on static tasks \citep{dutta2024accuracy}; agent-compression benchmarks report averages \citep{dong2025acbench}.

\paragraph{Forks and counterfactual replay.} \emph{The Replay Gap} \citep{gonuguntla2026replay} evaluates per-step model switching for routers. It forks live SWE-bench Verified trajectories at 30\% and 70\% of their length, re-executes the prefix in a fresh container, continues each fork with the other model of a pair (Qwen3-4B FP8 and Qwen3-14B AWQ), and compares against same-model control forks; swaps rewrite most post-fork actions, all five observed outcome flips occur in swap arms and none in 359 control forks, and log-stitching replay mispredicts outcomes. Live forking and a same-model control are therefore not new. Its swap replaces the next action and the continuation together, which is our ``both'' contrast, and its control holds the model fixed to measure sampling and replay noise. We cross the action source with the continuation controller at a bit-exact frozen state, and our reference changes the model while holding the bit width fixed. % src: outputs/.drafts/replay_gap_2608.08239_excerpt.md; outputs/.drafts/storyline-v7-lit.md (row 1)
Restoring a state, replacing one action and letting the original policy continue locates decisive errors \citep{zhang2025agentracer}, attributes failures through a do-intervention on one step \citep{shah2026car}, and turns alternative actions into training signal \citep{li2026cso,li2026crr}. This is our ``this step only'' contrast, and the factorial design itself is standard. We found no work among the 32 closest that crosses the action with the continuation at a precision-induced divergence or reads the result against a same-precision alternative. % src: outputs/.drafts/storyline-v7-lit.md (TL;DR, findings (b))

\paragraph{Precision routing and its signals.} DMR \citep{li2026dynamicmp} learns a per-step BF16/W4 router from BF16-versus-quantized KL supervision and episode reward. Sensitivity has also been localised to phases \citep{lu2026mixquant} and task types \citep{zhang2026quantclaw}, and for quantized reasoning models to high-entropy, high-KL token positions \citep{lotfi2026quantized}. We do not propose a router. We measure the task value a router should learn, and our simple frozen trigger (\S\ref{sec:r4}) does not test, let alone refute, a learned router such as DMR.

\paragraph{Evaluation noise and user simulators.} Prefix caching changed multi-turn tool-use trajectories in 36.2\% of episodes at 16-bit and 75.0\% at 4-bit \citep{patodiya2026same}; agentic evaluations vary from run to run \citep{bjarnason2026randomness,chen2026noisefloor} and with the user model \citep{seshadri2026lost,jain2026userproxybench}. % src: outputs/.drafts/storyline-v7-lit.md (row 16)
