\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

We position prior work by the quantity it identifies.

\paragraph{Episode-level comparisons of quantized agents.} \emph{Flat Score, Amplified Failures} \citep{jang2026flat} is the closest study in setting. On \tautwo{} retail and telecom it shows that 4-bit agents keep a flat task score while process damage grows. It reports a two-sided paired task split (33 tasks worse, 26 better for Gemma-4-31B on telecom). It explains the flat score by the error budget, through a counterfactual projection from logs, and by recovery, which is equally frequent at both precisions (55\% after a failed call). It also tests a repair prompt. % src: docs/insights/FLATSCORE_COMPARISON.md
Its comparisons are between independently sampled episodes, so the precision of the action that went wrong and the precision of the controller that must recover change together. Our design holds the state fixed and varies the two separately. $H$ measures what the recovering controller contributes on identical histories, and the $(\DQ,\DB)$ table measures whether an action's consequence depends on that controller. Two-sided flips under compression were first shown on static tasks \citep{dutta2024accuracy}; agent-compression benchmarks report averages \citep{dong2025acbench}.

\paragraph{Live forks with a model switch.} \emph{The Replay Gap} \citep{gonuguntla2026replay} forks live SWE-agent trajectories at a fixed step and continues them with a different model (Qwen3-4B FP8 vs.\ Qwen3-14B AWQ), against same-model control forks. At the switch point the new model produces the next action \emph{and} the continuation, so the two change together; this is our $F$ contrast, not $G$ or $H$. Its 359 control forks had 0 success flips, and its FP8-served controls diverged in action sequence on 90--96\% of forks. % src: outputs/.drafts/storyline-v7-lit.md (row 1)
We claim no noise advantage over that work. Our zero replay noise is a property of our deterministic serving (\S\ref{sec:replay}). Single-step action swaps under a fixed continuation fill one cell of our design and are used for training \citep{li2026cso,li2026crr}, attribution \citep{shah2026car} and locating decisive errors \citep{zhang2025agentracer}. None of the 32 closest works we checked crosses the action with the continuation controller at a precision-induced divergence. % src: outputs/.drafts/storyline-v7-lit.md (TL;DR, findings (b))

\paragraph{Precision routing and its proxies.} DMR \citep{li2026dynamicmp} learns a per-step BF16/W4 router from BF16-versus-quantized KL supervision and episode reward. Precision sensitivity has also been localised to phases \citep{lu2026mixquant} and task types \citep{zhang2026quantclaw}. We do not propose a competing router. We measure what a router would harvest, namely the intervention gains $G$, $H$ and $F$ at given states, and we test whether a Q-visible proxy predicts them. For quantized reasoning models, \citet{lotfi2026quantized} trace damage to high-entropy, high-KL token positions, where the quantized model over-samples markers such as ``wait''. A logit penalty on those markers both repairs the damage and validates the mechanism. \S\ref{sec:r4} tests the analogous uncertainty view for agent actions.

\paragraph{Evaluation noise and user simulators.} Prefix caching changed multi-turn tool-use trajectories in 36.2\% of episodes at 16-bit and 75.0\% at 4-bit, and runs were bit-identical with the cache off \citep{patodiya2026same}. % src: outputs/.drafts/storyline-v7-lit.md (row 16)
Agentic evaluations vary from run to run \citep{bjarnason2026randomness,chen2026noisefloor}. Changing the user model moves $\tau$-bench retail success by up to 9 points \citep{seshadri2026lost} and reward by 15.2 points \citep{jain2026userproxybench}. % src: outputs/.drafts/storyline-v7-lit.md (rows 23-24)
