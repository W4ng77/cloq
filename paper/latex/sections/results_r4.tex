\section{R4: Can Beneficial Replacements Be Found in Advance?}
\label{sec:r4}

$G$, $H$ and $F$ are oracle quantities that require running B and all four branches; a deployable rule sees only Q. Following \citet{lotfi2026quantized}, we scored every Bank U f01 state at the token level (1140 cell $\times$ state rows, 58 flips), teacher-forcing both models on the generated ids of $a_{\mathrm{B}}$ and $a_{\mathrm{Q}}$. We record truncated top-20 entropy, the top-1 margin and a top-20-union KL, not full entropy or KL. % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261001T185031Z.md (§0); docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W88
All analyses are exploratory, and f01 is development data.

\paragraph{Uncertainty locates divergence.} Q's entropy at the start of its action separates states where B and Q act differently from states where they act identically, with AUC 0.66--0.91 per sub-bank and model. Over all ordinary states, flips also sit at lower Q margins (U1: AUC 0.31 [0.22, 0.40] for q4, 0.24 [0.13, 0.36] for q8, stratified by cell and action length). % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261001T185031Z.md (§1a, §1b "differing vs exact"); docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W88

\paragraph{Among divergent states, it does not reliably identify consequences.} Restricting to states where the actions differ removes the ``any difference at all'' signal. In U1 the effect vanishes: no Q-visible statistic has an interval excluding 0.5. Only q4 U2B keeps a moderate signal (mean entropy over $a_{\mathrm{Q}}$, AUC 0.73 [0.53, 0.89]). At the first divergent token, entropy and KL do not separate flips from non-flips, with one exception on 4 flips (q4 U2Q, KL AUC 0.80 [0.65, 0.93]). No class of first divergent token reproduces the marker enrichment of \citet{lotfi2026quantized}. The largest q4 enrichment is ordinary word choice (rate ratio 1.89, Fisher $p=.019$, not corrected). % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261001T185031Z.md (§1a differing_only, §1b, §2); docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W88
On development data, uncertainty thus marks \emph{where} quantization changes the action much better than \emph{which} changes matter.

\paragraph{A frozen trigger, tested on held-out blocks.} We froze a Q-visible trigger on f01 before any f02/f03 state was scored: escalate one decision when the minimum Q margin over $a_{\mathrm{Q}}$ is low, with per-cell thresholds at 5, 10 and 20\% escalation rates (rule hash in Appendix~\ref{app:oracle}). % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261001T185031Z.md (§3); docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W88
On held-out f02/f03 (1020 U1 rows, 69 turnover states) it escalated 50, 114 and 222 states. Net gains were $+4$, $+2$ and $+5$ (rescues/damages 6/2, 7/5, 16/11) against matched-count random expectations of $+0.21$, $+0.98$ and $+2.38$ (exact one-sided hypergeometric $p=.0345$, $.417$, $.262$). It captured 8, 12 and 27 turnover states against 3.48, 7.52 and 14.30 expected ($p=.0182$, $.0613$, $.0002$). % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3 Validation, U1 S_mm PRIMARY)
With no level fixed in advance, net gain passes a nominal one-sided .05 only at the 5\% rate, and not after Holm correction. At 20\% (21.8\% of rows selected), the net gain of about 0.49\pp{} exceeds random (0.23\pp{}) by 0.26\pp{}. % derived: 0.49=100*5/1020; 0.23=100*2.38/1020; 21.8=100*222/1020
The frozen Q-only rule enriched outcome-changing decisions in later nonce blocks but has not been shown to select net-beneficial replacements; two new nonce blocks do not establish routing gains on new tasks.

\paragraph{Headroom.} A hindsight action-replacement oracle with the same per-cell counts nets $+3.33$, $+3.73$ and $+3.73$\pp{}; the rule's $+0.39$, $+0.20$ and $+0.49$ capture 4/34, 2/38 and 5/38 of that net. A three-option menu oracle, $E[\max(G,H,F,0)]$, reaches 0.202 on W4 Bank P divergent states against 0.117 for the action alone; it allows other replacement types and is not a same-cost deployment comparison. % src: analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1a, §1b)
A post-hoc reference auxiliary baseline, top-20-union KL$(\mathrm{B}\|\mathrm{Q})$ with a tail bucket fit on f01, captures turnover above chance (33 vs.\ 16.7 at 20\%) but no significant net gain (Holm $p=1.0$); it needs a BF16 prefill per decision, so it is not Q-only, and is not a DMR reproduction (Appendix~\ref{app:oracle}). % src: analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§2)
