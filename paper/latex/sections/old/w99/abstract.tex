\begin{abstract}
When a quantized agent and its BF16 reference act differently at a state, should BF16 supply this step, take over from the next step, or both? We measure this with a crossed same-state intervention: at a frozen state that replays bit-exactly, a $2\times2$ factorial design crosses the source of the current action (BF16 or quantized) with the controller of the rest of the episode. We study Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B under simulated weight and KV-cache quantization with BF16 compute, using one greedy user simulator in \tautwo{} and forking at the first decision where the two models' actions differ in tool structure. % nocheck: model sizes and design label
We report three findings. (i)~The value of an action depends on the controller that continues. In 26.9\% of development W4 divergent states, the same action swap has a different outcome effect under BF16 and under quantized continuation, and this dependence also appears, to differing degrees, on BFCL and ALFWorld, which have no LLM user. % src: analysis/fork/out/ghf_20261001T211519Z.md (§2); analysis/fork/out/ext_r2_20261002T024433Z.md (§3, §6)
(ii)~Local upgrades redistribute success and failure. Replacing a W4 agent's action rescues or damages 15.5--20.9\% of task-block units, with a near-zero net effect on development tasks. On 34 pre-registered unseen telecom tasks, the swap's estimated effect was $-4.6$\pp{} (90\% CI $-7.2$ to $-2.0$); the pre-registered readings, which had no negative category, classify it as inconclusive. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md; analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md; docs/insights/PREREG_FORK_CONFIRM.md §0

(iii)~A frozen uncertainty trigger finds outcome-changing decisions more often than random selection at two of three rates, but its net gain is not significant after correction. % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3)
Precision upgrades for agents should be judged by the task value of the replacement under the execution that follows it.
\end{abstract}
