\begin{abstract}
Precision upgrades in closed-loop agents can replace the current action, the subsequent controller, or both. We present an auditable same-state protocol that measures these interventions separately and compares higher-precision replacement with a same-precision alternative.
On Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B in \tautwo{}, replacing a W4 agent's first tool-structure-divergent action with BF16 changes 15.5--20.9\% of task--block outcomes, while net effects range from $-1.9$ to $+2.9$ percentage points. On 34 held-out telecom tasks under a frozen protocol, the net effect is $-4.6$ points (crossed 90\% interval [$-7.2$, $-2.0$]). % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md; analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md
Crossed branches expose complementary upgrades and cases where continuation takeover undoes an action-level rescue.
On ordinary BF16-sourced states of Mistral-Small-3.2-24B, turnover is approximately 10\%, again comprising rescues and damages. % src: analysis/fork/out/newmethod_m24_20261004T024453Z.md
Same-precision checkpoint substitutions also produce substantial turnover, while BF16's additional action value on common states remains unresolved. % src: analysis/fork/out/sameprec_20261004T070601Z.md (Amendment A1); analysis/fork/out/commonstate_q8ext_20261003T231159Z.md; analysis/fork/out/commonstate_q4_20261004T001159Z.md
A frozen uncertainty trigger enriches outcome-changing decisions without demonstrating reliable net benefit. % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3); analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1a)
These findings separate intervention sensitivity, upgrade value, and the extra value of higher precision, providing a protocol for evaluating precision choices through paired task consequences.
\end{abstract}
