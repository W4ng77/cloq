Independently of the results, the design has two practical readings. First, $\Gall$ is the expected benefit of sending exactly one decision per episode --- the first one on which the quantized agent departs from the reference --- back to the reference model; it therefore measures what step-level precision routing would gain at that one decision under the tested continuation, not over a whole episode. Second, the decomposition tells an evaluator where to look: a large $C$ with a small $A$ points to the subsequent execution rather than to any single action, whereas a large $A$ points to the actions themselves and to the annotation of \S\ref{sec:annotation}. Which reading applies here is an empirical question that the tables above will answer.
