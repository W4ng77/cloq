\begin{abstract}
When a weight-quantized LLM agent acts in a closed loop, a single changed action alters everything that follows, so aggregate task success and open-loop probability probes cannot say which consequences quantization causes or where they come from. We ask: on identical interaction histories, which task consequences do quantization-induced local action changes cause, and do they come from the current action, from the subsequent execution, or from their interaction? We build a same-state four-branch evaluation. At the first semantic action divergence between a BF16 agent and its W4 counterpart reading the identical history, we execute either agent's action and continue with either agent, which yields a one-replacement gain $G$, its split into rescues and new damage, and an additive decomposition of the total change into current-action, continuation and interaction terms. We run it on Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B with GPTQ-W4 and AWQ-W4 in \tautwo{} under byte-deterministic serving, with independent prompt-nonce blocks as replicates, blinded human review of the mechanism labels, and a frozen confirmation on a held-out telecom split. \pending{findings: one sentence per headline result, filled only from the development and confirmation analyses (\S\ref{sec:fork-results}, \S\ref{sec:confirm})}
\end{abstract}
