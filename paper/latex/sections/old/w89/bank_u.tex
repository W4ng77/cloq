\section{Layer 2: Shared-State Consequence Structure}
\label{sec:banku}

The fork states of \S\ref{sec:atlas} are selected: each is the first divergence, and text-only differences before it are not intervened on. To learn \emph{which} differences carry consequences at ordinary states, we built Bank U, a bank of states sampled independently of any outcome.

\paragraph{Design.} Each Bank U state is one uniformly drawn decision per task, with the draw fixed by a hash, and the manifest was frozen before any generation. U1 contains states from each quantized cell's own trajectories. U2B contains BF16-sourced states, and U2Q a small set of Q-sourced shared states. Every configuration of a model receives the identical full U2 state, which allows same-state comparison across quantizers. Every state runs all four branches unless $a_{\mathrm{B}}$ and $a_{\mathrm{Q}}$ are token-identical, and the suffix effect is never imputed as zero. A frozen 25\% subsample of U2 is also continued by one common controller Q0, the GPTQ-W4 checkpoint of the same model. This puts every quantizer's action under the same continuation. Here we report block f01: 85 U1, 85 U2B and 20 U2Q states per cell, for q4/q8 $\times$ \{GPTQ-W4, AWQ-W4, KV8\}. With one block, the intervals resample tasks only. % src: analysis/fork/out/bank_u_f01_20261001T134456Z.md; docs/insights/FORK_PROTOCOL.md §15 amendment 1

\paragraph{Consequence rates at ordinary states.} At ordinary states, quantization changes the outcome less often than at first divergences. Per-state $M$ is 8--12\% for q4 W4 and 0--5\% for q8 and KV8, and $G$ covers zero everywhere (Table~\ref{tab:banku}). % src: analysis/fork/out/bank_u_f01_20261001T134456Z.md
Exact action agreement is 0.44--0.85 per cell and sub-bank. Identical actions never change the outcome (class (i), Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}), as the deterministic protocol implies. % src: analysis/fork/out/bank_u_f01_20261001T134456Z.md (column "exact")

\paragraph{Which differences matter: mostly text.} After deduplication, 39 of the 56 outcome-changing differences ($\approx$69.6\%) are class (ii): the two actions have the same tool structure but different text, nearly always two different natural-language replies (Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}). Only 5 of the 56 involve a different tool name, call count or argument. % src: analysis/fork/out/bank_u_f01_20261001T134456Z.md; docs/REPORT_GPT61_20261001_0915.md (correction 1)
This is a \emph{composition share}. Text differences dominate the flips mainly because they are common. Per instance, a text difference flips the outcome in 0--18\% of rows depending on model and sub-bank. An act/talk difference flips in 0--54\%, though on small denominators. % src: analysis/fork/out/bank_u_f01_20261001T134456Z.md (M rate column of the class composition table)
Simple content features do not separate flipping from non-flipping text pairs: a question, a confirmation request, a claim of completed action or a new entity on one side only. Only a larger length difference co-occurs with flips, and only for q4 (median $|\Delta\text{len}|$ 118 vs.\ 71 characters in U1; 97 vs.\ 48 in U2B). This is descriptive; no test was run. % src: analysis/fork/out/bank_u_f01_20261001T134456Z.md (§4)
The first-divergence atlas of \S\ref{sec:atlas} does not intervene on text-only differences. On ordinary states, such differences account for most outcome changes.

\paragraph{Shared fragility across quantizers (a lead).} On the 85 shared q4 U2B states, cell pairs co-flip 7 times, against about 1.4 expected under independence, and 6 of the 7 co-flips go in the same direction. None of the joint flips has an identical quantized action across cells. These 7 co-flips come from only \textbf{5 distinct states} in one block. % src: analysis/fork/out/bank_u_f01_20261001T134456Z.md (§6, q4:U2B); docs/REPORT_GPT61_20261001_0915.md (correction 2)
On q8 U2B, the 5 flipping states each flip under a single cell. We therefore read this as a lead toward \emph{state-level} fragility, where different actions at the same state flip the outcome, and not yet as a finding. \todo{BANKU-F023: per-sub-bank $M$, taxonomy and co-flip counts at blocks f01--f03 with block intervals, from \nolinkurl{analysis/fork/analyze_bank_u.py}}

\paragraph{Common continuation Q0.} Under Q0, every cell's BF16 branch has the identical outcome on all 46 shared states, which checks determinism. Q0 turnover is at least the native turnover on the same rows; for example, q4 AWQ-W4 U2B gives 3/21 versus 1/21. With $n=6$--21 per cell and sub-bank, this is too small to attribute consequences to the controller. % src: analysis/fork/out/bank_u_f01_20261001T134456Z.md (§5)
