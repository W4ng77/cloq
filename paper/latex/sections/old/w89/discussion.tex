\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{How action changes become consequences.} Replacing a quantization-induced action at the first divergence changes the outcome in 15.5--20.9\% of W4 task-block units. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md
 It matters in both directions, so the gain from replacing the action at the fork is small on average. Which way a change goes depends on the state. The concentration of flips in fragile tasks, the co-flips of different quantizers with different actions at the same state, and the dominance of text differences all point to properties of the state and of the full language action. They do not point to a stable ``quantization error'' that a later controller repairs or not. The injection study and the process audit bound the alternative story: an injected local defect is about as consequential as a generic process perturbation, and failing continuations differ by \emph{wrong} changes and loops rather than by more writes.

\paragraph{Implications for evaluating quantized agents.} (1)~Report $r$, $d$ and $M$ next to any net quantity. A small $\Gall$ coexisted with 15.5--20.9\% turnover. % src: analysis/fork/out/dev_all_20261001T070326Z.md
(2)~Keep the forked-state gain apart from the end-to-end success comparison; they answer different questions, and the latter needs its own paired, task-resampled interval (Appendix~\ref{app:e2e}). (3)~Treat language-only differences as actions. At ordinary states they carry most consequential differences, and tool-call metrics do not see them.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} We do not claim success equivalence between BF16 and W4 agents. Equivalence tests at $\pm$3\pp{} fail. % src: analysis/insights/iter_4/out/success_ni_ALIGNED_20260930T213009Z.md
We do not claim a W$\times$KV interaction, whose pre-registered replication was not supported. We do not present static precision restoration as a method, and we do not claim that ``errors are absorbed'' (Appendix~\ref{app:withdrawn}). $\Gall$ is a retrospective oracle quantity, since finding the fork state requires running both models. It is not the value of a deployable escalation policy.
