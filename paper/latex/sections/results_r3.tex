\paragraph{The frozen trigger enriches outcome-changing decisions at two of three rates.} A router needs a signal that picks net-positive upgrades, but $G$, $H$ and $F$ require running B, and a deployable rule sees only Q. On one development block, Q's token uncertainty separates states where B and Q act differently from states where they agree, but no Q-visible statistic reliably separates flips from non-flips among differing states (Appendix~\ref{app:token}). % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261001T185031Z.md (§1a; §1b differing vs exact)
We then froze a Q-only trigger on that block, before any later state was scored: escalate one decision, meaning that B replaces only the current action and Q continues (the $G$ contrast), when the minimum Q margin over its action is low (margins predict cross-precision divergence; \citealp{du2026greedy}), with per-configuration thresholds targeting 5, 10 and 20\% on the development block. These fixed thresholds do not impose exact quotas on later states; Figure~\ref{fig:trigger} shows realised counts, and random selection matches those counts within each configuration. The margin score was chosen among three Q-only candidates (entropy, margin, mean log-probability) by its net gain over random on that block. On two later blocks of the development tasks (not the telecom holdout), at ordinary states from Q's own trajectories (six configurations: four W4, two KV8; 1020 states, 69 outcome-changing) it captured 8, 12 and 27 outcome-changing states, against 3.48, 7.52 and 14.30 expected at random ($p=.0182$, $.0613$, $.0002$; Figure~\ref{fig:trigger}a). At ordinary states from BF16 trajectories (secondary), only the 20\% rate enriches (Appendix~\ref{app:token}). % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3 Validation, U1 S_mm PRIMARY; U2B S_mm secondary); manifests/fork/token_trigger_rule_v1.json (population; candidates; selection_criterion)

\paragraph{Its net gain is not reliably above random.} The captured states were rescues and damages alike (6/2, 7/5 and 16/11). Net gains were $+4$, $+2$ and $+5$ outcomes, against random expectations of $+0.21$, $+0.98$ and $+2.38$ (exact one-sided $p=.0345$, $.417$, $.262$). No escalation rate was designated primary in advance, and the 5\% result does not survive Holm correction over the three rates. A hindsight oracle with the same per-configuration counts nets $+34$, $+38$ and $+38$ outcomes ($+3.33$, $+3.73$ and $+3.73$\pp{}; Figure~\ref{fig:trigger}b). This headroom is positive by construction, because the oracle picks only rescues; the trigger selects 6, 7 and 16 of the oracle's 34, 38 and 38 rescues. A post hoc KL$(\mathrm{B}\|\mathrm{Q})$ score, which needs BF16, also captures turnover above chance on these states without a significant net gain (exploratory, post hoc; Appendix~\ref{app:oracle}); configuration-level KL analyses are in Appendix~\ref{app:kl}. % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3 Validation, U1 S_mm PRIMARY); analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1a, §2)

\paragraph{The negative result is specific to this rule.} Enriching outcome-changing decisions is not enough for reliable net gain \citep[cf.][]{zhang2026credit}. The later blocks reuse the development tasks, and only this simple frozen rule is tested. The result does not test learned or other uncertainty-based escalation \citep{li2026dynamicmp,piatrashyn2026redact,lei2026tacit} and does not refute sensitivity routing in general.
