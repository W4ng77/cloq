\S\ref{sec:value} and \S\ref{sec:sameprec} show that outcomes are sensitive to the action and that sensitivity alone does not identify a need for more bits. A router needs more: a signal that picks the upgrades with positive net value. We test a fidelity diagnostic on shared states and a frozen Q-only trigger.

\paragraph{KL tracks whether the action changes, not what the change does.} On ordinary BF16-sourced states shared by every method (U2B), we compare methods by $D_{cc}=\YY{QB}-\YY{BB}$, Q's action against B's under the shared BF16 continuation (Appendix~\ref{app:panels}). Across the eleven q8 cells (exploratory, descriptive Spearman), mean teacher-forced approximate token KL$(\mathrm{B}\|\mathrm{Q})$ follows the action-difference rate ($\rho=0.99$); its relations with the flip rate given a difference ($0.46$) and with the net $D_{cc}$ ($-0.44$) are weak. Its correlations with the rescue rate ($\rho=0.81$) and damage rate ($\rho=0.93$) run through the difference rate. Within cells it separates differing from identical actions (mean AUC 0.93) but not flips among them (0.46). SpinQuant-W4A4 is closer to BF16 than QuaRot-W4A4 (KL 0.165 against 0.199), yet the pre-registered contrast of their task value is $+1.2$\pp{} ($t$, 3 blocks [$-3.9$, $+6.2$]; crossed [$-4.7$, $+7.1$]; Holm-adjusted $p=1.0$; one block rests on an A7 re-run). An error-dose scan agrees: action agreement with BF16 falls at every dose step, while success responds only at the largest steps of one stress quantizer (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:dose}). % src: analysis/fork/out/kl_decomp_20261005T000559Z.md (TL;DR; §2; §3); analysis/fork/out/newmethod_q8_20261004T051538Z.md; analysis/fork/out/newmethod_q8_20261004T051538Z.json (tau2.contrasts; fidelity.cells.*.KLu_mean); docs/insights/NEWMETHOD_PANEL_PROTOCOL.md (§2.1, §4, A1.2, A7); analysis/fork/out/backfill_20261003T193042Z.md (TL;DR 2; §2)

\paragraph{Frozen trigger.} $G$, $H$ and $F$ require running B and all four branches; a deployable rule sees only Q. We test whether a Q-only signal that finds outcome-changing decisions also finds the ones worth upgrading.
Scored at the token level on one development block, Q's entropy at the start of its action separates states where B and Q act differently from states where they act identically (AUC 0.66--0.91 per sub-bank and model), but among states where the actions differ, no Q-visible statistic reliably separates flips from non-flips (Appendix~\ref{app:token}). % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261001T185031Z.md (§1a, §1b)
We then froze a Q-only trigger on that block before any later state was scored: escalate one decision when the minimum Q margin over its action is low (margins predict cross-precision divergence; \citealp{du2026greedy}), with per-cell thresholds at 5, 10 and 20\% escalation rates. On two held-out blocks (1020 rows, 69 outcome-changing states) it captured 8, 12 and 27 outcome-changing states, against 3.48, 7.52 and 14.30 expected at random ($p=.0182$, $.0613$, $.0002$; Figure~\ref{fig:oracle}b). % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3 Validation, U1 S_mm PRIMARY)

\paragraph{It does not yet select net-beneficial replacements.} The captured states were rescues and damages alike (6/2, 7/5 and 16/11), for net gains of $+4$, $+2$ and $+5$ against random expectations of $+0.21$, $+0.98$ and $+2.38$ (exact one-sided $p=.0345$, $.417$, $.262$); no escalation rate was designated as primary in advance, and the 5\% result does not survive Holm correction over the three rates. A hindsight oracle with the same per-cell counts nets $+3.33$, $+3.73$ and $+3.73$\pp{} (Figure~\ref{fig:oracle}a). This headroom is positive by construction, because the oracle picks only rescues and never a damage; the trigger captures 4/34, 2/38 and 5/38 of it. A post-hoc KL$(\mathrm{B}\|\mathrm{Q})$ score, which needs BF16, also captures turnover above chance without a significant net gain (Appendix~\ref{app:oracle}). % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3 Validation, U1 S_mm PRIMARY); analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1a, §2)
Enriching outcome-changing decisions is therefore not enough for reliable net gain. The held-out blocks reuse the development tasks, and the test concerns this simple frozen rule: a learned router trained on task reward, such as DMR \citep{li2026dynamicmp}, is not tested here.
