\section{R3: Do Sampling and Environment Change the Answer?}
\label{sec:r3}

Bank P skips text-only differences. We ask whether the picture changes at ordinary states, under another user simulator, at unseen tasks, and without a user simulator.


\paragraph{Ordinary states turn over less, mostly through text under our simulator.} At ordinary states, replacing Q's action changes the outcome less often than at first divergences. Over blocks f01--f03 (task-equal; $t$ intervals over block means), $M$ per sub-bank is .078--.090 for the q4 W4 cells in U1 and U2B, .047--.090 for the q8 W4 cells and at most .047 for KV8. The net gain lies within $\pm$.028 (U1, U2B) and $\pm$.050 (U2Q), and every interval covers zero (Appendix~\ref{app:banku}). % src: analysis/fork/out/bank_u_f01_f02_f03_20261002T003037Z.md (§2)
Identical actions never change the outcome, as determinism implies. After deduplication, 150 of the 208 outcome-changing cell$\times$state rows ($\approx$72.1\%) are class (ii); the rows are not independent states, since the three blocks repeat the same tasks (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}): the same tool structure with different text, nearly always two natural-language replies. 26 involve a different tool name, call count or argument. % src: analysis/fork/out/bank_u_f01_f02_f03_20261002T003037Z.md (§3); derived: 150=45+28+44+25+10+6-5-3; 208=99+99+18-5-3; 72.1=100*(45+28+44+25+10+6-5-3)/(99+99+18-5-3); 26=1+11+4+9+0+1
This is a \emph{composition share}: per instance, text, tool/argument and act/talk differences flip the outcome in 14.4\%, 18.2\% and 18.9\% of rows. Text dominates because it is common (1045 of 1357 differing rows), not because each text difference matters more. % src: analysis/fork/out/bank_u_f01_f02_f03_20261002T003037Z.md (§3); derived: 14.4=100*(45+28+44+25+10+6-5-3)/(309+202+277+199+63+33-24-14); 1045=309+202+277+199+63+33-24-14; 18.2=100*(1+11+4+9+0+1)/(19+47+24+40+5+11-1-2); 18.9=100*(10+4+3+14+1+0)/(38+33+34+56+4+7-1-2); 1357=309+202+277+199+63+33-24-14+19+47+24+40+5+11-1-2+38+33+34+56+4+7-1-2
Simple content features do not separate flipping from non-flipping text pairs (Appendix~\ref{app:banku}). % src: analysis/fork/out/bank_u_f01_f02_f03_20261002T003037Z.md (§3 M rate column; §4)
Bank P cannot see these differences by design. These flip labels come from one simulator; the sensitivity check below, run on f01 flips, found that they do not reproduce reliably under another.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to the user simulator.} The user simulator is a plausible mediator of text consequences; changing it moves $\tau$-bench outcomes by 9--15 points \citep{seshadri2026lost,jain2026userproxybench}. % src: outputs/.drafts/storyline-v7-lit.md (rows 23-24)
We froze a control before running it: the 39 deduplicated class-(ii) flips of block f01 and 39 matched non-flipping states. Their BQ and QQ branches were each re-run three times with a different user simulator, Sonnet 5.5 answering through a file relay from Claude Code subagents. % src: docs/RESEARCH_LOG.md W90, W91 (manifests/fork/simctrl_v3.json)
Judged by the sign of the replicate mean, only 7 of the 39 flips keep their direction (95\% CI 0.075--0.335). Six reverse, and 26 vanish (15 both succeed, 2 both fail, 9 ties). Among the matched non-flip states, 11 of 39 become flips (0.15--0.45). Replicates of the same arm disagree in 41\% of flip arms and in 21--26\% of control arms. % src: analysis/fork/out/simctrl_20261001T212742Z.md
Dropping two mechanically flagged replies leaves this unchanged (6, 6 and 25 of 37; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:simctrl}). % src: analysis/fork/out/simctrl_relay_flagged_20261001T225847Z.md
In either direction, 13 of the 39 original flip states flip again, against 11 of 39 new flips at matched non-flip states; this does not show that the two groups are equivalent. % derived: 13=7+6
\textbf{The Bank U text-difference outcome labels did not reproduce reliably under this alternative relay setting.} This is not evidence that text consequences are simulator artefacts: the control changes the simulator model, adds sampling randomness and uses a relay interface whose responders may have seen other requests. Its scope is narrow: fixed histories from the original simulator, a stochastic relay rather than API Sonnet or real users, block f01 only and three replicates.

\paragraph{Sensitivity of the four-arm results.} Before running, we sampled 80 Bank P states without outcome information (20 per W4 cell, stratified by domain and block) and re-ran all four arms five times each with Sonnet 5.5 as the user, one isolated call per user turn (7896 calls; all integrity checks passed). % src: analysis/fork/out/simctrl_p_20261002T015200Z.md; manifests/fork/simctrl_p_v1.json
Per-state arm outcomes track the original single runs ($r=0.63$--$0.68$). Pooled over cells, $G=-3.0$ [$-10.2$, $+4.8$], $H=+5.5$ [$+0.0$, $+11.2$] and $G-H=-8.5$ [$-14.8$, $-2.8$]\pp{} under Sonnet, against $G-H=-2.5$ [$-11.2$, $+6.2$] on the same states under the original simulator (change $-6.0$ [$-16.2$, $+4.2$]). % src: analysis/fork/out/simctrl_p_20261002T015200Z.md (pool, all batches)
This sample is too small to test the few-pp $G-H$ contrasts of Table~\ref{tab:ghf}; in it, even the original simulator gives $G-H=0.0$ for both q8 W4 cells. Under Sonnet, replacing the continuation gains at least as much as replacing the action in the pool. We therefore treat the within-cell orderings of R2 as simulator-specific. % src: analysis/fork/out/simctrl_p_20261002T015200Z.md (q8 cells)

\paragraph{Unseen tasks: telecom holdout.} Before any holdout unit was run, we froze a confirmatory protocol (sha256 \texttt{902892dd\ldots}). It covers 34 unseen telecom tasks $\times$ 12 fresh nonce blocks $\times$ 3 cells (q4 GPTQ-W4, q8 GPTQ-W4, q8 AWQ-W4, weighted 1/2, 1/4, 1/4). The primary endpoint is $\Gall$ on a crossed 90\% interval against $\pm$3\pp{}; secondaries are two-sidedness and repeatability of task fragility (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}). % src: docs/insights/PREREG_FORK_CONFIRM.md §0; manifests/fork/splits_v1.json (holdout.prereg_sha256)
All 12 blocks were complete; two task-cell pairs were excluded as structural, and the complete-case analysis is identical. % src: analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md; analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_cc_20261002T012358Z.md
The primary endpoint is $\Gall=-4.57$\pp{}, crossed 90\% interval [$-7.17$, $-1.96$] (block-$t$ [$-6.08$, $-3.05$]). The pre-registered readings define only a positive gain, a gain above 3\pp{} and a small effect, so the decisional reading is \emph{inconclusive}. Descriptively, the interval lies entirely below zero: on these unseen tasks, replacing the first divergent action with BF16's lowered average success. Both secondaries pass. Rescues and damages are each above zero ($r=+5.21$ [$+3.30$, $+7.11$], $d=+9.77$ [$+6.45$, $+13.10$]\pp{}), and task fragility repeats across independent nonce halves ($\rho=0.59$, permutation $p=.0001$). % src: analysis/fork/out/holdout_confirm_20261002T012353Z.md
The holdout confirms what the protocol pre-specified, two-sided consequences and repeatable task fragility, and no positive average benefit of current-action replacement; it does not confirm the rest of the paper's development results.

\paragraph{No user simulator: BFCL and ALFWorld.} BFCL multi-turn and ALFWorld have no simulated user, and each item runs once. Replacing a W4 agent's first divergent action changes the outcome in 5.2--24.6\% of items, in both directions. The net gain covers zero except for BFCL q4 AWQ-W4, where it is $-3.8$ [$-6.5$, $-1.3$]\pp{}. % src: analysis/fork/out/ext_20261001T135106Z.md
Two-sided turnover is therefore not specific to a user simulator. Intervals here are item-level only.
