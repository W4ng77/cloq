\section{Result 3: Finding Changes Is Not Selecting Gains} % nocheck: section number
\label{sec:r4}
\label{sec:trigger}



\paragraph{Selecting outcome-changing decisions.} $G$, $H$ and $F$ require running B. We test selection under a Q-only constraint: the rule uses no BF16 computation before choosing an upgrade. On one development block, Q's token uncertainty separates states where B and Q act differently from states where they agree, but no Q-visible statistic reliably separates flips from non-flips among the former (Appendix~\ref{app:token}). % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261001T185031Z.md (§1a; §1b differing vs exact)
We then froze a Q-only trigger on that block, before any later state was scored: escalate one decision (B replaces only the current action and Q continues: the $G$ contrast) when the minimum Q margin over its action is low (margins predict cross-precision divergence; \citealp{du2026greedy}), with per-configuration thresholds targeting 5, 10 and 20\% on the development block (Figure~\ref{fig:trigger}). These frozen thresholds give selection rates rather than exact validation quotas; random and oracle baselines match the actual counts. The margin score was chosen among three Q-only candidates (entropy, margin, mean log-probability) by its net gain over random on that block. On two later blocks of the development tasks (not the telecom holdout), at ordinary states from Q's own trajectories (six configurations: four W4, two KV8; 1020 states, 69 outcome-changing) its capture of outcome-changing states exceeded matched-count random selection at 5 and 20\% (exact one-sided $p=.0182$ and $.0002$; $p=.0613$ at 10\%; Figure~\ref{fig:trigger}a). At ordinary states from BF16 trajectories (secondary), only the 20\% rate enriches (Appendix~\ref{app:token}). % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3 Validation, U1 S_mm PRIMARY; U2B S_mm secondary); manifests/fork/token_trigger_rule_v1.json (population; candidates; selection_criterion)

\paragraph{Testing benefit against random selection.} The captured states were rescues and damages alike (Figure~\ref{fig:trigger}a). Against matched-count random selection, its net gain has exact one-sided $p=.0345$, $.4169$ and $.2617$ (Figure~\ref{fig:trigger}b). No escalation rate was designated primary in advance, and the 5\% result does not survive Holm correction over the three rates. A hindsight oracle with the same per-configuration counts nets far more (Figure~\ref{fig:trigger}b), positive by construction because it picks only rescues. A post hoc KL$(\mathrm{B}\|\mathrm{Q})$ score, which needs BF16, also captures turnover above chance on these states without a significant net gain (exploratory, post hoc; Appendices~\ref{app:oracle} and~\ref{app:kl}). % src: analysis/fork/out/token_mech_20261002T003130Z.md (§3 Validation, U1 S_mm PRIMARY); analysis/fork/out/oracle_trigger_20261002T024423Z.md (§1a, §2)

\paragraph{What this test establishes.} Enriching outcome-changing decisions is not enough for reliable net gain \citep[cf.][]{zhang2026credit}. The later blocks reuse the development tasks, and only this simple frozen rule is tested. Learned and other uncertainty-based escalation rules require their own net-benefit tests \citep{li2026dynamicmp,piatrashyn2026redact,lei2026tacit}.
