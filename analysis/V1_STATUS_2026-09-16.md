# CLOQ v1 — interim status, 2026-09-16 (updated 16:05 EDT)

Calibration construction is finished and all twelve W4 checkpoints exist. No quantized
checkpoint has been evaluated yet, so **v1 currently says nothing about whether the method
works**. The closed-loop development evaluation (stage 5) started at 15:36 and is the first
step that produces a method number.

Model `Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507` @ `cdbee75f17c0`, tau2-bench text mode, GPTQ W4 group 128.
Frozen constants in `configs/frozen_v0.json`. Plan: `analysis/CLOQ_v1_2x4080_full_experiment_plan.md`.

---

## 1. What v1 is testing

v0 established three phenomena and no method win:

1. **Horizon amplification** — the quantization gap is concentrated in long tasks
   (Static: 0.000 short / 0.189 long, one-sided p = 0.039).
2. **Open-loop fidelity overstates closed-loop reliability** — tool-call agreement is
   0.81–0.85 across arms while closed-loop success spreads 0.24–0.36.
3. **Support shift after first divergence** — distance to the FP-Traj buffer jumps from
   ~0.06 pre-divergence to 0.12–0.30 post-divergence.

The method (CLOQ) did not beat trajectory calibration: pooled `cloq − fp_traj` was
−0.012, 95% CI [−0.106, 0.082]. On the long-task subset CLOQ led every quantized arm
(0.189 vs 0.135 FP-Traj, 0.108 Static) but every interval crossed zero.

The diagnosed cause was calibration support, not the idea. Under a 64k **token** budget one
agent context costs 5–8k tokens, so each v0 arm rested on 10 contexts drawn once, and the
CLOQ on-policy half contained only 5 Q0 contexts. Draw variance was not separated from
method effect.

v1 fixes exactly that and asks one question:

> Does refreshing calibration with **quantization-induced novel / post-divergence states**
> (CLOQ-Novel) beat nominal trajectory calibration (FP-Traj) and undirected on-policy
> refresh (CLOQ-All)?

## 2. Calibration support is now fixed — the v1 premise holds

The budget was redefined from tokens to **GPTQ activation rows**, which is what actually
determines quantization quality. Every arm and draw hits 64000 rows exactly.

| quantity | v0 | v1 (per draw) |
|---|---:|---:|
| independent calibration states | 10 | 57 |
| on-policy (Q0) states in the CLOQ arm | 5 | 29 |
| calibration draws per arm | 1 | 3 |
| activation rows | not controlled | 64000, exact |

All four arms use the same 57 states per draw, the same 39 retail / 18 airline split, and
the same task split hash `7d167115…`. The arms differ only in *which* states:

| arm | source | depth quartile Q0 / Q1 / Q2 / Q3 |
|---|---|---|
| Static | 57 FP, turn 0 only | 57 / 0 / 0 / 0 |
| FP-Traj | 57 FP, depth-stratified | 15 / 14 / 14 / 14 |
| CLOQ-All | 28 FP + 29 Q0, undirected | 12–18 / 10–14 / 10–17 / 15 |
| CLOQ-Novel | 28 FP + 29 Q0, novelty-selected | 8–10 / 10–11 / 9–17 / 22–29 |

CLOQ-Novel is the only arm whose mass concentrates in the deepest quartile, which is the
intended behavior: it is buying the states the other buffers miss.

## 3. The novelty signal replicates across all three draws

Novelty = distance from a Q0-visited state to the FP-Traj calibration support, in the frozen
BF16 final-layer representation at the action boundary.

| draw | Q0 states | post-div. states | novelty pre-div. | novelty post-div. | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| c1 | 1321 | 1211 | 0.031 | 0.156 | 5.0× |
| c2 | 1277 | 1161 | 0.034 | 0.154 | 4.6× |
| c3 | 1666 | 1558 | 0.029 | 0.146 | 5.0× |

All 57 tasks produce post-divergence states in all three draws, and 91–94% of visited states
are post-divergence. This is the v0 occupancy finding reproduced on three independent draws
at 5× the sample size, and it is what makes "novel state" a well-defined selection target
rather than a relabeling of "on-policy state".

Caveat worth stating plainly: the novelty score is defined against the FP-Traj support, so a
large pre/post ratio is partly built into the construction. It establishes that the selector
fires on the intended states. It does **not** establish that those states are the ones that
matter for success. Only stage 5 can do that.

## 4. Q0 rollout behavior (stage 2)

The Q0 rollouts that supply the on-policy half also serve as a behavioral readout of the
FP-Traj W4 checkpoints on calibration tasks.

| draw | domain | mean decisions | premature | repeated tool calls | truncated context |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| c1 | retail | 21.8 | 0.103 | 0.338 | 0.541 |
| c1 | airline | 26.1 | 0.167 | 0.080 | 0.635 |
| c2 | retail | 25.3 | 0.154 | 0.107 | 0.608 |
| c2 | airline | 16.2 | 0.056 | 0.107 | 0.421 |
| c3 | retail | 35.6 | 0.282 | 0.144 | 0.718 |
| c3 | airline | 15.4 | 0.056 | 0.469 | 0.462 |

Draw-to-draw spread is large — retail mean decisions run 21.8 to 35.6, and airline repeated
tool calls run 0.080 to 0.469. This is the draw variance v0 could not see with a single draw,
and it is the reason the three-draw crossed bootstrap in the plan is necessary rather than
decorative. It also warns that a one-draw method difference in stage 5 will not be
interpretable on its own.

Context truncation runs 42–72%, consistent with v0. The 8192-token context with a ~4.8k-token
system prompt and tool schema remains the dominant failure channel and is a property of the
setup, not of any arm.

## 5. Integrity

- Every calibration manifest carries `split_file_sha256 = 7d167115…`; held-out evaluation
  tasks were never touched during calibration construction.
- Row budget satisfied exactly (`rows_within_budget: true`, 64000 rows, 1122–1123 rows per
  state) for all twelve manifests, so the arms are compute-matched at the quantizer.
- `prompt_ids_from_server_frac` and `prompt_ids_match_local_frac` are 1.000 in every stage-2
  run, so local token accounting matches what the server actually saw.
- `cloq.report_v1` (stage 7, never run before) was dry-run against the current tree: it parses,
  imports, exits cleanly on missing runs, and its inputs check out — `configs/frozen_v1.json`
  carries the three draws and the long-horizon threshold of 11, the task split has horizon data
  for all 85 evaluation tasks, and `metrics.json` supplies the `task_id` / `db_match` fields it
  reads. The final step will not fail on a defect that was already visible.
- Determinism depends on `VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT=1` and on not restarting the user-simulator
  server mid-experiment. v0 verified a zero noise floor under these conditions; stage 5
  re-serves the agent per checkpoint but leaves the user server up, which preserves it.

## 5a. Amendment 1 — the depth confound, closed before results

CLOQ-Novel varies **novelty and trajectory depth together**: its states concentrate in the
deepest quartile (section 2), and deep contexts are also the truncated ones. A CLOQ-Novel win
would therefore admit a competing explanation that the current four arms cannot exclude —
that the arm simply calibrated on more late, deep, truncated contexts.

Two amendments were frozen in git before any arm had been evaluated, and both are reproduced
in full under `analysis/amendments/`:

- **Amendment 1 — CLOQ-MatchedRandom** (`51c8dd8`, frozen while only `runs/bf16_v1/retail` was
  running and no arm existed). Per Q-half task it holds CLOQ-Novel's depth quartile, truncation
  status and post-divergence status fixed and randomises only which state inside that stratum
  is taken. The FP half is asserted bit-identical to CLOQ-Novel's.
- **Amendment 1a — CLOQ-MatchedAlt** (`b4a6e37`, frozen while only the BF16 baseline existed).
  Same matching, with CLOQ-Novel's own state excluded from the draw.

Amendment 1a exists because building Amendment 1 triggered its own pre-committed power clause.
The matched stratum is small — a task has few decisions inside one depth quartile, median pool
2–3 states — so the unbiased random draw returns CLOQ-Novel's own pick surprisingly often:

| draw | collisions with Novel | novelty separation | buffer shared with Novel |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| c1 | 10 / 29 | 0.145 | 67% |
| c2 | 16 / 29 | 0.052 | 77% |
| c3 | 8 / 29 | 0.070 | 63% |

Excluding Novel's own state cuts collisions to 0 / 2 / 2 and roughly doubles the novelty
separation (c1 0.205, c2 0.105, c3 0.087), while the depth-quartile histogram still matches
CLOQ-Novel exactly in all three draws.

The five arms then decompose cleanly:

| arm | Q states | isolates |
|---|---|---|
| FP-Traj | none | nominal trajectory support |
| CLOQ-All | random on-policy | whether on-policy states help at all |
| CLOQ-MatchedRandom / MatchedAlt | depth- and truncation-matched | whether late / off-nominal states help |
| CLOQ-Novel | same stratum, highest novelty | **whether the novelty score adds anything** |

`CLOQ-Novel − CLOQ-MatchedRandom` stays the pre-registered headline; `− CLOQ-MatchedAlt` is the
power-recovering companion. Because 63–77% of the MatchedRandom buffer coincides with
CLOQ-Novel's, **a null on the pre-registered contrast must be read as inconclusive, not as
evidence that novelty is useless.** That reading was committed before the numbers existed.

Six additional checkpoints are queued behind the running job (`scripts/pipeline_amend1.sh`),
which waits for the GPUs rather than competing for them. All twelve pre-existing manifests were
verified to rebuild bit-identically, activation-row indices included, so no pre-registered arm
was disturbed.

## 5b. Known control still outstanding

**Context length.** The system prompt and tool schema alone are ~4.8k tokens against an
8192-token policy, and 42–72% of contexts truncate. A reader can reasonably ask whether
long-horizon failure is quantization-induced occupancy shift or an artifact of history being
dropped. Every arm uses the identical context policy, so **the method comparison is unaffected**;
what is unproven is that horizon amplification survives a longer context. A 16k/32k replication
on a task subset belongs in the full paper. It is deliberately not queued now, because it would
compete with the method experiment for the same two GPUs.

## 6. What is still unknown, and what answers it

| stage | produces | est. wall clock |
|---|---|---|
| 5 (running, started 15:36) | closed-loop dev success for 13 checkpoints, divergence, judge | ~10 h |
| 6 | open-loop replay fidelity for 12 checkpoints | ~4 h |
| 7 | occupancy extraction + `analysis/REPORT_V1.md` | ~3 h |

Expected completion around midday 2026-09-17. The fresh-test protocol (plan section 17) is a
separate run after a variant is selected, and is not in this queue.

**Decision rules, frozen before any v1 result was seen** (plan sections 16 and 19):

- *Selection:* take CLOQ-Novel to the fresh test only if its mean long-task success across the
  three draws exceeds CLOQ-All, and pooled mean is not worse than CLOQ-All by more than 2 pp.
  If long-task means differ by under 1 pp, take the simpler CLOQ-All.
- *Strong GO:* CLOQ-Novel beats Static on long tasks with a crossed-draw 95% CI excluding 0,
  or beats FP-Traj on long tasks with a positive sign in all three draws plus a coverage or
  behavioral improvement.
- *Rethink:* three draws show no reproducible Static-versus-trajectory difference, or
  CLOQ-Novel fails to improve coverage over CLOQ-All, or the novelty-selected states turn out
  to be unrelated to post-divergence behavior.

No selector outside this list may be introduced after the numbers land.

## 7. Honest summary

The premise of v1 is confirmed and the mechanism replicates on three draws: quantized agents
leave the nominal calibration support after their first divergence, and a novelty selector
reliably targets that region. Whether repairing that support improves closed-loop success is
still the open question, unchanged from v0. The stage-2 draw variance reported above is large
enough that a single favorable draw in stage 5 should not be read as a method win.

As of this update the design can also distinguish *why* a win happens, which it could not this
morning: with the matched controls in place, "novelty helps" and "deep contexts help" are
separately measurable rather than confounded.
