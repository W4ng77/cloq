**Why a near-zero mean I coexists with a large off-diagonal share.** I = Δ_Q − Δ_B ∈ {−2, …, +2}, and its mean is E[I] = P(I = +1) + 2P(I = +2) − P(I = −1) − 2P(I = −2). The off-diagonal share is P(I ≠ 0) = P(I > 0) + P(I < 0). A mean near zero therefore says only that the controller-dependent states with I > 0 (the action swap helps more, or hurts less, when Q continues) and with I < 0 are about equally frequent; it does not say the action effect is the same under both controllers. Example (q8:gptq_w4_d1): P(I > 0) = 10.9%, P(I < 0) = 12.0%, so off-diagonal = 22.9% while E[I] = -1.7 pp. Most off-diagonal mass is in cells where one controller shows an effect and the other shows none (one coordinate 0); strict sign reversals are the small corners (40 of 337 off-diagonal states in this cell). The mean interaction is the right estimand for average policy value; the off-diagonal share is the right one for "is the consequence of an action a property of the action alone?" — and it is not, for roughly 1 in 4 (W4) to 1 in 7 (KV8) divergent states. Cross-cell differences in these shares (W4 vs KV8) are descriptive: the cells select different states.
