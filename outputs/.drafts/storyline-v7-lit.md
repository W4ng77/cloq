# Storyline v7: related work on same-state counterfactual forks for quantized closed-loop agents

TL;DR: No work found (as of 2026-10-01) that does the exact design: fork at the *first BF16-vs-quantized divergence*, replay the identical state, and cross {current action: BF16 vs Q} x {continuation controller: BF16 vs Q} to get 4 branches and signed rescue/damage (r, d, M=r+d, G=r-d). The closest prior work covers parts of it. **Replay Gap (2608.08239)** forks live agent trajectories and swaps the *continuation model*, with same-model control forks, and reports rescue and loss flips. **CSO / CRR / CAR / AgenTracer** swap the *action at a step* and let the policy continue. **Flat Score (2607.27275)** and **Accuracy is Not All You Need (2407.09141)** cover "flat score hides damage / flips" under quantization. **DMR (2602.02711)** routes precision per step using BF16-vs-Q KL on ALFWorld. Novelty risk: **medium**.

All URLs below resolved (HTTP 200 on arxiv.org/abs, with the title checked) on 2026-10-01. Abstracts were read for every entry. Full text was grepped only for #1, #2 and #3. Evidence: fetched HTML in the session scratchpad. Not kept.

## Findings by question

**(a) Quantization and agents.** Status: done. Aggregate benchmarks find 4-bit nearly lossless on scores [13][31] but with hidden damage: process errors on tau2-bench [2], flips on static tasks [4], and decision-margin shrinkage that flips tool-call decisions on BFCL [14]. Precision-sensitivity is localized to a few steps [3], phases [28] or task types [29]. The collapse threshold replicates across models but failure modes do not [15]. [2] is the most direct prior on our exact setting: tau2-bench, BF16 vs INT4, "flat score", and a shared failure set across precisions. Our "flips concentrate in fragile tasks shared across quantizers" must be positioned against its "failure set is the same at every precision". Inference: our contribution there is *signed outcome* turnover at the task level from forks, versus their *error-channel volume*.

**(b) Counterfactual and branch replay.** Status: done. Action-axis interventions with same-policy continuation are now common: CSO [5], CRR [6], CAR [7], AgenTracer [8], CausalFlow [9], and turn-level history edits [10]. Controller-axis swaps (fork, then continue with a different model, with same-model controls) appear in Replay Gap [1]. **None of the works found crosses both axes in a 2x2 design. None anchors forks at the first divergence between two precisions of the same model. None defines rescue/damage/turnover/net as estimands.** [1] is the main novelty threat. Its counts of "upgrades rescuing" and "downgrade losing" are informal r/d, and it notes FP8 vs AWQ serving determinism.

**(c) Evaluation reliability and flips.** Status: done. Two-sided flips under compression [4], negative flips under updates [20][21], rerun flip floors on BFCL [19], early trajectory divergence [17], numerical nondeterminism [18], and quantization-amplified prefix-cache divergence in multi-turn tool use [16]. [16] matters operationally for our replay: same-state forks need prefix caching disabled, or else need a measured control floor as in [1].

**(d) User-simulator sensitivity.** Status: done. Changing the user LLM moves tau-bench retail success by up to 9pp [23] and reward by 15.2 points [24]. [24] also finds user-spec violations in 24.4% of successes. Inference: because our consequential differences are "full-message language differences", the user simulator is a plausible mediator. A reviewer will likely ask for a user-simulator swap or control on the forks.
